# Shared terms

Each term has one meaning in every file. This file holds only terms used in more than one file; a term used in one file is defined there. Where two terms are close, the distinction is stated.

## Inputs and records

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Brief Ledger | The Phase 1 record of everything known about the piece, each item classified by necessity and provenance |
| Necessity | **mandatory** (user said it must appear, or the piece cannot work without it), **optional** (offered or helpful; the piece works without it), **excluded** (user said not to include it) |
| Provenance tags | **given** (stated by the user), **fixed asset**, **inferred** (reasonable deduction), **researched** (found externally), **creative choice** (the skill's own decision) |
| Fixed asset | Any user-supplied element used exactly as provided: logo, brand colors, typefaces, photographs, QR, QRIS, or other codes, established identity |
| Gap classes | **explicit** (stated), **inferable** (reliably deducible), **researchable** (discoverable externally), **materially uncertain** (missing, not discoverable, and would change the design). A **blocking gap** is a materially uncertain gap where a wrong guess would waste the generation or mislead the audience |
| Face | One printed side or panel of a piece |

## Qualities, copy, and supporters

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality requirement | Any experiential, tonal, affective, or priority statement in the brief, and any style, color, or technique the user names, however brief or vague ("calm", "premium", "brand first", "easy to scan"). Binding, not a preference; a minimal brief makes these the primary requirements |
| Quality translation | For one quality requirement: the visual targets it implies (value key, chroma, temperature, density, formality, warmth, hierarchy priority, sensory cues), its **quality carriers** (the visible features that deliver it), and the opposite signals that would contradict it |
| Value key | The overall lightness structure of the piece: the area-weighted balance of light, mid, and dark masses, with its chroma and temperature (high-key, mid-key, low-key) |
| Content role | The job a text item does: identify, hook (voice, persuasion, memory), transact (price, size, offer), inform, reassure (trust claim), act (contact, location) |
| Treatment grade | **Designed** (built as a graphic: lettering or layout with its own scale play, emphasis, rhythm, container, or motif) or **set** (plain typesetting in the informational face), by content role |
| Text-only baseline | The piece imagined with only its required text, identity unit, and background; the reference for finding visual deficits |
| Supporter family | A class of non-text visual resource: icons and symbols; subject, in-use, or place imagery; figures; spot illustration; image treatment or set; pattern or texture; border or frame; divider; pointer, connector, or direction cue; shape block, band, tag, or edge shape; lettering-as-graphic. A tag is never a regulated or trust mark |
| Referent | What a content item is about: the business, the offer, the hero as a whole, a specific depicted part, or an action the viewer takes |
| Coupling mode | How an item relates to its referent: **integrated** (on, around, or pointing into the thing it describes), **attached** (in the same group, sharing its field, axis, or rail), or **separate** (its own zone) |
| Hero group | The hero plus the content coupled to it, read as one object at the glance stage; supporting text inside it is always subordinate to the hero |
| Distinct job | The one purpose an element performs that no other element in the piece performs; two elements with the same job are merged toward the one that serves the hierarchy better |
| Weight ceiling | The most weight (scale, contrast, color, isolation) an element may take: below the required information it serves. Only the hero, the identity unit (per mark role), and a designed hook or transaction lead; decoration never outranks required information |

## Context and analysis

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Communication Problem | The piece's job: primary and secondary objectives, desired audience response, necessary information, desired brand perception, the weakest link in the chain attention → comprehension → credibility → action, and the tensions between competing needs |
| Environment Profile | The setting analysed as a design input: physical setting, surrounding visual landscape (dominant colors, density, scale, order or clutter, finish level of neighbors), competing stimuli, light, audience behavior there, cultural context, viewing stages, departure and fit dimensions |
| Viewing stage | A moment in meeting the piece: **glance** (shortest exposure, farthest distance), **approach** (closer, interest sustained), **close inspection** (deliberate reading). A piece may have only one. **Stage assignment** is the Phase 5 decision giving every content item the one stage at which it is needed; the items sharing a stage form a **tier** |
| Impression and expression | **Impression** is the quality the brand wants to be read as; **expression** is the visual means that deliver it in a given setting |
| Departure and fit dimensions | **Departure dimensions** are those where the neighbors are uniform and the piece can differ to be seen (color field, density, scale, form language, finish, tone). **Fit dimensions** are those where the piece must conform for credibility or appropriateness |
| Visual source inventory | The Phase 2 list of (1) the apparent associations, named as the baseline, (2) deeper true specifics of the business, product, place, or community that could become visual ideas, (3) which of those are visually workable |
| Design implication | A finding converted to a consequence: "because [finding], the design must / should / must avoid [consequence]", tagged by provenance |

## Decisions and exploration

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consequential decision | A decision that defines identity, effectiveness, or coherence: core idea, style synthesis, hero, logo mark, color strategy, typographic voice, composition (spatial) strategy. All others are **trivial** and decided directly |
| Apparent option | What most people produce on first association from the category, culture, place, or product. A benchmark to beat, never a default. The image model has its own: the value it supplies for any attribute left undecided. **Beyond the apparent** means drawn from true, lesser-seen specifics (typically the Visual source inventory) |
| Candidate | One complete, distinct alternative for one consequential decision; in Phases 3 to 5 a **candidate card** whose fields the phase reference sets, each filled in specialist terms (a label alone is not a candidate) |
| Structural axis | A dimension on which candidates must differ to count as distinct: what carries the identity, what the viewer is asked to do first, how information is structured, register and commercial posture, image-making method. Color or ornament alone do not count |
| Hybrid / compromise | A **hybrid** combines candidates and can be stated as one idea. A **compromise** needs "and also" to describe and is rejected |
| Brand design / theme | **Brand design** is the enduring visual expression of the brand, stable across pieces. **Theme** is the creative idea for this piece, built on the brand design |
| Core idea | The single unifying concept from which theme, hero, style, and layout follow: specific, visual, generative, truthful, functional |
| Hero | The dominant visual idea of the piece, or the deliberate absence of one |

## Identity

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wordmark / logo mark / lockup / identity unit | The **wordmark** is the business name set in lettering. The **logo mark** is a distinctive visual form that can stand for the brand independently of the name. The **lockup** is how they combine. The **identity unit** is the lockup designed as one cohesive object, with a shared construction logic and a defined spatial relationship, distinct from everything else on the piece |
| Styled name | The business name set in an attractive typeface with no further identity device. Not an identity |
| Personality device | An ownable, conceptually grounded feature that gives an identifier character, is specific to the business, is readable at small size, and could not be removed without the identifier collapsing back into plain lettering |
| Form class | The kind of visual construction a shape belongs to. A mark reads as a mark when its form class differs from the text around it |
| Mark role | Whether the identity unit **leads** the hierarchy, **anchors** it, or **signs off** |
| Figure | A mascot, brand character, or depicted person: supplied, or created only when the user wants one. Each appearance holds one role (identify, lead, demonstrate, guide, or carry mood), with one design shared across roles and faces |
| Identity forms | The ways the identity appears: the full unit (the one identifier in a view), a symbol-only form (only where the viewer can link it to the name), and a supplied name-only wordmark |

## Space

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Space intent | Decided in Phase 3: the share of the format that must stay quiet and what that space does. Its Phase 4 application is the **element allowance**: every element has a space cost and the total must fit the space intent; elements are removed or merged before any is shrunk |
| Space plan | Decided in Phase 5 before placement, as proportions of the format: the **outer margin** (quiet border), **isolation zones** (protected space around glance content, hero, or mark), **separations** (space between content groups), and **resting fields** (quiet areas placed to support the eye's path). Space intent decides how much stays quiet; the space plan decides where |

## Control

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Design Log | The persisted state record (`SKILL.md` section 5): the design's complete state, updated at every gate |
| Phase entry | The compact record of a phase's decisions, options (in a table), and choices, in plain Indonesian, printed to the user at the gate of each of Phases 2 to 5 in guided mode; Phases 2 to 4 add a confirmation question |
