# Shared terms

One meaning per term in every file. Only terms used in more than one file are here; single-file terms are defined where used.

## Inputs and records

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Brief Ledger | Phase 1 record of everything known about the piece; each item carries necessity, provenance, referent, and (for text) its verbatim form |
| Necessity | **mandatory** (user said it must appear, or the piece cannot work without it), **optional** (offered or helpful), **excluded** (user said not to include it) |
| Provenance | **given**, **fixed asset**, **inferred**, **researched**, **creative choice** (the skill's own decision) |
| Fixed asset | A user-supplied element used exactly as provided: logo, brand colors, typefaces, photographs, QR, QRIS or other codes, mascots, established identity |
| Gap classes | **explicit**, **inferable**, **researchable**, **materially uncertain**. A **blocking gap** is materially uncertain and a wrong guess would waste the generation or mislead the audience |
| Face | One printed side or panel of a piece |
| Design Log | The persisted state record (`SKILL.md` section 4): the standing block **S**, one **Forward block** per phase (decisions as specifications, carry-forward obligations, open flags), and the **Record** (candidates, reasons, objections, rejected directions, research), which never travels forward |
| Carry-forward obligation | A mitigation, accepted cost, or "amplify here" move recorded by the protocol that a later phase must realize or record as accepted |

## Qualities, copy, supporters

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality requirement | Any experiential, tonal, affective, or priority statement in the brief, and any style, color, or technique the user names, however vague ("calm", "premium", "brand first", "easy to scan"). Binding, not a preference; a minimal brief makes these the primary requirements |
| Quality translation | For one quality requirement: the visual targets it implies, 2 to 4 **quality carriers** (visible features that deliver it), and the contradicting signals |
| Value key | The area-weighted balance of light, mid, and dark masses (ground and large bands included), with chroma and temperature: high-key, mid-key, low-key |
| Content role | The job a text item does: identify, hook (voice, persuasion, memory), transact (price, size, offer), inform, reassure (trust claim), act (contact, location) |
| Treatment grade | **Designed** (built as a graphic with its own scale play, emphasis, rhythm, container, or motif) or **set** (plain typesetting in the informational face), by content role |
| Text-only baseline | The piece imagined with only its required text, identity unit, and background; the reference for finding visual deficits |
| Supporter family | A class of non-text visual resource: icons and symbols; subject, in-use, or place imagery; figures; spot illustration; image treatment or set; pattern or texture; border or frame; divider; pointer, connector, or direction cue; shape block, band, tag, or edge shape; lettering-as-graphic. A tag is never a regulated or trust mark |
| Referent | What a content item is about: the business, the offer, the hero, a specific depicted part, or an action |
| Coupling mode | **integrated** (on, around, or pointing into the thing described), **attached** (same group, sharing its field, axis, or rail), **separate** (its own zone) |
| Hero group | The hero plus the content coupled to it, read as one object at the glance stage; its text is always subordinate to the hero |
| Rank / lever | **Rank**: an element's place in the stated order of attention at a viewing stage (first, second, third …). **Levers** are the means that create it: size, position, color, value contrast, weight, shape or container, spacing and isolation, plane and overlap, detail density, alignment, typographic style, edge contact |
| Character carrier / link | **Character carrier**: a quiet, ownable feature that keeps a piece interesting and specific to its business at its density (depth within a main element, a recurring signature motif, subtle surface, small iconography, typographic detail, edge or container character). **Link**: a stated tie between two major elements (shared axis, overlap, shared color or shape, echoed motif, connector, proximity group) |
| Shape language / container | **Container**: any form that holds, separates, flags, or frames content (panel, band, tag, badge, border, cut shape). **Shape language**: the one corner, edge, and geometry logic shared by all containers, rules, and supporters, derived from the style and the business, never from the model's default |
| Visual weight / balance | **Visual weight**: an element's pull on the eye, roughly area share × intensity, raised by leverage (distance from center). **Balance**: the weight centroid of the composed piece sits where the chosen balance type puts it (`05_design_execution.md` A3). A **counterweight** is a quiet carrier that gains weight from area and distance, not intensity |
| Distinct job | The one purpose an element performs that no other element performs; two elements with the same job merge toward the one that serves the hierarchy better |
| Weight ceiling | The most weight (scale, contrast, color, isolation) an element may take: below the required information it serves. Only the hero, the identity unit (per mark role), and a designed hook or transaction lead |

## Context and analysis

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Communication Problem | The piece's job: objectives, desired audience response, necessary information, desired brand perception, the **message** (the one sentence the glance viewer takes away: written here, carried by the core idea, confirmed in Phase 5), the weakest link in attention → comprehension → credibility → action, and the tensions between needs |
| Environment Profile | The setting as a design input: physical setting, neighbors (colors, density, scale, finish), competing stimuli, light, audience behavior there, cultural context, viewing stages, departure and fit dimensions |
| Viewing stage | **glance** (shortest exposure, farthest distance), **approach** (closer, interest sustained), **close inspection** (deliberate reading); a piece may have only one. **Stage assignment** gives every content item the one stage at which it is needed; items sharing a stage form a **tier** |
| Departure / fit dimensions | **Departure**: dimensions where neighbors are uniform and the piece can differ to be seen (color field, density, scale, form language, finish, tone). **Fit**: dimensions where the piece must conform for credibility |
| Visual source inventory | Phase 2 list of (1) the apparent associations as baseline, (2) deeper true specifics of the business, product, place, or community, (3) which of those are visually workable |
| Design implication | A finding turned into a consequence: "because [finding], the design must / should / must avoid [consequence]", provenance-tagged |

## Decisions and exploration

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consequential decision | Core idea, style synthesis, hero, logo mark and lockup, spatial strategy: Full protocol. Other exploratory decisions use the compact comparison or are direct (`protocol.md` section 1) |
| Apparent option | What most people produce on first association from category, culture, place, or product: a benchmark to beat, never a default. The image model has its own: the value it supplies for any undecided attribute. **Beyond the apparent** means drawn from true, lesser-seen specifics |
| Candidate / card | One complete, distinct alternative for one decision; a card has the fields the phase sets, filled in specialist terms (a label alone is not a candidate) |
| Structural axis | A dimension on which candidates must differ to count as distinct: what carries the identity, what the viewer does first, how information is structured, register, image-making method. Color or ornament alone do not count |
| Hybrid / compromise | A **hybrid** combines candidates under one logic, statable as one idea. A **compromise** needs "and also" and is rejected |
| Brand design / theme | **Brand design**: the enduring visual expression of the brand. **Theme**: the creative idea for this piece, built on it |
| Core idea | The single unifying concept from which theme, hero, style, and layout follow: specific, visual, generative, truthful, functional |
| Hero | The dominant visual idea of the piece, or its deliberate absence |

## Identity

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wordmark / logo mark / lockup / identity unit | **Wordmark**: the name in lettering. **Logo mark**: a distinctive form that can stand for the brand without the name. **Lockup**: how they combine. **Identity unit**: the lockup designed as one object with shared construction logic and a defined spatial relationship, distinct from everything else on the piece |
| Styled name | The name in an attractive typeface with no further identity device. Not an identity |
| Personality device | An ownable, conceptually grounded feature specific to the business, readable at small size, without which the identifier collapses to plain lettering |
| Form class | The kind of visual construction a shape belongs to; a mark reads as a mark when its form class differs from the text around it |
| Mark role | Whether the identity unit **leads** the hierarchy, **anchors** it, or **signs off** |
| Figure | A mascot, brand character, or depicted person: supplied, or created only when the user wants one. Each appearance holds one role (identify, lead, demonstrate, guide, carry mood) and one shared design |
| Identity forms | The full unit (the one identifier in a view), a symbol-only form (only where the viewer can link it to the name), a supplied name-only wordmark |

## Space and layering

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Space intent | Phase 3: the share of the format that must stay quiet and what that space does. Phase 4 applies it as the **element allowance**: every element has a space cost, the total fits the space intent, and elements are removed or merged before any is shrunk |
| Space plan | Phase 5, before placement, as proportions of the format: **outer margin**, **isolation zones** (around glance content, hero, mark), **separations** between groups, **resting fields** supporting the eye's path. Space intent decides how much stays quiet; the space plan decides where |
| Plane / layering | **Planes**: front, middle, ground (a piece may use one). **Layering** assigns elements to planes with **layer cues** (overlap, relative scale, contrast and detail, tonal and temperature step, focus, light and shadow, surface texture). Plane order is a hierarchy means and a way to integrate content with the hero. A flat style keeps cues in the picture plane: tonal steps, surface detail, overlap of flat shapes |
| Canvas | The ratio the image is generated at, decided in Phase 5: the print ratio when the generator renders it, otherwise the nearest ratio it accepts plus the crop; content sits inside the crop-safe region |
