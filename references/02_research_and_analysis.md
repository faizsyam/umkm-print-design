## 1. Process

1. Triage the research questions: name the decision each informs; drop those that inform none.
2. Understand the brand beyond its description (A).
3. Find differentiation: list candidate differentiators, evaluate each for truth, relevance, distinctiveness, and visualizability, choose a strategy (or execution-led differentiation if none is strong), and record the options weighed (B).
4. Build the Environment Profile (C).
5. Build the audience encounter (D).
6. Define the Communication Problem, including its weakest link and tensions (E).
7. Research per F, evaluating source reliability and confirming that a found business is the right one.
8. Ground culturally and build the Visual source inventory (G).
9. Convert findings into design implications. Discard any finding that yields none.
10. Pass the gate; record the decision-summary entry.

Steps 3 to 8 interleave in practice; the order above is the order of conclusions, not of fact-finding. Research early enough that the Environment Profile and inventory rest on it.

## 2. Detailed guidance

### A. The brand beyond its description
Values, personality, character, core message, positioning, credibility, aspirations, relationship with customers. Distinguish established facts from plausible interpretations. Do not manufacture a narrative to make the design more elaborate: a small business with little evidence gets a modest, truthful brand reading, not a richer story.

### B. Differentiation
Candidate differentiators come from product specifics, process, materials, origin, scale and intimacy, service behavior, price logic, location, people, name, signature item, consumption ritual. Evaluate each on four tests: **true** (the business can honestly claim it), **relevant** (the audience cares at the moment of encounter), **distinctive** (not shared by dozens of competitors), **visualizable** (it can become something seen). A differentiator failing truth is discarded; failing visualizability may still shape tone or content hierarchy. If none is strong, choose execution-led differentiation: the piece differs through how it is composed and made, and the record says so. Record the options weighed.

### C. The Environment Profile (a governing design parameter)
Reconstruct the real encounter:
- **Physical setting and surrounding visual landscape;** competing stimuli and what they promise; ambient light and conditions; audience behavior there (moving or still, attention, dwell time); cultural context of the place.
- **Viewing stages,** with distance and duration for each. A piece may have only one.
- **Impression versus expression.** Determine what the landscape already says, and what expression would still carry the intended impression there. Hold impression constant; let the setting decide expression.
- **Departure and fit dimensions.** List where neighbors are uniform (color field, density, scale, form language, finish, tone) and where fitting is required for credibility or appropriateness. Louder is not the same as distinct; quieter can be the departure in a loud landscape. Choose the departure dimension(s) by weighing real options and record why.
- **Provisional status.** If the setting cannot be established, state the most probable one and mark the profile provisional.
- **Downstream use.** State how the profile is used: a candidate source and scoring criterion in Phase 3; the basis for color, typographic, mark, and background judgments in Phase 4; the basis for viewing stages, density, and the final context review in Phase 5.

### D. The audience
Cultural references, expectations, behavior, motivations, needs, purchasing considerations. Examine how they meet the piece: what earns attention, makes the offer relevant, builds confidence, and prompts action. No demographic stereotypes or unsupported psychology. State the audience encounter as a short sequence of what they do and decide at each viewing stage.

### E. The Communication Problem
Record: primary and secondary objectives; desired response; necessary information; desired brand perception; the **weakest link** in attention → comprehension → credibility → action (the link most likely to fail in this setting, chosen by weighing which failure costs most). List the tensions: visibility versus sophistication, density versus readability, memorability versus commercial clarity, legibility distance versus content volume versus area, ambition versus production budget. Leave their resolution to Phase 3.

### F. Research
Priorities: the actual business and its public materials; category visual conventions to differentiate from; local competitive context; the environment; cultural references; product reality. Prefer authoritative and first-hand sources. Never copy competitors. Treat reviews as signals of perception, not verified fact. Confirm a found business is the right one (same name does not mean same business). Stop when further research would not change a decision. If research tools are unavailable, reason from the brief, mark conclusions provisional, and avoid choices that depend on unverified specifics.

### G. Cultural grounding and the Visual source inventory
Indonesia's cultures, languages, religions, and commercial conventions vary widely; do not treat "Indonesian" as one aesthetic or audience. Preserve the user's language register. Verify the meaning of any culturally loaded symbol and omit it if uncertain. Use regional or traditional references only when the business genuinely connects to them.

Look past the first association. The emblem most widely linked to a culture, place, category, or product is what everyone reaches for first; cultures and businesses hold far more that is true and distinctive. Dimensions to mine: practices and rituals; materials and tools; how things are made, handled, and shared; spatial and sensory details of daily life; language and its play; geography and seasons; the history of this business. Representing a business through a lesser-seen but true element often signals belonging more convincingly than its emblem.

Build the inventory in three parts, each entry grounded in the brief or research:
1. **The apparent associations,** named as the baseline.
2. **Deeper true specifics** that could become visual ideas.
3. **Which are visually workable** (can be seen, drawn, and rendered at the viewing distance) and which are not.

## 3. Failure modes

| Failure | Detection | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Research for its own sake | Findings that change no decision | Drop them; keep only those with an implication |
| Narrative richer than evidence | Brand claims with no source | Downgrade to modest reading; tag as inference |
| Category-level differentiation | The strategy fits dozens of competitors | Re-run B with this business's specifics or choose execution-led |
| Abstract environment | The profile could describe any location | Add concrete neighbors, light, distance, behavior; mark provisional if unknown |
| Loud equals distinct | Departure chosen as simply "bigger or brighter" | Re-weigh departure dimensions against the actual landscape |
| Inventory of emblems | Part 2 contains only the apparent associations or their variants | Mine the seven dimensions again; require at least one true specific beyond the baseline |
| Unverified symbol | A culturally loaded element with no checked meaning | Verify or omit |
| Competitor copying | A finding about a competitor's visuals carried forward as a model rather than a baseline to differ from | Re-state it as a departure dimension; never reproduce it |
| Manufactured alternatives | Differentiation options that are one idea reworded | State that evidence leaves one defensible option |
| Stereotyped audience | Demographic assumptions without evidence | Replace with observed or inferred behavior at each viewing stage |

## 4. Completion criteria, summary record, and hand-off

Complete when the phase card's "Before leaving" check holds.

**Record for the decision summary (Analysis block):** the weakest link and the options weighed; the departure and fit dimensions chosen and why; the differentiation strategy and options weighed (or "fixed by evidence"). Mark any decision the evidence fixed as such. Update the Design Log: Communication Problem, Environment Profile, Visual source inventory, open issues.

**Hand-off to Phase 3:** Communication Problem; Environment Profile; Visual source inventory; differentiation strategy; tagged design implications; tension list; plus Phase 1's content volume, fixed assets, preferences, and exclusions.
