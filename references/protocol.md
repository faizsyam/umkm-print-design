# Exploration protocol and decision quality standard

Read once, on first entering Phase 2. Phase references apply it by pointing here. Depth (`SKILL.md` section 8) scales the work inside it and never removes a step.

## 1. Where the protocol applies

| Phase | Decisions | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Differentiation strategy, weakest link, departure and fit dimensions | Genuine option sets only; never manufacture alternatives |
| 3 | Core idea, style synthesis, hero (other Phase 3 decisions get the standard in section 3 at lighter depth) | Full protocol |
| 4 | Color strategy, typographic voice, logo mark and lockup, any open hero treatment | Full protocol; the mark uses the extended development in the Phase 4 reference; copy treatments and supporters get a lighter two-option comparison per real need |
| 5 | Spatial strategy | Full protocol |
| 1, 6 | None | Not applicable |

A consequential decision resolved in one pass, with no recorded challenge step, has not been decided.

## 2. Protocol steps

1. **Name the apparent option first,** as the benchmark. It may stay in the field as one candidate but must earn its place.
2. **Generate at least three candidate cards,** at most one apparent, at least one from beyond the apparent. Candidates differ on a structural axis. Source them differently: product truth, customer moment, environment, differentiator, category tension, name or origin, material, the Visual source inventory, the Phase 3 territory survey. Write and complete all cards before evaluating any.
3. **Evaluate in a different order than generated,** starting with the last, through the context matrix (3b). Record concrete strengths, weaknesses, and trade-offs, one line each, not adjectives.
4. **Challenge before choosing.** Name the runner-up (strongest remaining candidate). State the strongest case against the leader and for the runner-up. Remove the leader and see which of the rest would be chosen; then compare head to head. State the leader's margin as one decisive criterion (the single criterion on which its margin over the runner-up rests); if it cannot be stated, keep working. An apparent option wins only by naming the criterion on which the less obvious candidates fell short; "it is fitting" or "it is common for this category" is not a reason.
5. **Engineer the strongest solution** (3d): select outright, transplant a specific strength, combine under one logic (a hybrid, never a compromise), repair an identified weakness, or build a new candidate from what the comparison revealed.
6. **Record** (below) and, at the gate, print the entry.

**Decision record** (one per consequential decision, in this order, before moving on): apparent option; candidate cards (each with structural axis and source; at least three); evaluation, last candidate first; runner-up and the case against the leader; decisive criterion; engineered result with the mitigation or accepted cost for each weakness; what was kept from the others. A missing field means the decision is not made.

## 3. Decision quality standard (Phases 3 to 5; Phase 2 option sets use it at lighter depth)

Shallow, generic, or isolated decisions come from labels instead of candidates and from single-variable reasoning. The standard removes both.

**a. Candidate cards.** Fill every field the phase reference sets (Focused: the fields that tell candidates apart plus compatibility with each quality requirement). State lineage, technique, and behavior concretely, in the specialist vocabulary of (g). An option not describable field by field is not yet an option.

**b. Context matrix.** Evaluate every candidate against the full parameter set, not the one that favors it:
- communication objective and weakest link;
- the brief's quality requirements (including named style, color, and technique choices) and priorities: mark each candidate supports, strains, or neutral against every one; a candidate that contradicts one is eliminated unless `SKILL.md` section 9 forces the trade;
- brand identity and positioning;
- audience encounter at each viewing stage;
- environment: neighbors, light, distance, chosen departure and fit dimensions;
- content volume and capacity;
- space intent;
- medium, material, and production limits, including budget;
- cultural fit and sensitivities;
- renderability by an image model;
- coherence with every decision already made and those still to come.

Record strength, weakness, or neutral in one line per parameter wherever it matters to the candidate. An argument from one parameter ("it looks refined") is not an evaluation.

**c. Interaction check.** For each decision already made, state how the candidate amplifies it, conflicts with it, or is neutral. Name weaknesses that would compound across decisions.

**d. Strength-weakness engineering** (protocol step 5). For the chosen direction: every material weakness gets a named design move that reduces it, or is recorded as an accepted cost with the reason; every key strength gets a named place where it is amplified. A weakness with neither move nor acceptance is unresolved, and the decision is not made.

**e. Originality and sophistication tests.** Each leading candidate must pass:
- *Apparent-choice:* a stranger could not predict it from category and place alone.
- *Lineage:* the style, depiction, or technique can be named by its specific lineage and defining traits, not by a mood.
- *Synthesis:* it arises from this brief's conditions, not imported whole.
- *Depth:* a specialist would find it considered, not a label with adjectives.
- *Distinctness:* a merely competent candidate (fits, offends no one, memorable to no one) must beat an ambitious one on a stated decisive criterion; otherwise the ambitious one is developed further.

A failing candidate is rebuilt, not defended.

**f. Realization trace (Phases 4 and 5).** Every element and placement decision names the plan commitment it realizes (core idea, a style dimension, the hero, a governing principle). An element realizing none is removed; a plan commitment realized by no element is a gap to close.

**g. Specialist terminology.** Name things at a specialist's precision, using a term only if it can be defined:
- style by lineage and defining traits;
- imagery by depiction mode, technique, light, and finish;
- color by hue family, value structure, chroma, temperature, harmony structure, and area proportion;
- type by classification, stroke contrast, proportion, x-height, widths, and spacing;
- composition by axis, alignment, grid logic, figure-ground relation, focal hierarchy, grouping, visual weight, and tension;
- print by ink, substrate, and finish.

A prestigious word that cannot be defined is vagueness, and it is cut.
