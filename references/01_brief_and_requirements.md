# Phase 1: Brief

## Phase card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | A complete, classified, verifiable record of what is known, required, and uncertain, so no later phase guesses or invents |
| Receives | User message, attachments (logos, photos, references), prior conversation; for a revision, the log |
| Decisions | Necessity and provenance of every item; verbatim form of every mandatory string; gap class of every gap and which are blocking; quality translation of every stated quality and priority; intake questions; depth |
| Exploration | None: record and classify. An ambiguous phrase takes the interpretation best supported by the rest of the brief, tagged |
| Output | Brief Ledger (with quality translations), research questions (each naming the decision it could change), tensions; then the guided intake and the confirmed summary and mode (`00_conversation_ux.md` section 3). On confirmation the ledger becomes S (`SKILL.md` section 4) |
| Forward | S (ledger fields, quality translations, depth, mode); research questions; tensions; open flags (inferred setting and other tagged assumptions) |

## 1. Process

1. Read everything given, attachments included. Capture the print medium and context (A); infer what is missing and tag it.
2. Capture business and brand (B), content (C), preferences, fixed assets, exclusions, sensitivities (D). Treat references as evidence of taste, never templates.
3. Record every stated quality and priority in the user's words and translate it (D).
4. Sort every gap (E). Infer the inferable (tagged), pass the researchable to Phase 2, ask about blocking and valuable gaps through the guided intake.
5. Note tensions; set the depth.
6. Run the gate checks (`quality_control.md` section 1), show the compact summary, ask for confirmation and mode, and wait. Corrections update the ledger and show only changed rows. Phase 1 closes on the user's confirmation; then create the Design Log with S and the Phase 1 Forward block.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Print medium and context
Medium and format; faces; dimensions, aspect ratio, physical area; purpose and objectives; placement and viewing environment (distance, orientation, dwell time, visibility, lighting); production limits (material, finish, color limits, budget tier, quantity). If the setting is not stated, infer the most probable one, tag it provisional, and pass it on; ask only if plausible settings would need opposing designs. Record physical area beside content volume: their relation drives density, space, and scale later. Record any image generator or output language the user names (Phase 6 uses it) and the design aspect ratio: the user's intended ratio or, if not given, one derived from stated dimensions or the medium's standard proportions and tagged inferred. The piece is designed and generated at this ratio, never substituted.

### B. Business and brand
Name and industry; products and services; operating context; audience; differentiators; positioning and existing visual identity. Record what the user said, not what the category implies.

### C. Content
Capture all content that must or may appear (identifiers, text, product names, descriptions, taglines, offers, calls to action, prices, menus, trust indicators, contacts, social handles, addresses). For each item record: exact string, necessity, provenance, referent, face, share of content volume.

- **Verbatim:** spelling, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, price and contact formats, non-standard brand spellings. A suspected typo is preserved and flagged for the notes.
- **Never fabricate** prices, claims, testimonials, certifications, regulated or trust marks, contacts, ingredients, years in business, or statistics. Regulated or trust marks appear only when supplied.
- **Supplied codes** (QR, QRIS, barcode) are fixed assets: record as attached, with the size needed to scan. Never imitate one.
- **Supplied visuals and structure.** Record images, depicted people, mascots, characters, and handles as fixed assets: what is fixed, and whether permission to show a depicted person is known. Record whether the user wants a figure created and what it should do; an unmentioned figure is not assumed and may be offered once as a suggestion. Record structure the content already has (sequence, levels, item-value rows); later phases may express structure visually only from what is given.
- **Generated copy.** Functional labels derived from supplied content are acceptable. A headline, tagline, or descriptor is written only when the piece cannot function without one or the user requested copy: factual, modest, tagged creative choice, flagged in the notes.
- **Necessity.** Mandatory if the user said it must appear or the piece cannot work without it; optional if offered or helpful; excluded if the user said to leave it out. When unsure, treat as mandatory: ask once only if the answer would change content volume; otherwise Phase 5 fits it by demotion or grouping, never omission.

### D. Preferences, constraints, quality requirements
Style, color, mood, theme, references, assets, exclusions, cultural or religious sensitivities. From a reference, extract what the user responds to (structure, tone, density, material), never reproduce it. List each fixed asset with exactly what is fixed; for a supplied logo image record form, construction, and colors precisely so Phase 6 can describe it if the model cannot take an image.

**Quality requirements (binding).** Every experiential, tonal, affective, or priority statement, and every named style, color, or technique ("calm", "premium", "the brand comes first", "easy to scan"), however vague, is recorded in the user's words. A minimal brief makes these the primary requirements. For each write the **quality translation**: (1) visual targets in specialist terms (value key, chroma, temperature, density, formality, warmth, hierarchy priority, sensory cues); (2) two to four quality carriers from color and value key, shape language, surface, imagery, type treatment, spacing; (3) contradicting signals (dominant dark masses contradict "light and airy"; dense small type contradicts "easy to scan"). A whole-piece quality constrains the dominant field and largest masses, not only an accent. Qualities that pull apart ("refined" and "approachable") are recorded as a pair with a carrier for each side. Ambiguous words are interpreted by the rest of the brief and tagged.

### E. Gaps
- **Explicit:** record. **Inferable:** infer and tag. **Researchable:** pass to Phase 2 naming the decision it could change; drop it if it changes none.
- **Materially uncertain:** a **blocking gap** when its answer would change which face carries the message, whether mandatory content exists, or whether the piece can physically contain the content. Ask blocking gaps and valuable gaps (answers that change story, setting, content, or look) through the intake; never ask what can be derived or investigated.
- Business facts are never invented. A missing aesthetic preference is never blocking (Phase 3 decides it); a vague quality statement is not a missing preference but the requirement.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- A colleague could design from the ledger without the original message.
- Every mandatory string is exact; every item has necessity, provenance, and referent; fixed assets and exclusions are listed; no unsourced fact; no suggestion recorded as given.
- Every stated quality and priority is kept in the user's words with a full translation (a mood-word brief that yields no qualities fails).
- Physical area, design ratio, content volume, and setting are recorded (inferred and tagged if unstated); references recorded as taste, never layout requirements; supplied visual material listed as fixed assets.
- Intake stayed within limits, every question had an example or default, none answerable from the brief; summary shown with the mode question (Phase 2 starts only after the user confirms).
- Objection (print designer): are medium, size, material, and viewing conditions captured well enough to design for?
- Depth is recorded with the Full condition that set it, or none.
- Even a simple brief has a written ledger; simplicity shortens it, never removes it.
