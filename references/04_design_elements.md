# Phase 4: Design Elements

## Phase card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | A coherent visual system of color, type, identity unit (mark, wordmark, lockup), supporters, background and unifying devices; every element realizes a named plan commitment and has a role, treatment, job, weight, and space cost |
| Receives | Phase 3 plan (core idea, style, hero, principles, space intent, mark role, survey); Phase 2 Environment Profile and Visual source inventory; Phase 1 content, fixed assets, production limits, preferences |
| Decisions | Color strategy, palette, and value key; typographic roles and character; the identifier (fixed asset, or new mark with personality device, lockup, distinction mechanisms) and its identity forms; treatment grade of every text item; supporters (from the text-only baseline); background and unifying devices; system checks |
| Exploration | Full protocol with the extended development for the **mark and lockup** (C). Compact comparison for **color** (A), **type** (B), copy treatments (B2), and supporters (D) |
| Output | The element system: each element with role, treatment, plane, job, weight, space cost |
| Hand-off to Phase 5 | The element system; the identity unit with its mechanisms; constraints the prompt must preserve |

## 1. Process

1. Inventory fixed assets and production limits.
2. Decide color (A) and type (B) as provisional constraints for the mark; both must realize Phase 3 style dimensions 6 and 7.
3. Develop the mark and lockup (C). If it exposes a conflict with color or type, adjust them within this phase.
4. Decide copy treatment by content role (B2); run the text-only baseline and choose supporters (D).
5. Decide background and unifying devices (E).
6. Evaluate the system as a whole (F); gate.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Color
Inputs: Phase 3 chromatic stance; fixed brand colors and production limits (ink count, substrate, finish); the surrounding color field and ambient light. Options differ in logic (what color does here: organize, signal, separate, evoke, depart from the landscape), not only hue. Per option: roles (dominant, supporting, accent; number of hues and area proportion), harmony, value structure, chroma, temperature, contrast per viewing stage, behavior under production limits and in the real light, verified cultural associations, quality compatibility. Monochrome, dual-tone, restrained multicolor, and complex systems are all valid when justified.

**Value-key check (mandatory).** Compute the key from area proportions, counting the ground and every large band or block. Dark masses make a low-key piece however light the accents. Eliminate any option whose key contradicts a quality requirement; achieve departure through hue, chroma, or form within a compatible key. Describe colors to the model by named hues with qualifiers and roles; hex only for supplied brand colors or where precision is essential.

### B. Typography
Roles the content needs (display, supporting, informational, numerals and prices, small print) against the lettering of the surroundings and Phase 3 typographic expression. Options differ in stance: classification and construction per role (stroke contrast, proportion, x-height, widths, terminals, spacing), pairing logic, numerals and prices, readability at real distance and light, rendering risk.

**Differentiation by purpose.** Each role is styled to the success measure of its purpose: inform = ease of reading; hook = voice and memorability; transact = speed of finding the figure; identify = distinctiveness; act = findability. Roles present in the same text group must be told apart at a glance by at least one clear contrast (classification, weight, width, posture, case, scale, color); same-role items share one style everywhere. One family is acceptable only when weight, posture, case, or width contrast alone separates the roles; otherwise pair two families at most, with variants, to stay within what the model renders reliably. Keep roles few but never indistinguishable; sizes are ratios between roles that follow viewing stages. The wordmark's lettering must differ from the informational type in construction, weight, or treatment (C). Dense text raises rendering risk: plan structure (grouping, rows, fewer styles). Describe type by characteristics; name a font only for a stated brand requirement.

### B2. Content roles and copy treatment
Every text item is an element with a job; its treatment follows its content role, viewing stage, and the qualities it can carry (voice, warmth, confidence).
**Treatment follows purpose and rank.** An item is Designed when its role carries voice, persuasion, memory, or the transaction, or when its rank is first or second at any stage: scale alone is not treatment. A ranked item left plain must have plain setting recorded as the style's intentional choice, and then holds its rank through the other levers (position, isolation, contrast). Designing changes form only (letterform character, breaks, emphasis, mixed scale or weight within the line, container, integrated graphic detail) and never the words; wording changes only for copy the skill itself wrote.

- **Designed** for items carrying voice, persuasion, or memory (hooks, slogans, taglines, headline claims) and the transaction itself (prices, sizes, offers): graphic lettering with at least one deliberate device from the style's lettering lineage: line breaks that set rhythm; scale or weight contrast between lines or words; emphasis on the key word; baseline or alignment play; a shaped or cut container; a fitted motif or underline; a distinct color role; text following the arc or contour of the hero; a tag overlapping the hero; text emerging from or carried by the depicted thing; text passing in front of or behind part of the hero with every letterform kept whole.
- **Reassurance claims** that matter at approach: a marked line or badge-like container made as a graphic (never a regulated or trust mark).
- **Set** for informational and functional items (descriptions, item lists, addresses, contact lines): plain typesetting in the informational face, hierarchy by size, weight, spacing, grouping.

Rules: a voice-bearing line set in the informational face on bare ground is under-served. A hook or claim about the depicted thing is designed with it (an arc over a dome), within the hero's dominance. Designed treatments cost space and attention: no more than the tiers can carry, one lettering logic, none competing with the identity unit. Write each as a concise spec (letterform character, line breaks, emphasis, container, color role, relation to neighbors). Treatment never alters verbatim text: line breaks, scale, and emphasis only; no word omitted, added, or reordered. Compare two treatments per designed item in one line; test role-fit (name the role, show the treatment serves it).

### C. The identity unit: mark, wordmark, lockup
A basic, generic, or forgettable identifier fails even if it passes every test; one settled quickly was not scrutinized.

**Hard rules**
- A new identifier is never only a styled name: it carries a personality device (transformed lettering, a symbol integrated with the name, a distinctive silhouette or container, a symbol beside the name, or a combination), never decoration added to look designed.
- A supplied logo, plain wordmark included, is used as given: not redesigned, restyled, simplified, or recolored unless the user asks; an explicit request for plain text governs. With a supplied lockup, mark development is not required; decide only role, region, clear space, and relation to the system. With a mark and a separately supplied name, leave both unaltered and decide only arrangement, spacing, alignment, and color roles.
- A supplied mascot or character is a fixed asset, never redrawn; decide its role (part of the unit, hero, supporter). A figure the user wants created inside a new mark is a seed like any other, with extra scrutiny for cliché and renderable geometry. A tagline is copy (B2) unless supplied inside a lockup. An emblematic form never borrows the look of an official seal or certification.

**Extended development** (do not collapse into one pass)
1. **Concept mining.** Write raw seeds (Full: six or more; Focused: four) from different sources: the name's meaning, sound, shape, or initials; the product's defining truth; process or craft; origin; the customer's ritual; the inventory and survey; the environment; a visual signature of the business. Include seeds beyond the apparent. Keep only seeds that are true, ownable, and visually workable; discard any the business cannot honestly claim.
2. **Three developed candidates,** each a complete identity-unit idea: seed and personality device; construction family; lockup (arrangement, proportion, shared construction logic); behavior at small size. They differ in concept source or construction; at most one is apparent. Construction families to weigh: **enclosed** (symbol held within a boundary), **contained** (elements held by a frame or shape), **integrated** (symbol fused with lettering), **emblematic** (crest-like unified arrangement), **open** (freestanding symbol, no boundary).
3. **Scrutiny** of each candidate: *generic* (survives a competitor's name?); *story* (what true thing it says); *memory* (one-sentence description, redrawable from memory); *silhouette and reduction* (recognizable in one solid color, small); *device necessity* (without the device, is it a styled name?); *cliché* (the first association?); *renderability* (can a model reproduce the geometry from a description?).
4. **Revision pass:** at least one genuine revision after critique (transplant a strength, sharpen the idea, remove excess, make it more ownable while true). Then choose by the protocol with runner-up and decisive criterion.
5. **Winner tests:** *lockup* (below); *separation* from hero, lettering, and decoration; *text-neighborhood* (distinct beside the densest text, squinting). If the winner still feels basic, return to mining with the weakness named.
6. **Generation preparation.** Models render simple, geometric, clearly described forms far more reliably than intricate ones: simplify the execution of the idea, never the idea into something generic. Define geometry, key shapes, relationships, and color concretely.

**Lockup integration** (one unit, not two objects near each other). Decide:
- **Arrangement** (beside, stacked, held in one container, mark integrated into lettering), weighed against viewing stage and the region it occupies.
- **Shared construction logic:** a module from the mark (stroke weight, curvature, angle, proportion) the lettering echoes, so both look drawn by one hand; spacing derived from a measurable unit of the mark; a shared alignment axis; optical balance of mass.
- **Fixed scale proportion** between mark and wordmark (the unit scales as one); **one color role scheme** for the unit; which element leads inside it.
- **Separability:** the mark works alone; the wordmark alone still carries the construction logic. Mark and wordmark share logic but not form class (mark reads as shape and mass first, wordmark as lettering), so integration never becomes blending.
- Tests: *unit* (squint: one object?), *disconnection* (cover each in turn: does the other remain recognizably the same identity?).

**Distinction from type and content.** A mark reads as more type when it shares a form class with surrounding text, worst in text-heavy layouts; the wordmark is also not body type. Judge distinction as a relationship in the composition, for the **whole unit**. Use as many of these mechanisms as the composition needs (usually two or more in dense layouts): (1) *form class:* silhouette of shapes and masses; (2) *mass and texture:* compact solid weight against the even linear texture of text; (3) *color role:* a color or field the unit holds and body text does not share; (4) *scale step:* sized to the unit's role, not enlargement compensating for weak distinction; (5) *isolation:* protected clear space measured from the unit that nothing enters; (6) *containment or anchoring:* a container, field, or fixed anchor region repeated across faces. In dense layouts resolve distinction by composition, never endless enlargement. The unit's weight follows the Phase 3 mark role.

**Identity forms.** Decide which appear and where. The full unit identifies the source once per view; a second identifier in the same view is removed unless it does a job the unit cannot. A symbol-only form serves compact or repeated recognition and appears only where the viewer can link it to the name (the unit is in the same view or set, or the symbol is established or supplied). A name-only form exists only for a supplied wordmark. Every form's weight follows the mark role.

### D. Visual supporters
Find what the piece lacks beyond text, identity unit, and background, and meet each real lack with the best-suited resource in the piece's style register. No supporter is a default; a piece that needs none is a valid result, and a user's exclusion of imagery stands.

**Step 1: text-only baseline.** Imagine only required text, identity unit, and background. Name deficits as concrete observations across: visual interest and variety; evidence of the offer (use, sensory, or appetite cues); mood beyond type and color; scanning and hierarchy aids (entry points, markers, category cues, rhythm along long lists); balance (empty weight, lopsided mass); brand personality, material, cultural grounding; reading comfort of dense text; quality requirements type and color cannot carry alone. A deficit is real when its absence would weaken a stated quality, the message, the reading, or the balance, or when the piece at its density would read as blank or category-generic (a character deficit). Variety of scale, form class, density, and surface is read from the whole system (hero, ground, type), never answered by a supporter with no deficit. A text-dense piece usually shows deficits of interest, scanning, and rhythm; the answer is purposeful visuals and structure, never more words or filler.

**Order of remedy: enrich before add.** Close a deficit by the route that adds the least new weight: (1) *deepen the main elements*: integrate layered information into the hero and main text, give them finish, crop, or surface that rewards a closer look; (2) *link* the major elements (shared axis, overlap, shared color or shape, echoed motif, connector); (3) *quiet surface*: subtle pattern, texture, tonal steps; (4) *small supporters*: simple icons, spot forms, dividers, an ownable recurring motif from the inventory; (5) *typographic and edge detail*: secondary-line detail, labels, container or border character. Each stays below the information it serves and inside the space intent.

**Step 2: weigh at least two families per real deficit** and adapt the chosen one to the style register (premium: fine monoline, one ink, one grid, low contrast; minimal: few, small, uniform; clean: geometric, equal weight; warm: hand-drawn, cut-paper, stamp-like, bolder motifs). Draw forms from the inventory and survey; the category's obvious decorative signifiers are the benchmark to beat.

| Family | Job | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Icons and symbols (icon set, pictogram, list marker, level mark, channel glyph) | Quick recognition of parallel items; action understood without words; rhythm along a list; a supplied degree at a glance | Parallel items or lines need quick identification and meaning needs no explanation | Ambiguous or merely decorative; a lone self-evident item; imitating a regulated, safety, or trust symbol; a level mark showing anything not supplied (never a rating); a glyph of an unlisted channel or invented logo |
| Subject, in-use, place imagery | Proof of the offer; the outcome or experience; location and atmosphere | The viewer must see or sense the offer, its benefit is experiential, or the place is part of the value, and the generator can depict it accurately | Appearance is irrelevant or not accurately renderable (a depiction that misrepresents the offer is a fault); generic place; complex hands, crowds, fine faces |
| Figures (see below) | One role per appearance | A role answers a real deficit and the figure is supplied or wanted | Unasked; another element holds the role; permission for a supplied person unknown; a real person's likeness or implied endorsement |
| Spot illustration | Personality, story, or mood at a content block | A block needs a small visual cue | It only fills space or crowds content |
| Image treatment and sets (cutout or mask; image set) | Cutout lets an image overlap, wrap, or host integrated text; set gives breadth across equally important images | The image must interact with layout and its edge reads clean; several subjects share equal importance and no hero leads | Original surroundings carry meaning; edge would render poorly; one message must dominate; set exceeds what the generator keeps consistent |
| Pattern and texture | Ownership and rhythm in a ground; tactile richness | The ground needs identity or richness and can stay secondary | Information sits directly on it; it muddies fine content; it fills a quiet field the plan reserved |
| Borders and frames | Border closes the composition; frame isolates one item | The composition needs closure, or one item needs emphasis | Near the trim (keep inside the safe margin or bleed deliberately); dense layout; many framed items so none stands out |
| Dividers | Separate content groups | Space alone cannot separate | Spacing already does; an ornament interrupts reading |
| Pointers, connectors, direction | Tie a depicted part to its label or an item to its value; direct attention; show a given sequence | A relationship, direction, or sequence would otherwise be unclear | Proximity already groups; no given order; pointers many or crossing |
| Shapes (block, band, tag, edge shape) | Zone or base for content; segment or anchor an edge; flag one short fact; give a transition direction | A region needs separation, one fact truly needs notice, or a transition should feel directed | Carries nothing; a band cuts a focal element; a tag flags a long or unimportant message, competes with other tags, imitates a trust mark, or states an invented claim |
| Lettering-as-graphic and numerals | Hook, price emphasis, brand memory | As B2 | As B2 |

**Figures (mascot, character, person).** A designed element, never a default. Origin decides entry: supplied → fixed asset; wanted by the user (or accepted after a labeled suggestion) → created here; otherwise absent. Each appearance names one role:

| Role | Job | Standing | Care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify | Part of the identity unit | Per mark role | Follows C; reads as silhouette and in one color |
| Lead | The hero | Leads, within hosting capacity | One clear pose and expression; no competing mark |
| Demonstrate | Shows the offer in use | Subordinate to the offer, which stays the visual subject | Simple interaction; offer rendered accurately |
| Guide | Leads the eye or hand to a target | Below the information it points to | Gesture or gaze ends on the target, covers nothing |
| Carry mood | Humanity at small scale | Below required information | Same figure, reduced, in the system's line quality |

One figure, one design, defined once (form class, proportions, silhouette, signature trait, palette) and described identically everywhere, so roles change scale and detail, never identity. A figure in the mark recurs elsewhere only for a job the mark does not do, never as a second identifier. A created figure is fictional and non-identifiable, never a real person's likeness or an implied customer, staff member, or endorsement; check dress, features, and gesture for cultural fit; render only in forms the generator holds reliably.

**Step 3: supporter card** per chosen supporter: deficit answered; family and form; the one distinct job (balance, hierarchy, readability, style reinforcement, mood, personality); relation to hero and identity unit; scale tier, region, plane; weight ceiling; space cost; renderability. Each supporter shares the style's form language, line quality, and finish; micro-details are few.

### E. Background, unifying devices, and other systems
Each only where the piece needs it; none is a default.
- **Ground:** the base field that unifies the piece, separates it from its landscape, and keeps contrast with foreground content. **Layered ground:** where the layering stance calls for it, in more than one quiet step (tonal fields, overlapping shapes, surface detail); quiet where information lives; in a flat style the main way to enrich the piece.
- **Scrim:** a translucent tonal layer behind a text zone, only when content must sit on an uneven image or field and a quiet ground cannot be planned (quiet ground is the first remedy).
- **Edge behavior:** which elements run off the trim (ground, hero, large blocks, surface) and which stay inside; mandatory content, the identity unit, and fine borders stay inside the safe margin.
- **Zone transitions:** a plain edge, or a curved or angled boundary where the transition should feel directed, never colliding with aligned content.
- **Surface:** pattern or texture (D) as secondary, quiet under information, never filling a quiet field the plan reserved.
- **Other systems** (shapes, framing, material effects, image treatments, information graphics): include only what improves the design, each with a named job, drawn from the D catalogue. Lighting and shadow are decisions: state direction and softness, or leave them out.

### F. Element evaluation in the system
Each element is judged in relation to the whole.
- **Realization trace.** Each element names the plan commitment it realizes (core idea, a style dimension, hero, principle, quality requirement); an element realizing none is removed; a commitment realized by no element is a gap to close. Every quality requirement needs a visible carrier among the elements.
- **Effect and interaction.** For each element, one line on where it supports or strains the plan, the audience encounter, the environment, capacity, and renderability, wherever it matters; for each pair of major elements (identity unit and type, hero and background, color and imagery, supporters and hero) say whether they amplify, compete, or are neutral, and repair any competition.
- **Element allowance:** sum space costs against the space intent; if over, remove or merge elements before shrinking any.
- **Removal test:** if removing an element changes nothing the piece needs, remove it (after asking whether a second job its form could carry earns it a place; extra jobs never raise space cost or weight above its ceiling).
- **Distinct-job check:** two elements with the same job merge toward the one serving the hierarchy better.
- **Weight-ceiling check** at each viewing stage: no decoration outranks the required information it serves, a forward plane's prominence included. **Plane check:** each element sits on one plane and its cues agree.
- **Single voice and consistency:** line quality, finish, abstraction level, light direction, rendering treatment read as one designer's work.
- **Subtraction pass:** would removing one more thing improve the piece? **Additive pass:** re-read the text-only baseline; if interest, evidence of the offer, scanning aids, or balance are still lacking, close each real gap by the order of remedy (D), never filler. **Connection check:** every major element is tied to at least one other by a stated link. **Character test:** with the name covered, the system still carries at least two ownable character carriers from different levers.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- A designer could build every element from the specification; every element names the plan commitment it realizes and every commitment is realized.
- Color, type, and mark each show their comparison (apparent option named, options, decisive criterion); palette and type were judged against surroundings and light, not only the brand; the value key supports every quality requirement.
- Mark: fixed asset untouched, or the extended development is recorded (seeds, three candidates, scrutiny, revision, tests); personality device present; mark and wordmark pass the unit and disconnection tests; the unit passes text-neighborhood with named distinction mechanisms; no second identifier; no cliché; geometry describable in a few concrete shapes.
- Each text item's treatment fits its content role and rank; no first- or second-rank text left as scaled plain type without a recorded style reason; roles in one group are distinguishable by a clear typographic contrast and same-role items share one style; font names appear only for a stated brand requirement.
- The text-only baseline was run; each supporter names its deficit, holds a distinct job, and stays below the information it serves; whole families were not dismissed without comparison; each figure names origin and role and shares one design; no invented level, rating, or trust mark.
- Character carriers and links are named for the system; total fits the space intent; treatment is consistent; shadow or glow exists only by decision.
- Objection raised and resolved (owner of the riskiest decision, usually the mark: graphic designer or senior art director; two objections at Full depth, the second often from the prompt specialist on renderability).

**Entry content:** color strategy (with value key) and typographic voice (with designed copy treatments by role): apparent option, candidates (name, strength, weakness), choice and why it beat the runner-up. The identity unit: the truth it expresses, personality device, construction family chosen against the others, runner-up, how mark and wordmark integrate, distinction mechanisms used (or "fixed asset"), any symbol-only form and where. Each supporter with the deficit it answers (or the finding that the baseline has none); any figure with origin and roles; any system decision with real alternatives.
