## 1. Process

1. Inventory fixed assets and production constraints.
2. Decide the color strategy (A) and typographic voice (B) by the protocol, as provisional constraints for the mark; both must realize the Phase 3 style dimensions (chromatic stance; typographic expression).
3. Develop the mark and lockup through the extended development (C). If it reveals a conflict with color or type, return within the phase and adjust them.
4. Decide copy treatment by content role (B2); run the text-only baseline and select visual supporters (D).
5. Decide background and unifying devices (E) and any additional systems (F).
6. Evaluate the system as a whole (G): effect profiles, interactions, realization trace, element allowance check, removal and subtraction passes.
7. Record candidates and choices; pass the gate; guided mode: print the Phase 4 entry, ask for confirmation, stop (Phase 5 begins on the go-ahead); auto mode: print nothing and continue.

## 2. Detailed guidance

### A. Color system

**Analyze:** the Phase 3 chromatic stance and the light and atmosphere it sets; fixed brand colors and production limits (ink count, substrate, finish); the surrounding color field and ambient light from the Environment Profile.

**Candidate card fields (color strategy)**
1. The logic: what color does in this piece (organize, signal, separate, evoke, depart from the landscape).
2. Roles: dominant, supporting, accent where relevant; number of hues and area proportion of each.
3. Harmony structure, value structure, chroma, temperature.
4. Contrast structure for each viewing stage.
5. Relationship to imagery and to the mark.
6. Behavior under production limits and in the real light.
7. Cultural and category associations (verified).
8. Value key (area-weighted light, mid, dark balance), chroma, temperature, and compatibility with each quality requirement.

**Evaluate**
- Monochrome, dual-tone, restrained multicolor, and complex systems are all valid when justified.
- Whether the palette departs from or sits within the landscape, and on which dimension.
- **Value-key check:** compute the key from area proportions, counting the ground and every large band or block. Dark masses make a low-key piece however light the accents. Eliminate a candidate whose key contradicts a quality requirement; achieve departure through hue, chroma, or form within a compatible key.
- The context matrix, interaction with style and hero, and the originality tests.
- Describe colors to a model by named hues with qualifiers and roles; use hex only for supplied brand colors or where precision is essential.

### B. Typography

**Analyze:** the Phase 3 typographic expression; the roles the content needs (display, supporting, informational, numerals and prices, small print); the lettering of the surroundings.

**Candidate card fields (typographic voice)**
1. Classification and stance for each role.
2. Construction traits: stroke contrast, proportion, x-height, widths, terminals, spacing and line-spacing character.
3. Relationships between roles (pairing logic) and size ratios that follow viewing stages.
4. Treatment of numerals and prices.
5. Readability at the real distance and light.
6. How the wordmark's lettering relates to and differs from the informational type (C, unit distinction).
7. Rendering risk for an image model.
8. Treatment of designed copy items by content role (B2).

**Evaluate**
- Keep roles few; set sizes as ratios between roles, not independent sizes.
- Dense text raises rendering risk; plan structure accordingly (grouping, rows, fewer styles).
- Describe type to a model by characteristics, since font names are unreliable; name a font only for a stated brand requirement.
- The context matrix and interaction with mark and hero.

### B2. Content roles and copy treatment

**Purpose.** Every text item is an element with a job; its treatment follows that job, not only its size.

**Analyze** each text item: content role (identify, hook, transact, inform, reassure, act), viewing stage, and the quality requirements it can carry (voice, warmth, confidence).

**Decide the treatment grade** (a one-line comparison of two treatments per designed item)
- **Designed** for items carrying voice, persuasion, or memory (hooks, slogans, taglines, headline claims) and for the transaction itself (prices, sizes, offers): graphic lettering with at least one deliberate device from the style's lettering lineage. Devices: line breaks that set rhythm; scale or weight contrast between lines or words; emphasis on the key word; baseline or alignment play; a shaped or cut container; a fitted motif or underline; a distinct color role; text following an arc or contour of the hero; a tag overlapping the hero; text that appears to emerge from or be carried by the depicted thing; text passing in front of or behind part of the hero with every letterform kept whole.
- **Reassurance claims** that matter at the approach stage: a marked line or badge-like container made as a graphic (never a regulated or trust mark).
- **Set** for informational and functional items (descriptions, item lists, addresses, contact lines): plain typesetting in the informational face, hierarchy by size, weight, spacing, and grouping.

**Rules**
- A voice-bearing line set in the informational face on bare ground, differing only in size or color, is under-served.
- A hook or claim about the depicted thing is designed with it: consider a treatment sharing the thing's shape or motion (an arc over a dome, a flow with an overflow), within the hero's dominance (Phase 5, A2).
- Designed treatments cost space and attention: no more than the tiers can carry, all in one lettering logic, none competing with the identity unit.
- Write each treatment as a concise spec (letterform character, line breaks, emphasis, container, color role, relation to neighbors) so Phases 5 and 6 can place and describe it.
- Treatment never alters verbatim text: line breaks, scale, and emphasis only; no word omitted, added, or reordered.

**Test.** *Role-fit:* name each item's role and show its treatment serves it.

### C. The identity unit: logo mark, wordmark, and lockup

A basic, generic, or forgettable identifier is a failure even if it passes every test. One settled quickly was not scrutinized.

**Hard rules**
- **A new identifier is never only a styled name.** Every identifier the skill creates carries a personality device: transforming part of the lettering, integrating a symbol with the name, a distinctive silhouette or container, a symbol beside the name, or a combination. Never decoration added to look designed.
- **Fixed assets are respected.** A supplied logo, including a plain wordmark, is used as given: not redesigned, restyled, simplified, or recolored unless the user asks. An explicit request for plain text governs. With a supplied lockup, record that mark development was not required; remaining decisions are its role, region, clear space, and relation to the system. With a supplied mark and a separately supplied name, leave both unaltered and decide only arrangement, spacing, alignment, and color roles.
- **Characters, taglines, and seals.** A supplied mascot or brand character is a fixed asset, never redrawn; decide its role (part of the unit, hero, or supporter). A figure the user wants created as part of a new mark is a mark seed like any other: same scrutiny, with extra attention to cliché and to reduced, renderable geometry; its roles beyond the mark follow Figures in D. A tagline is copy (B2) unless supplied as part of a lockup, where it belongs to the fixed asset. An emblematic form is a construction choice only and never borrows the look of an official seal or certification.

**Extended development** (do not collapse into one pass)
1. **Concept mining.** Write raw concept seeds (Full: at least six; Focused: at least four) from different sources: the name's meaning, sound, shape, or initials; the product's defining truth; process or craft; origin; the customer's ritual; the Visual source inventory and Phase 3 survey; the Environment Profile; a visual signature of the business. Include seeds beyond the apparent. Mark each as true, ownable, and visually workable, or discard it; discard any seed the business cannot honestly claim.
2. **Three developed candidates.** Each candidate card is a complete identity unit idea: seed and personality device; construction family; the lockup (arrangement, proportion, shared construction logic, see below); behavior at small size. Candidates differ in concept source or construction; at most one is the apparent option. Weigh these construction families as genuine alternatives: **enclosed** (symbol held within a boundary), **contained** (elements held together by a frame or shape), **integrated** (symbol fused with lettering), **emblematic** (a unified crest-like arrangement), **open** (a freestanding symbol with no boundary).
3. **Scrutiny of each candidate** (eight questions):
   - *Generic:* if the name were swapped for a competitor's, would it still work?
   - *Story:* what true thing does it say, and can the business claim it?
   - *Memory:* can it be described in one sentence and redrawn from memory?
   - *Silhouette:* is it recognizable in a single solid color?
   - *Device necessity:* without the device, does it fall back to a styled name?
   - *Cliché:* is it the first association?
   - *Lockup:* do mark and wordmark read as one object while each keeps its own form?
   - *Renderability:* can an image model reproduce its geometry from a description?
4. **Revision pass.** At least one genuine revision after critique: transplant a strength, sharpen the idea, remove excess, ask what would make it more ownable while still true. Then choose by the protocol, including runner-up and decisive criterion.
5. **Tests** (five): *isolation*; *reduction* (one color, small); *separation* (from hero, lettering, decoration); *text-neighborhood* (beside the densest text, squinting); *memory* (describe, then redraw). If the winner still feels basic, return to concept mining with the weakness named.
6. **Preparation for generation.** Image models render simple, geometric, clearly described forms far more reliably than intricate ones. Simplify the execution of the idea, never the idea itself into something generic. Define geometry, key shapes, relationships, and color concretely.

**Lockup integration** (one cohesive unit, not two objects placed near each other). Decide each explicitly:
- **Arrangement:** how the two combine (beside, stacked, held in one container, mark integrated into the lettering), weighed against the viewing stage and the format region the unit occupies.
- **Shared construction logic:** a common module drawn from the mark (stroke weight, curvature, angle, proportion unit) that the wordmark lettering echoes, so they look drawn by one hand.
- **Spatial relationship:** spacing derived from a measurable unit of the mark, not arbitrary; a shared alignment axis (baseline, cap height, or center); optical balance of mass between mark and lettering.
- **Weight and scale relationship:** a fixed proportion between mark and wordmark; the unit scales as one.
- **Color logic:** one role scheme for the whole unit; mark and wordmark share a color family with defined roles.
- **Internal hierarchy:** which element leads inside the unit; the unit reads in a single glance as one object.
- **Separability:** the mark works alone; the wordmark alone still carries the same construction logic.
- **Form class:** mark and wordmark share logic but not form class (the mark reads as shape and mass first, the wordmark as lettering), so integration never becomes blending.

Tests: *unit test* (squint: one object?); *disconnection test* (cover the mark, then the wordmark: does each remain recognizably of the same identity?).

**Distinction of the identity unit from typography and content.** A mark reads as more type when it shares a form class with the surrounding text, most severely in text-heavy layouts. The wordmark is also not body type: it must differ from the informational typography in construction, weight, or treatment. Judge distinction as a relationship in the composition, applied to the **unit as a whole**. Use the six distinction mechanisms as the composition requires, usually two or more in dense layouts:
1. **Form class:** a silhouette of shapes and masses; a device formed from lettering reads as shape first, letter second.
2. **Mass and texture:** compact solid weight against the even linear texture of text.
3. **Color role:** a color or field the unit holds and body text does not share.
4. **Scale step:** a deliberate size step sized to the unit's role, not enlargement compensating for weak distinction.
5. **Isolation:** protected clear space, measured from the unit, that no text, ornament, or pattern enters.
6. **Containment or anchoring:** a container, field, or fixed anchor region with a stable edge and position, repeated consistently across faces.

In dense layouts resolve distinction by composition (the unit's own region, neighboring type not echoing its form), never by endless enlargement. The unit's weight follows the Phase 3 mark role.

**Identity forms.** Decide which forms appear, and where. The full unit identifies the source once in a view; a second identifier in the same view is removed unless it does a job the unit cannot. The symbol-only form serves compact or repeated recognition (a small area, a later viewing stage, a repeat across faces) and appears only where the viewer can link it to the name: the unit is already present in the same view or set, or the symbol is established or supplied. A name-only form exists only for a supplied wordmark. The weight of every form follows the mark role.

### D. Visual supporters

**Purpose.** Find what the piece lacks beyond text, identity unit, and background, and meet each real lack with the resource best suited to it, in the piece's own style register. Restraint is carried by scale, count, finish, and form language, not by the absence of supporters where a real deficit exists.

**Selection principle.** No supporter is a default; the whole context decides. Choose one only when it answers a real deficit, holds a job no other element holds, and takes a weight ceiling below the required information it serves. A piece that needs none is a valid result.

**Step 1: text-only baseline.** Imagine the piece as only its required text, identity unit, and background. Name the deficits as concrete observations across:
- visual interest and variety (any focal point or surface other than type);
- evidence of the offer (can the viewer see or sense what is offered; use, sensory, or appetite cues);
- mood and emotion beyond what type and color deliver;
- scanning and hierarchy aids (entry points, markers, category cues, rhythm along long lists);
- balance (areas of weight with nothing in them, lopsided mass);
- brand personality, material, and cultural grounding;
- reading comfort of dense text (breaks, row guidance);
- quality requirements whose carriers type and color cannot supply alone.

A deficit is real when its absence would weaken a stated quality, the message, the reading, or the balance. Record each.

**Step 2: survey the families.** For each real deficit consider at least two families before choosing, and adapt the chosen one to the style register.

| Family and elements | Job each performs | Use when; avoid when | Register adaptations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Icons and symbols: icon set; instruction pictogram; list marker; level mark; contact and channel glyph | Icon set: recognition of parallel items. Pictogram: action or direction understood without words. Marker: rhythm along a list. Level mark: a supplied degree at a glance. Draw forms from the Visual source inventory and Phase 3 survey; the category's obvious decorative signifiers are the benchmark to beat.

**Figures (mascot, character, or person).** A figure is a designed element, never a default. Origin decides entry: supplied, it is a fixed asset; wanted by the user (or accepted after a labeled suggestion), it is created here; otherwise absent. Every appearance names one role:

| Role | Job | Standing in the hierarchy | Care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify | Part of the identity unit, as its mark or personality device | Per the mark role | Follows C; reads as a silhouette and in one color |
| Lead | The hero, carrying the glance message | Leads, within the hero's hosting capacity | One clear pose and expression; no competing mark |
| Demonstrate | Shows the offer in use or the service performed | Subordinate to the offer, which stays the visual subject | Simple interaction; the offer rendered accurately |
| Guide | Leads the eye or hand to a named target | Below the information it points to | Gesture or gaze ends on the target and covers nothing |
| Carry mood | Personality or humanity at small scale, as illustration, icon, pictogram, or marker | Below required information | The same figure, reduced, in the system's line quality |

Principles for every role: one figure, one design, defined once (form class, proportions, silhouette, signature trait, palette) and described the same way everywhere, so roles change scale and detail, never identity; a figure inside the mark recurs elsewhere only for a job the mark does not do, never as a second identifier; a created figure is fictional and non-identifiable, never a likeness of a real person and never implying a customer, staff member, or endorsement; its dress, features, and gesture are checked for cultural fit and tokenism; render it only in forms the generator holds reliably (reduced shapes, few simple poses; hands, faces, and groups only where reliable).

**Step 3: record a supporter card** for each chosen supporter: the deficit answered; family and form; the one distinct job (balance, hierarchy, readability, style reinforcement, mood, personality); relation to hero and identity unit; scale tier and region; plane; weight ceiling; space cost; renderability.

**Evaluate**
- Each supporter answers a named deficit and shares the style's form language, line quality, and finish.
- Supporters exist only for real deficits; a baseline with none is a valid result, recorded with its reason, and a user's exclusion of imagery stands. Read variety of scale, form class, density, and surface from the whole system (hero, ground, type, supporters); never answer it with a supporter that has no deficit.
- A text-dense piece usually shows deficits of interest, scanning, and rhythm. The answer is purposeful visuals and structure, never more words and never filler.
- **Weight ceiling and distinct job.** Each supporter card states both; the system-level checks in G enforce them.
- Micro-details are few; accumulation is noise.
- Accuracy and reliability: depictions of the offer match what is offered; avoid real identifiable people, tokenistic portrayals, and compositions that depend on complex hands, crowds, or fine faces unless reliability allows. Supplied people, characters, and photographs follow the fixed-asset rules; created figures follow Figures above.

### E. Background and unifying devices

**Analyze:** color, texture, imagery, tonal structure, spatial structure.

**Consider:** the relationship to the foreground; whether connecting devices are needed to unify zones; that the background separates the piece from its landscape (judge it against neighbors and light).

**Decide** (each only where the piece needs it; none is a default)
- **Ground:** the base field, solid or graphic, that unifies the piece and separates it from its landscape; keeps contrast with the foreground content.
- **Scrim:** a translucent tonal layer behind a text zone, only when content must sit on an image or field of uneven contrast and a quiet ground cannot be planned instead; quiet ground is the first remedy.
- **Edge behavior:** which elements run off the trim (ground, hero, large blocks, surface) and which stay inside. Mandatory content, the identity unit, and fine borders stay inside the safe margin.
- **Zone transitions:** how adjacent zones meet: a plain edge, or a curved or angled boundary where the transition should feel directed, never colliding with aligned content.
- **Surface:** pattern or texture (D) when the ground needs identity or richness; secondary, quiet under information, never filling a quiet field the plan reserved.
- **Layered ground:** where the Phase 3 layering stance calls for it, the ground in more than one quiet step (tonal fields, overlapping shapes, surface detail), carrying richness and separating the planes in front; quiet where information lives, and in a flat style the main way to enrich the piece while it stays flat.

**Evaluate:** it stays quiet where information lives; whether its surface (grain, material, tonal pattern) should carry a supporter job (D) rather than stay plain; its effect profile (G) on mark, hero, and text.

### F. Additional design systems

**Analyze:** graphic shapes, framing, borders, patterns, textures, material effects, lighting, shadows, image treatments, iconography, information graphics, spatial transitions.

**Evaluate:** include only what improves the design, each with a named job; each system is drawn from the catalogue in D, so its use and avoid principles apply. Lighting and shadow are decisions, not inherited defaults: state direction and softness, or leave them out.

### G. Element selection and evaluation in the system

**Purpose.** Select each element for what it does to the whole design, evaluated in relation to the complete system, not in isolation.

**1. Effect profile (every element).** State the element's effect (supports, neutral, or strains, with one concrete line wherever it matters) on: brand design and positioning; the core idea and theme; the style dimensions; the communication objective and weakest link; the audience encounter at each viewing stage; the environment (neighbors, light, distance; fit and departure); content capacity and space cost; production and renderability.

**2. Interaction check.** For each pair of major elements (identity unit and type, hero and background, color and imagery, supporters and hero), state whether they amplify, compete, or are neutral; repair any competition.

**3. Realization trace** (`protocol.md` 3f). Each element names the plan commitment it realizes; an element realizing none is removed; a commitment realized by no element is a gap to close. Quality requirements count as plan commitments: each needs a visible carrier among the elements.

**4. System-level checks**
- **Element allowance:** every element has a space cost; sum the costs against the Phase 3 space intent; if over, remove or merge elements before shrinking any.
- **Removal test:** if removing the element changes nothing the piece needs, remove it, after asking whether a second job its form or position could carry (information, structure, guidance, emphasis, mood) would earn it a distinct place. Extra jobs never raise space cost or weight above its ceiling and are never added to justify it.
- **Distinct-job check:** name each element's distinct job; two elements with the same job merge toward the one that serves the hierarchy better.
- **Weight-ceiling check:** read each viewing stage; no decoration outranks the required information it serves, a forward plane's added prominence included.
- **Plane check:** each element sits on one plane and its layer cues agree with that plane.
- **Cumulative effect and single voice:** does the whole read as one designer's work?
- **Consistency:** line quality, finish, level of abstraction, light direction, rendering treatment.
- **Subtraction pass:** remove one more thing and see whether the piece improves.
- **Additive pass:** re-read the text-only baseline (D). If the system still lacks interest, evidence of the offer, scanning aids, or balance, close each real gap with a purposeful supporter, never filler.

System-wide coherence outranks isolated appeal.

## 3. Failure modes

| Failure | Detection | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Styled name passed as identity | Without the device the identifier is plain lettering | Return to concept mining; require a personality device |
| Disconnected mark and wordmark | Unit and disconnection tests fail; spacing, alignment, or color logic arbitrary | Redesign the lockup with shared construction logic and derived spacing |
| Identity unit absorbed by type | Fails text-neighborhood test; wordmark echoes body type | Add distinction mechanisms by composition; differentiate wordmark from informational type |
| Mark settled in one pass | No seeds, three candidates, revision, or tests | Run the extended development |
| Cliché mark | The mark is the first association | Re-mine beyond the apparent; apply the decisive criterion honestly |
| Mark too intricate for a model | Geometry cannot be described in a few concrete shapes | Simplify execution, keep the idea |
| Supplied logo touched | Any redesign, restyle, simplification, or recoloring | Restore as given |
| Element with no plan source | Realization trace finds no commitment it realizes | Remove it, or return to Phase 3 if the plan has a gap |
| Element judged in isolation | No effect profile or interaction check | Complete both |
| Decoration without a deficit | A supporter answering no deficit | Remove it |
| Space cost ignored | Total exceeds the space intent | Remove or merge elements before shrinking any |
| Palette judged only against the brand | No reference to surrounding field, light, or full parameter set | Re-judge against the Environment Profile and context matrix |
| Font names used as direction | Font names without a stated brand requirement | Replace with characteristics |
| Inconsistent rendering | Mixed line quality, finish, or light direction | Unify treatment |
| Default lighting and shadow | Shadow or glow no decision explains | Decide or remove |
| First-candidate color or type | No candidate cards or alternatives | Run the protocol |
| Value-key drift | Dominant field or large bands signal the opposite of a stated quality | Recompute the key; revise color strategy before any element |
| Voice-bearing copy set plain | A hook, slogan, or tagline typeset like informational text | Apply B2: choose a designed treatment |
| Text-only look | Visual content is type plus one motif; no variety of scale, form, or surface | Run the text-only baseline and additive pass |
| Supporter as filler | A supporter names no deficit, or shares no form language with the system | Remove it or re-derive it from a deficit |
| Supporter families unexamined | Whole families dismissed without comparison | Weigh at least two families per real deficit |
| Duplicate jobs | Two elements do the same job (two identifiers, separators, flags) | Keep the one serving the hierarchy better; merge or remove the other |
| Decoration outranks information | An ornament, pattern, tag, or border out-weighs the information it serves | Lower it to its weight ceiling, or remove it |
| Second identifier | A symbol-only or repeated identifier where the unit already identifies the source, adding no new job | Remove it |
| Invented asset | A rating or level mark nobody supplied, or a figure that is a likeness of a real person or implies an endorsement | Remove it |
| Unwanted or roleless figure | A figure the user neither supplied nor wanted, or with no role only it performs | Remove it, or offer it once as a suggestion |
| Drifting figure | The same figure drawn differently across roles or faces | Define it once; restate it identically |

## 4. Completion criteria, summary print, and hand-off

Complete when the phase card's "Before leaving" check holds.

**Print for the decision summary (Design Elements entry, at the gate):**
- color strategy (with value key) and typographic voice (with designed copy treatments by role): apparent option, candidates compared (one line each: name, strength, weakness), selection and why it beat the runner-up;
- the identity unit: the truth it expresses, personality device, construction family chosen against the other candidates, runner-up, how mark and wordmark are integrated, distinction mechanisms used (or "fixed asset"), and any symbol-only form with where it appears;
- each supporter with the deficit it answers and the families weighed (or the finding that the baseline has none); any figure with its origin and roles; any system decision that had real alternatives.

Update the Design Log: color, type, mark and lockup, element costs, open issues.

**Hand-off to Phase 5:** the element system with every element's role, treatment, plane, relationships, effect profile, and space cost; the identity unit with its mechanisms; the constraints the prompt must preserve.
