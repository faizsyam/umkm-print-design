# Phase 3: Design Plan

## Phase card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | One unified, defensible, original direction: brand design, theme, core idea, style, hero, governing principles (incl. space intent and mark role), synthesizing all findings into a single concept, not a style assigned to each insight |
| Receives | Phase 2 hand-off (Communication Problem, Environment Profile, Visual source inventory, differentiation, implications, tensions); Phase 1 content volume, fixed assets, preferences, exclusions, quality translations |
| Decisions | Territory survey; brand design and theme; core idea, style (seven dimensions), hero or its deliberate absence; governing principles (F); carriers for every quality requirement; resolution of each Phase 2 tension; stress-test results |
| Exploration | Full protocol (`protocol.md` section 2) for core idea, style, hero, in that order: the winning idea is a criterion for style, the winning style a criterion for the hero. Write no style card before the core idea is chosen. Each decision has at least one card from the survey, inventory, or departure dimensions. Check renderability throughout |
| Output | The Design Plan and the record of rejected directions |
| Hand-off to Phase 4 | Plan (core idea, style across seven dimensions, hero, principles incl. space intent and mark role), the survey (for mark seeds), Environment Profile, Visual source inventory, Phase 1 content, fixed assets, constraints |

## 1. Process

1. Decide brand design and theme (A), survey territories (B).
2. Decide the core idea (C), then style (D), then hero (E), each by the full protocol.
3. Decide the governing principles and resolve the Phase 2 tensions (F).
4. Evaluate the assembled plan as one system and run the stress tests (G); gate.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Brand design and theme
Compare two ways to express the brand's impression in this environment, then state **brand design** (enduring identity: fixed assets bind it; for a new brand, proposed with restraint proportional to evidence) separately from **theme** (the idea for this piece, built on it) so the theme can vary without damaging the brand. Check the theme does not contradict the brand design (else the theme is wrong, or the brand needs a deliberate, approved evolution) and leaves room for a specific core idea.

### B. Territory survey
Shallow options come from the model's category associations; widen the field before writing any card. Survey territories across source families (Full: five territories from three families; Focused: three from two): historical and movement lineages in design, illustration, lettering, and print; regional and vernacular traditions of the business's place or community; craft, material, and production traditions; contemporary practice in other categories and regions; the business's own world (inventory and profile).

Per territory record, in one table row: defining traits (depiction mode, technique, line and form behavior, material and surface, light, chromatic logic, typographic stance, compositional behavior), the lineage and conditions it arose from (so it is used with understanding, not as surface), where it is true to this business and where it would be imported whole, behavior at glance and approach in the real setting, print and renderability demands. With search tools look at real precedents first; without, reason from knowledge and mark the survey provisional. Study precedents for principles, never copy a competitor or an existing design; verify any culturally loaded territory or omit it.

### C. Core idea
A core idea relates something true about the business to something the audience recognizes, in a way that suggests a visual structure. A mood word or product category is not one. Cards differ on a structural axis (what carries the identity, what the viewer does first, how information is structured, register, image-making method).

**Card fields:** (1) the idea in one sentence; (2) source (territory, inventory item, departure dimension, weakest link, differentiator, name or origin); (3) the true thing and the audience recognition it relates; (4) the visual structure it suggests; (5) behavior in the setting at each viewing stage; (6) risks (renderability, capacity); (7) compatibility with each quality requirement.

**Five properties** every leader must show: **specific** (this business's), **visual**, **generative** (theme, hero, style, layout can follow), **truthful**, **functional** (helps the piece work in its setting and reads at the glance stage without being decoded). Also check interaction with brand design and theme.

### D. Style
One synthesis resolved across seven dimensions, each in specialist terms: (1) visual language: form and line behavior, abstraction, geometric or organic; (2) influences, named by lineage and the traits borrowed; (3) compositional behavior: axis, alignment, grid logic, figure-ground, plane behavior and layer cues, tension, rhythm; (4) material character: surface, texture, the physical process evoked; (5) image-making technique and rendering logic; (6) typographic expression: classification, stroke contrast, proportion, spacing character; (7) light, atmosphere, chromatic stance: light direction and quality, tonal key, value structure, temperature and harmony logic (Phase 4's palette must realize it).

**Card:** one entry per dimension, plus territories drawn on, the core idea realized, and quality compatibility. Mood adjectives are not a card. For each dimension of the leading cards compare at least two options and repair any conflicting dimension before choosing. Style comes from synthesis of this brief's conditions, not arbitrary additions; familiar combinations need an evaluated justification.

### E. Hero
Options: symbolic marks, objects, products, in-use scenes, places, figures (supplied or wanted), illustrations, photography, abstract forms, typography-led concepts, an equal set of images, or no hero. Choose a hero only when one subject deserves primary attention and a strong, renderable depiction exists; when several messages weigh equally or no suitable depiction exists, choose no hero or a small equal set, never a forced focal image. A figure leads only when supplied or wanted, as a designed character (Phase 4), never a stock figure or a real person's likeness. Depiction mode follows what must be true: where the viewer must see exactly what is sold, a mode that stylizes it away is ineligible. A standalone logo request makes the mark the hero by definition (the protocol still runs for idea and style).

**Card:** (1) what it is, down to the true specifics (form, material, construction, arrangement), with depiction mode and technique; (2) what it truthfully represents; (3) the stage at which it registers; (4) relation to the mark (supports or competes); (5) renderability and reliability (avoid real identifiable people and dependence on complex hands, crowds, or fine faces); (6) space cost against space intent; (7) quality compatibility; (8) hosting capacity: what its form and visible parts can carry (text arcing over a dome, callouts to named parts, an overlapping tag), at what scale and edge behavior (isolated, or running to the trim), and which parts may sit in front of or behind text. The hero's scale, crop, and edge behavior must express the message, not merely fit: a hero too small or contained for its message fails even if attractive.

### F. Governing principles (the stance sheet)
Each is a decision, not a default. **Compared** ones: two or more options, one line of strength and weakness each, against the core idea, style, hero, and quality requirements (compact comparison). Others: decided directly with a one-line basis. The depth table in `SKILL.md` section 6 says which are compared.

**Compared at every depth**
- **Space intent.** The share of the format that stays quiet and its job (separate glance content, rest the eye, signal positioning), derived from format size, viewing distance, content volume, positioning. Larger media raise space intent or the strength of the main idea, not element count. Rough capacity test: mandatory content at legibility minimums must fit the non-quiet share; if not, lower the space intent or plan triage now.
- **Imagery stance and supporter budget.** Which supporter families are open, the share of the format they may take, their shared form language. An open family is a permission, never an obligation; supporters rank below the hero, identity unit, and required information they serve; heavy text raises the need for relief, never licenses clutter. A whole family is excluded only for a stated reason (brief, style, production, culture).
- **Integration stance.** Whether content about the hero is composed with it (integrated or attached) or in separate zones. Default: couple content that labels, explains, or prices the hero. Exception: a hero that carries the message alone, or one that coupling would dilute (competing for its edge, hiding its form, shrinking it); there, separate zones with isolation are stronger. Decide from core idea, hosting capacity, content inventory.
- **Mark role.** Whether the identity unit leads, anchors, or signs off, against the Communication Problem and the hero. It fixes the weight Phases 4 and 5 give it.

**Compared at Full depth (direct at Focused)**
- **Layering stance.** One to three planes, what each carries (hero, information, supporters, base field), how strongly the order is expressed; plane order ranks content, integrates it with the hero, and adds richness. Compare options with a single plane among them; more planes only for a named gain. A flat style keeps one picture plane and gains richness from tonal steps, quiet surface detail, and overlap of flat shapes. Overlap is allowed wherever it passes the function test (`05_design_execution.md` A); layering adds no element beyond space intent; a plane's added prominence counts against the weight ceiling.
- **Tone and positioning.** Tone: personality and register as checkable qualities (formality, warmth, assertiveness), governing typographic stance and the hero's manner; a tone signaling the opposite of a stated quality is eliminated. Positioning: where the offer sits from economical to premium and the cues that signal it together (restraint or abundance, finish, scale, space, price visibility); honest to the offer; one contradicting cue undoes the rest.
- **Density and rhythm.** A stated density level (element count, information grain), bounded by space intent and the hero's need to dominate; content beyond it is triaged now (cut, merge, demote), not shrunk. Higher density demands stricter grouping, never more decoration. Rhythm: the glance stage needs a clear first beat; uniform loudness or quiet is no rhythm.

**Decided directly**
- **Character and connection.** Name the ownable character carriers the plan relies on, each drawn from the core idea, inventory, or survey, and how major elements will be linked. Required at every density: the sparser or calmer the plan, the more each element must carry. Quality compatibility is not enough; the plan must also pass "with the name covered, could this belong to any business of its category at this density?"
- **Boundaries.** What the design must never become, as qualities and treatments, drawn from the apparent options and neighbors' look (the model's included), then brief, production, culture; each is realized later as a decided opposite trait, not a bare exclusion.
- **Point of tension.** At most one deliberate break from the plan's own coherent system (scale, contrast, placement, or concept), carried only by an element allowed to lead, compatible with every quality requirement, lowering reading effort or raising delight at the glance stage. None is the answer when the core idea, hero, or departure already supplies the unexpected, or when style and qualities call for calm.
- **Figure stance** (only when a figure is supplied or wanted). Roles (identify as part of the mark, lead as hero, demonstrate, guide, carry mood), each answering a need no other element answers, one figure for all roles, subordinate to required information except as hero or mark. An unmentioned figure is offered once as a suggestion where a real deficit calls for it and never enters unconfirmed.
- **Quality carriers.** Assign each quality requirement's carriers to plan decisions (color and value key, style, hero, density, tone); a requirement with none is a gap.

**Resolve the Phase 2 tensions** one by one: which side leads and what the other keeps.

### G. Assembled-plan evaluation and stress tests
Check as one system: do brand design, theme, idea, style, hero, and principles reinforce one another; does the plan answer the weakest link in the real setting; do content volume, space intent, medium, budget, and renderability fit together; do accepted costs compound? Return to the responsible decision on a conflict (two cycles per issue). Then run and record:
1. **Swap / category:** with a competitor's name, or the product removed, does it still work unchanged? If so it is generic or stereotyped.
2. **Context-swap:** equally right in another setting? Then it ignores the environment.
3. **Distance and attention:** does the first impression hold at the glance stage in the real setting?
4. **Contradiction scan:** do all plan decisions agree?
5. **Ambition:** does it fit content, budget, and format?
6. **Brief-quality:** does each stated quality have a carrier at the glance stage, and does any decision signal its opposite?

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- Survey meets the depth minimum; no direction derived from a competitor or existing design; no territory used as surface without its lineage understood.
- Every card is filled in specialist terms; no apparent option won without a decisive criterion; the leaders passed the protocol's three tests; every material weakness has a move or an accepted cost.
- Core idea fits one sentence and shows the five properties; style is one synthesis an illustrator or printer could act on; hero registers at its stage, truthfully represents what is sold, and is not forced; a figure exists only if supplied or wanted and has a role.
- Compared principles show their options; space intent states share and job and passed the capacity estimate; imagery stance leaves open the families Phase 4 may need and excludes none by blanket default.
- Character carriers and linking logic are named; six stress tests recorded; every quality requirement has carriers in the plan; every Phase 2 tension resolved.
- Objection raised and resolved (owner of the riskiest decision: senior art director or brand strategist; two objections at Full depth).

**Entry content:** for core idea, style, hero: the apparent option, candidates (name, key strength, key weakness), choice with one-sentence reason including why it beat the runner-up, decisive criterion, what was combined or repaired. One line each for the principles that had real alternatives (space intent, imagery stance, mark role always) and one line on how each stated quality is carried. Mark decisions fixed by the user or evidence as such.
