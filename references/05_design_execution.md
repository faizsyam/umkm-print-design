## 1. Process

1. State the one-sentence message the viewer takes away first.
2. Assign every content item to a viewing stage (B.1) and map its referent and coupling mode (A2).
3. Decide the space plan before placing anything (B.2), reserving the hero group's field as one region.
4. Decide the spatial strategy by the protocol (C).
5. Rank and place content (A), sizing each tier to its legibility minimum plus hierarchy needs, and triaging if content exceeds the space plan (B.4 and B.5); run the capacity check (B.8) so no size changes after the gate.
6. Implement elements (D) and backgrounds and transitions (E).
7. Account for physical print conditions (F) and multi-face structure (G).
8. Trace every placement to the plan and elements (H).
9. Review the complete composition (I). On failure, return to the earliest responsible decision and re-run forward.
10. Pass the gate; guided mode: print the Phase 5 entry and continue to Phase 6 after its wait note, without stopping; auto mode: print nothing and continue.

The order of steps 2 to 5 is fixed: viewing stages, then space plan, then sizes, then placement. The spatial strategy follows the space plan because it must arrange content within space already reserved.

## 2. Detailed guidance

### A. Visual hierarchy

**Determine**
- The order in which the audience should notice, interpret, and use the information.
- How many levels the piece needs (there is no standard count).
- Text and imagery ranked together.

**Assign dominance by several means**
- Scale, contrast, color, isolation, position, weight.
- The identity unit's prominence matches its Phase 3 role; its distinction is secured by mechanisms (Phase 4), not size alone.

### A2. Relationship map and grouping

**Purpose.** Group content by what it refers to, not only by when it is read. Text that labels, explains, qualifies, or prices a depicted thing delivers one message with that thing and is composed with it. Grouping by attention order alone yields a picture with captions.

**Map every text item** from its Ledger referent and Phase 4 content role: what it refers to, whether the hero's form can show or host it (Phase 3 hosting capacity), and its coupling mode.

| Mode | Use when | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated | The item names, describes, quantifies, or dramatizes the hero or a visible part of it, is short, and the hero can host it | Callout with a pointer to the part it names; label on or against the object; text following the hero's contour or an arc over it, or flowing with its motion; a price or offer tag overlapping the object; a headline emerging from the depicted thing |
| Attached | The item concerns the hero as a whole, or the hero cannot host it | Same group: shares the hero's field, axis, or rail; nearer the hero than any other group |
| Separate | Self-standing: the identity unit, action and contact, general claims, anything about the business rather than the depicted thing | Its own zone, by viewing stage |

**Rules**
- **Hero stays dominant.** Integrated and attached text is subordinate to the hero in scale, weight, and contrast. The hero group reads as one object at the glance stage; its details resolve at the approach stage.
- **Space does double duty.** The resting field around the hero is the natural host for the hero group's text. Empty ground beside the hero while its text is squeezed into a separate band is a misallocation; hosting integrated content counts as a job in the fill test.
- **Balance.** Distribute integrated items so their combined weight counterbalances the hero's composition; one-sided stacking only when the asymmetry is the plan.
- **Clarity.** Integrate only as many items as stay readable at the approach stage (typically five labels at most); align labels to one rail or one radial rhythm; one pointer style, short, uncrossed, ending on the part named; text sits on quiet ground, never across detailed parts of the hero.
- **Verbatim.** Integration changes position and form, never wording or order. Split a string across labels only if each fragment is verbatim, in order, and reads correctly alone; otherwise attach it whole. A group title stays with its items.
- **Group count.** Count the perceptual groups at the glance stage. Merging by relationship lowers the count; many equal separate bands make a list.
- **Reliability.** Use only forms the image model renders reliably: one short line on one smooth arc, straight pointers, few labels. If reliability is doubtful, attach instead.

**Tests.** *Referent test:* for every item, name what it refers to and confirm it sits nearest that thing. *Caption test:* if covering the text leaves a picture with captions beneath it, the coupling is missing.

### B. Scale, viewing stages, space, and density

Visibility requirements guide prioritization; they do not entitle every element to be large.

1. **Assign every content item to a viewing stage.**
   - By when the viewer needs it, not by how important the owner feels it is.
   - Stages come from the Environment Profile's viewing stages.
   - Only glance-stage content is sized for the farthest distance.
   - Record each item's stage and tier.
2. **Decide the space plan before placing anything,** as proportions of the format:
   - an **outer margin** that scales with the format (large formats need proportionally wide margins);
   - **isolation zones** around the glance content, hero, and identity unit;
   - **separations** between content groups (space is the first separator, ahead of rules and boxes);
   - **resting fields** positioned to support the eye's path.

   The plan honors the Phase 3 space intent. Place content into what remains; if it does not fit, change the content (triage), the grouping, or the sizes, never the space plan.
3. **Respect the medium's scale.**
   - A larger medium has more area but not a different viewer: it serves a farther viewer (the glance tier grows, the rest stays modest) or lets the same content breathe.
   - It rarely justifies more elements.
   - When content seems small for the area, the answers are a bolder single idea, a larger glance tier, or more space, never more items.
4. **Treat legibility as a minimum.**
   - Set a legibility minimum per tier from stage distance, material, light, and contrast.
   - Size beyond the minimum buys emphasis, which works only when most elements do not have it.
5. **Budget the content.**
   - Count groups and items per tier; competing groups at one stage should match what the viewer can parse in that stage's time.
   - If content exceeds the space plan, apply **triage**: demote items to a later stage, group, simplify, or omit optional content.
   - Never delete mandatory content, shrink below the minimum, inflate every tier, or reduce the space plan.
   - If triage cannot make mandatory content fit, record a volume risk for the notes and keep the content.
6. **Use space as visibility** (isolation raises glance visibility more reliably than size) and **express scale as relationships** (proportions and steps between tiers).
7. **Check.**
   - Name each element's stage and minimum; reduce any element far above its minimum that is neither the hero nor the glance message.
   - Run the **fill test** on each quiet area: an area with a job is kept; an area with none is reassigned in the plan, never filled with an item.
8. **Capacity check (before any size is printed).**
   - For each text string, estimate its width as characters times cap height times a width factor (about 0.75 to 0.9 for text faces, up to about 1.0 for heavy or wide display faces) and compare it with its container at the planned size; count the lines needed against the container height.
   - On failure, change in this order: line breaks and grouping, container size or shape, tier size down to (never below) its legibility minimum, triage.
   - Only then record final sizes. The sizes printed at this gate are the sizes the prompt uses.

### C. Composition and layout (the spatial strategy)

**Candidate card fields (spatial strategy).** Compare at least three structurally different strategies. Structural difference means a different organizing logic, not a different position of the same arrangement.
1. Where the eye enters and how it travels (directional flow, including behavior at the format's aspect ratio).
2. How groups relate: zones, groupings, alignments, grid logic.
3. How the hero, identity unit, and information divide the format; figure-ground relation.
4. Visual weight distribution and tension; density and rhythm.
5. How the space plan is realized (margin, isolation, resting fields).
6. Edge behavior and foreground-background relationship.
7. Behavior at each viewing stage; fit with and departure from neighbors.
8. Where supporters and designed copy sit, and how they relieve, group, and balance the text areas.
9. Coupling: which content is integrated with, attached to, or separate from the hero group (A2), and the resulting group count.

**Evaluate**
- The context matrix, interaction with the plan and elements, and the originality tests (`protocol.md` section 3).
- Judge density, scale, and finish relative to the surroundings.
- No universal grid, fixed number of zones, or stock layout without a compelling reason.
- At least one candidate couples the hero's related content to the hero (integrated or attached); zoning is kept only where A2 marks content separate. Message coherence outweighs tidy zoning.
- Extreme aspect ratios change how the eye travels; decide the path explicitly.

### D. Implementing elements

**For each element specify**
- Role, scale, weight, color, treatment, position, relationship, and the decision it traces to.

**Rules**
- Typography sizes follow viewing stages.
- Prices, calls to action, and contact details are functional text, clear at their own stage, not enlarged by default.
- The identity unit claims its own region with protected clear space measured from the unit.
- Elements interact as one art-directed whole.
- Specify where every mandatory text item sits and in what typographic role. Integrated items name the part they point to or follow, their side or path, and the pointer style; attached items name the group they join.
- Designed copy items are placed with their Phase 4 treatment (line breaks, emphasis, container, color role); they do not revert to plain text during layout. Supporters are placed where they answer their deficit.
- A supplied asset the generator cannot reproduce faithfully (a QR or QRIS code, a barcode, a supplied photograph, a supplied logo file that cannot be attached) gets a reserved asset panel: its own region at the size it needs (a code sized to scan at its use distance, with its quiet zone), inside the space plan, with isolation and a quiet frame in the system's colors. Never an invented stand-in.

### E. Backgrounds and transitions

**Decide**
- Continuity and contrast across zones.
- Transitions between major areas.
- Subordination to hierarchy.
- Edge-to-edge consistency.
- That the background stays quiet where information lives.

### F. Physical print conditions

**Account for**
- Viewing distance per stage, ambient light, material and finish.
- Production limits, handling and exposure.
- Format behavior (folds, panels, curvature).
- Carry forward only what changes the look or the generation; separate visual direction from technical production specifications.

### G. Multi-face and multi-panel pieces

**Decide**
- The shared identity, established once.
- A distinct job per face.
- Consistent treatment and continuity between panels.
- For each face: stage assignment, space plan, and spatial strategy, because faces differ in job and possibly viewing conditions.
- One standalone prompt per face.

### H. Plan realization and traceability

**Trace** (`protocol.md` 3f)
- Every placement, size relationship, and treatment names the plan commitment or element decision it realizes.
- Every plan commitment (core idea, style dimensions, hero, governing principles, space intent, mark role) is visibly realized in the composition.
- Gaps are closed in the responsible phase, not by adding items.

### I. Review of the complete composition

**Review from each team perspective** and assess:
- message at first glance;
- hierarchy;
- every stated quality requirement, read off the composed whole at the glance stage (value key included);
- the identity unit: integrated, distinctive, and passing the text-neighborhood test;
- concept consistency and the plan-realization trace;
- the contribution of every element;
- legibility;
- balance and completeness;
- background and detail;
- specificity to this business and context;
- proportion and space;
- fit among actual neighbors and light, with departure on the intended dimension;
- relationships: each text item nearest its referent; supporting text never competes with the hero; no picture-with-captions bands;
- visual variety: more than type and one motif carries the eye across the piece, with supporters where they relieve, group, or balance;
- absence of generic, templated, overdecorated, or AI-typical appearance.

On failure, name the earliest responsible decision (plan, element, or placement), return there, and re-run forward; cap at two cycles per issue.

## 3. Failure modes

| Failure | Detection | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract hierarchy | Importance assigned by owner preference, not by stage | Reassign by when the viewer needs each item |
| Size inflation | Many elements far above their minimums | Reduce non-glance elements toward minimums |
| Filling space | A quiet area with no job | Run the fill test; reassign the area in the plan |
| More area, more items | Content added because the format is large | Strengthen the main idea, grow the glance tier, or add space |
| Space plan shrunk to fit | Margins or isolation reduced to hold content | Restore the plan; triage content |
| Mandatory content deleted | A mandatory string missing from the specification | Restore it; regroup or rescale instead |
| Identity unit swallowed or split | Fails the text-neighborhood or unit test in composition; mark and wordmark separated across the layout | Give the unit one region; add mechanisms; change neighboring type |
| One-axis hierarchy | Dominance by size alone | Add contrast, isolation, position, weight, color |
| Strategies that are one layout | Three "strategies" with the same organizing logic | Rewrite on structurally different logics |
| Untraced placement | A placement names no plan or element source | Remove it, or fix the upstream gap |
| Fit without departure, or the reverse | Review shows blending into neighbors, or alienating them | Re-balance on the chosen dimension |
| Stock layout | The composition would work for any business | Re-derive from the core idea and the Environment Profile |
| Dense text ignored | Dense content with no grouping or row behavior | Group in reading order; describe rows once |
| Sizes changed after printing | Text does not fit its container, found late | Run the capacity check before the gate; amend only genuine returns |
| Treatment lost in layout | A designed copy item or supporter appears as plain text or is missing | Restore the Phase 4 treatment and placement |
| Qualities lost in composition | The composed whole signals the opposite of a stated quality | Return to the earliest responsible decision and re-run forward |
| Picture with captions | Text about the depicted thing sits in a separate band or zone | Apply A2: integrate or attach it |
| Hero hosts nothing | Empty ground beside the hero while its text is squeezed elsewhere | Move the hero group's text into the hero's field |
| Overintegration | Many labels, crossing pointers, or text over detailed parts | Keep the few that matter; attach the rest |
| List of bands | Many equal separate groups stacked | Merge by relationship; lower the group count |

## 4. Completion criteria, summary print, and hand-off

Complete when the phase card's "Before leaving" check holds.

**Print for the decision summary (Design Execution entry, at the gate):**
- the stage assignment in brief (what sits at glance, approach, close inspection) and the space plan with proportions, stated as decided by rule, not by candidate comparison; the capacity check passed; which content is integrated with, attached to, or separate from the hero;
- the spatial strategy: candidates compared (one line each: name, strength, weakness), selection, decisive criterion, runner-up.

Update the Design Log: viewing stages, space plan, open issues.

**Hand-off to Phase 6:** the Execution Specification: the message; hierarchy and reading order; stage assignment; space plan with proportions; spatial strategy; placement, scale, weight, color, and treatment of every element and text item (including mandatory text); background and transition behavior; print-condition adjustments; the review result.
