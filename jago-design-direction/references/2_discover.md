# Phase 2: discover

**Needs:** S. **Produces:** the communication problem (objective, message, weakest link, information groups with reading tiers, tensions), the context profile, a feasibility verdict, the **design truth**, the **code map** with its baseline, the **opportunity pool**, and tagged implications. S is not restated. **Guided:** no stop; findings lead the direction entry.
**Order:** research triage → business (A) → context and viewing (B) → problem (C) → feasibility (D) → design truth (E) → code map (F) → opportunity pool (G) → implications → gate. A finding that changes no decision is dropped.

## A. The business as it is
Record values, personality, and positioning shown by what it does and says; credibility actually held; the owner's aspirations; the audience relationship; price tier and scale; how much departure its standing can carry (an established, trusted name or a new one). Separate fact from interpretation and aspiration from reality; downgrade readings richer than their evidence. Record traits to express and claims never to make. Never build a fictional identity from a small fact.

## B. Context and viewing
One profile per distinct context; images sharing a context share it. Start from the Phase 1 inference and test it. Derive from how the image is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' color, density, scale, order, finish; competing stimuli), the audience's state (goal, motion, attention, time, distance, light, culture), the reading path.
- **Reading tiers** (derived, not assumed to be three): **glance** (what a passing viewer must take in at the farthest distance), **approach**, **close**. Assign by when the viewer needs an item, never by whether it fits. Glance holds the hero and at most two short items in the order the message needs them; the name takes a slot only where recognition drives the action (a known brand, a brand-led purpose), otherwise the offer or occasion comes first; one line records the choice. The reading size suited to a distance is about distance ÷ 150 for sustained lines and ÷ 115 for a passing glance (cap height, tagged assumption), used to judge feasibility only. **Core content** (what the action depends on: offers, prices, choices) keeps comfortable reading at its tier; only **reference content** (contacts, hours, fine print) may read deliberately.
- **Medium reading:** what the medium imposes, what the audience relies on, what it affords; record only what changes a decision.
- **Impression vs expression:** hold the impression (what the viewer should feel and conclude) constant; let the setting decide the expression.
- **Audience encounter:** references, motivations, trusted register, trust cues present or lacking, what they must conclude per tier. Drop stereotypes and unsupported psychology.
- If the context cannot be established, state the most probable one and mark it provisional.

## C. The communication problem
- **Objective:** a user-stated priority fixes the primary objective; the weakest link is chosen within it and never replaces it. Record the desired response, the necessary information, the desired perception (qualities included).
- **Information groups:** by the job each does toward the objective (carry the proposition, support what is seen first, enable the offer and action, substantiate trust, help evaluation, give practical information), never by similar looks or the user's list order. Per group: items, tier, what it refers to, internal hierarchy, volume. Items that label, price, or explain a depicted thing belong with it.
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility, register); state both needs and the resource. Where the conflict priority (`SKILL.md`) or the user's priority decides, record the lead side; Phase 3 decides the rest.
- **Weakest link:** attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs: the one most likely to fail here. Test the brief's own assumptions (images asked, audience assumed, success measure); one that undermines the objective becomes a tension.
- **Message:** one sentence: what the glance viewer takes away and does next. It serves the objective and contradicts no quality.

## D. Feasibility (one pass, only for content-heavy images)
Can this content be read at this distance in this format? Estimate once, for the most compact sensible arrangement (a hero beside its own text and equal units in columns share one band; extent = the larger): each group's need = planned lines × pitch (≈ 1.6 × cap for display lines, ≈ 2 × cap for body text) at its tier's reading size, as a share of the long side, plus ≈ 15% for gaps; add the hero's minimum extent and an overhead of 24% (structure and decoration 5, quiet 8, margins and separations 8, headroom 3). Over 100% is **dense**.
Dense is answered by structure, never by deleting mandatory content: (1) group and tier the content so core items lead and reference items recede; (2) simplify the hero before shrinking core content; (3) tell the user once the way out (larger format, less text, a second image); (4) carry the tiers and a density note into the brief (`5_compose_brief.md`). The verdict never becomes numbers in the brief.

## E. The design truth
The design truth is the one specific, true thing from which a distinctive look can be built, and the tension that makes it interesting.
1. **Differentiators:** list candidates from specifics, process, materials, origin, behavior, offer logic, location, people, name, ritual, and what each peer already claims. Test each: **true** (failure discards), **relevant** at the encounter, **distinctive** (not claimable by a named peer), **visualizable** (a failure may still shape tone or hierarchy).
2. **Tension:** find the productive contrast inside the business: small and ambitious, traditional and current, craft and accessible price, serious and playful, local and aspirational. Look also in the audience: what they feel or need at the moment of encounter that this business serves uniquely. A design built on a tension has somewhere to go; one built on a single adjective does not.
3. **State it** as "[specific truth], [tension]" in one sentence. It must fail the **swap test**: substituting a named peer makes it false. If nothing passes, say so: the piece then differs through idea and execution alone.

## F. The code map and the baseline
Every category and setting carries codes the audience reads without thinking. List them across: color, imagery and subject matter, lettering, layout and hierarchy, copy tone, finish and material, ornament. For each code decide:
- **Keep:** it carries credibility, function, or recognition the audience relies on (price legibility, trust cues, the conventions of an occasion or culture).
- **Bend:** keep its function, change its expression.
- **Break:** it is the apparent choice and breaking it gains attention or identity without costing credibility or function.
Together the kept and unbroken codes produce the **baseline**: the result a competent, generic treatment of this brief would give. Write it before any card exists, as the best generic execution and never a strawman, in one short description of what it looks like. Every later decision is judged against it. Break only where the design truth gives the break a reason; a break with no reason is noise, and a map with no breaks is the baseline.
Occasion, season, and culture named in the brief are fit dimensions: depart from their apparent signifiers by specificity and craft, never by omitting them.

## G. The opportunity pool
Seeds for directions come from the business and its world, never from the category's stock imagery. Work through the lenses and keep what each yields:
| Lens | Asks |
|---|---|
| Name and language | wordplay, sound, origin, local expressions, lettering traditions |
| Product truth | how it is made or behaves, the material, the moment of use |
| Ritual and occasion | the habit around it, the occasion's own signature |
| Place and origin | local architecture, signage, packaging, craft traditions, geography and season |
| Insider code | what this audience instantly recognizes as theirs: objects, formats, slang, artifacts |
| Content structure | the information's own form (a sequence, a range, a price ladder, a list) as a visual device |
| Constraint as asset | format, viewing distance, text volume, mandatory elements turned into the idea |
| Inversion | invert a code that can be afforded, shift scale or viewpoint, pair two true things unexpectedly |
| Adjacent lineage | a visual tradition from another domain whose logic fits (printing, signage, craft, sport, packaging) |
Screen each seed: **true**, **respectful** (no tokenism; omit a loaded symbol whose meaning is unverified), **workable** (it can be seen and depicted truthfully), **ownable** (a peer could not use it unchanged). Keep 4 to 8 seeds, at least one beyond the baseline, none taken from a competitor. A seed the category's obvious symbols would suggest (the stock object of the trade) is baseline, not a seed: look one level deeper for the specific truth behind it.
Where the user fixed the look, mine only what the fixed look leaves open.

## H. Research
A question, not a phase: run only for a question that names a direction decision it could change and that the brief and knowledge cannot answer. At most three targeted queries and one page read per question; no trend surveys; none needed means record "no research needed". Prefer first-hand sources; competitor visuals are a baseline to differ from, never a model. Without tools, reason from the brief and mark conclusions provisional.

## Implications and gate
Write each finding as "because [finding], the design must / should / must avoid [consequence]", tagged given, researched, or inferred.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every finding changes or confirms a named decision · the design truth is specific, true, and passes the swap test (or is recorded as absent) · the code map shows each code's decision and the baseline in one description · the profile names where the piece departs and where it fits · the objective follows the user's priority · the message is one sentence serving the objective · groups follow job, tier, and referent; glance slots are justified · the pool holds at least one seed beyond the baseline, each screened · a dense verdict, if any, has its way out. Then append the record.
