# Phase 6: compose the brief

**Needs:** S, the interpretation, the brand record, the theme record, the direction record. **Produces:** one English design brief per image, then the final reply (`7_delivery_and_revision.md`). Composing is translation: the brief is the reviewed picture, narrated for a maker who has never seen the analysis. If it needs a new idea, return to the layer that owns it (a new identity: Phase 3; a new concept: Phase 4).
The **maker** produces the image from the brief alone, with no access to the analysis, the alternatives, or the reasoning. A maker given a described picture, a defined look, and exact text does its best work; a maker given a list of parts and prohibitions fills the gaps with defaults.

## 1. Sort every decision by owner
Walk S and the direction record once. Each decision lands in one class and is stated once.
| Class | What belongs | In the brief |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** (user) | facts, exact text, assets with fidelity (a supplied logo or identity included), named preferences, palette, format, exclusions, what must be preserved | **Text**, **Mark** (supplied) and **Requirements**: precise, "must", exact quotes |
| **Decided** (skill) | the theme's picture and its composition architecture in zones and relations, how each group is presented, art direction at ceiling, hero design and situation, a new mark's narration and construction, the brand language as applied, ownable traits with scope, color roles, lettering construction, supporting graphics, a few guards | **Picture**, **Mark** (new) and **Look**: firm statements of what the image is and does |
| **Open** (maker) | exact placement, sizes, spacing, type pairing, position and count of small decorative detail | omitted; never listed, never handed back |
Every Decided item passes the **necessity test** (`SKILL.md`) or is omitted. State the least that achieves it: the intent and the traits that make it recognizable. A skill decision is never worded as the user's requirement.
A matter settled by a flagged default is stated firmly in the brief and flagged in the reply's notes; the brief never carries an unresolved item.

## 2. Anatomy
Outcome first, the picture second, the mark third, text fourth, look fifth. Plain English headings; include only what applies.
```
Request       Create a [kind of image] for [business] to [purpose]. [Orientation, ratio]; [where and how it is seen, from what distance];
              [for whom: the specific viewer in their moment, and what they should feel and do].
              The response it must trigger, and the user's qualities at their own intensity (two sentences at most).
The picture   The finished image narrated whole, in plain visual prose: the first read; how the hero and the headline lockup belong together;
              the zones in which the groups live, what each holds, and how its presentation carries the theme; depth and layers; where quiet
              stays open; what the ground does.
Mark          Only when the image carries the business's identifier. Supplied: the asset reproduced exactly, with its standing and clear field.
              New: the mark described as a designer would (kind, silhouette, construction, relation to the name, the one ownable feature),
              always a distinct object standing apart from every other element.
Text          Every mandatory line whole, in quotes, once, grouped by cluster, each cluster with its role, its relation to the picture,
              and its derived presentation (lettering form, grouping device, rendering). The rule: "Render exactly the quoted strings, each once. Add, translate, or correct nothing,
              and write no other text."
Look          Art direction at ceiling (lineage, construction, line, rendering and light, finish level) · hero design and situation ·
              the brand language as applied (shape logic, color roles and value key, lettering construction) · ownable traits with scope ·
              supporting graphics and their logic.
Requirements  Assets and fidelity; fixed palette; stated hero traits; the user's exclusions; what to preserve.
Guards        At most three, as positive opposites where possible.
Close         "Decide every unstated detail with craft judgment, at the same finish level. Ask no questions; invent no facts, text, or decoration without a purpose."
```
- The picture and the look lead, so the maker can restate the idea in one sentence and render the whole consistently.
- **Core** (Request to Requirements) is always present. **Precision** (reference map, fidelity, tiers, density note) appears only when the task needs control.
- A simple, complete request needs only a few sentences plus the picture's essentials.

## 3. Writing rules
1. **Voice:** English; the user's request to the maker, in natural prose with short lists only for the text block; no process commentary; a reason only as one clause where a reading could go wrong.
2. **Decisive:** state decisions as facts about the image. No "prefer", "ideally", "try", "could", "may", "consider", "if possible", "adapt if better". A non-decision is omitted. Conditionals only for real branches.
3. **Seen, not argued:** describe what is visible: shapes, light, color, depth, scale relations, gesture, edges. Convert every concept, the theme included, into its visual consequence on each carrier; the theme's name and the word "theme" are never the instruction. Never ask the image to *be* an object or a material; do describe how depicted things look, including their finish and rendering.
4. **Negation economy:** describe what is there. Exclusions are the user's own, the text rule, and at most three guards for named likely failures. A guard that names an unwanted object invites it: state the wanted state instead ("open sky blue ground", not a list of banned decorations). No reflexive "no X" for what the maker would not add unprompted, and no list of reductions ("no gradients, no shadows, no depth") unless the user asked for that look.
5. **Spatial intent in plain words:** zones and relations (top, middle, lower third, beside, overlapping, tucked behind, nearest the viewer), scale relations (clearly larger than, comparable to), and what stays open. No coordinates, percentages, millimeters, or pixels. State what the viewer needs and leave exact placement, sizing, and spacing to the maker.
6. **Verbatim block:** every mandatory line whole, punctuation included, each once. Line breaks are the maker's; the skill splits a string only where the user did. A lettering treatment may emphasize part of a string (a price inside its line) without splitting or repeating it.
7. **Density note** (dense verdict only): "Text-heavy. Group it into a few clear clusters, keep every line, keep [core items] comfortably readable from [distance], let [reference items] be smaller, never distort letters to fit." No sizes. Exact text sits in few, calm, well-separated zones.
8. **References and assets:** a reference map (purpose, what to take, which wins on conflict) and each asset's fidelity; a supplied logo or character is reproduced, never redesigned.
9. **Specificity:** depicted things by their true specifics, not category names; art direction by lineage and construction traits, never by medium words or mood adjectives alone; color by named hue with role and intensity.
10. **Once only:** one statement per decision, no restating the user's text outside the verbatim block, no stacked adjectives. A quality appears once, at the user's intensity, through the picture that carries it.
11. **Length follows the picture:** typically 350 to 550 words, hard cap 650 (750 when a new mark is narrated), **counted, not estimated**, excluding the quoted strings. Over the cap, cut in this order: Decided items failing the necessity test, secondary context, repeated statements; never Fixed, never the picture, the mark narration, the theme's carriers, or the brand constants and signatures in use.
12. **Several images:** one standalone brief each; the shared direction, a new mark's narration included, is repeated word for word; each names its own format and purpose and never refers to another brief.
13. **Editing a supplied image:** state what changes, what must stay identical, where, and how the change integrates.

## 4. Review before delivery (once)
1. **Prior test:** write in two sentences what the maker would produce from the user's words alone. Tag every sentence of the brief **Raise**, **Guard**, or **Fixed**; an untagged sentence is cut. If Guard sentences outnumber Raise sentences, the brief restricts more than it enriches: rewrite toward the picture. A brief that is the prior with more adjectives returns to the layer that produced it (Phase 3 or 4).
2. **Render forecast:** read the brief as the maker and state the image it would literally produce in the best plausible, the likely, and the worst plausible reading. The likely image must beat the prior. Rewrite any phrase that yields unintended texture, an object or mockup look, a clash with another phrase or quality, or the appearance of something a guard named.
3. **Meaning and intensity:** every ledger requirement is present at its priority; reread the user's original words on look, mood, and hero, and confirm each descriptive attribute survived compression in substance and has two carriers on the dominant masses; nothing Decided reads as the user's requirement.
4. **Hierarchy, downward:** the picture in the brief shows the theme through at least three carriers, information presentation included; the mark stands apart and whole; the brand reads with the mark covered; every Decided sentence traces to a parent in the theme, the brand, or a mandate, and one without a parent is cut.
5. **Coverage, upward:** check the foundations against the brief item by item, as visible statements, not as strategy. The message is what the first read delivers; the perception shift and atmosphere are carried by named devices on the dominant masses; the design truth and the stance appear as the idea and the ownable traits; each form consequence in the brand's trait-to-form map appears in the mark's narration or the language as applied; each brand constant and each signature in use appears within its scope; each theme carrier appears; each mandate (B, T, P) is visible or consciously declined with a reason on record. An item missing or reduced to an adjective is rewritten as its visual consequence, never added as an abstract word.
6. **Specificity:** remove the business name and the quoted text: does the brief still point to this business, this audience, and this purpose? A sentence that would serve any other business with the same purpose is struck and rewritten from the true specifics, or cut.
7. **Picture review** (`4_theme.md` §5) on the narrated picture inside the brief.
8. **Verbatim:** every string matches the ledger's whole line, punctuation included, appears once, and no extra text is invited.
9. **Truth:** no invented fact, claim, contact, date, or mark; no placeholder.
10. **Form:** English; no hedges; no list of open decisions; no numeric layout; standalone; the counted length within the rule (record the count); tiers and density note consistent with the verdict.
A failure returns to the earliest responsible decision (a missing requirement to Phase 1, a wrong reading to Phase 2, a weak or drifting identity to Phase 3, a weak concept to Phase 4, a weak element or composition to Phase 5).

## 5. Level of prescription
The default is Fixed and Decided as sorted above. After a result is reviewed (`7_delivery_and_revision.md`), move one notch at a time and only on evidence: **tighten** a trait that was lost (state it more explicitly), or **relax** items that stiffened the result (drop Decided items that did not earn their place, least consequential first). Record the setting.
