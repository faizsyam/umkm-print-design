# Choosing among options (shared by Phases 3 and 4)

**Used for:** the brand design (`3_brand.md`) and the theme (`4_theme.md`). Each supplies its **foundations**, candidate fields, review, what relatability and distinctiveness are measured against, added ranking criterion, and card. This file holds the method, stated once.

## 1. Openness and number
- **Fixed** (the user or an asset decides): record it, show no cards, still derive what the user left unsaid.
- **Steered or Open:** at least **3 candidates on different foundations** (§3). Under Steered, one is the **spine candidate** (the user's stated direction at its best craft, nothing added, in the register the perception contract sets unless the user stated it) and the others explore the open remainder. A narrow brief narrows the axes, never the count: fewer than three genuinely distinct candidates means the exploration is unfinished.
Volume and crowding never raise openness; they raise feasibility work. Never reduced: the prior read, the runner-up, the decisive criterion, and the judging in §4. Auto mode develops and judges the same candidates silently.

## 2. What qualifies as an idea
An idea is judged by how the finished work looks, never by how clever its premise is: it is what a viewer would tell a friend, not a rationale. It qualifies only if:
- the viewer need not learn its rationale for it to work;
- the maker renders it dependably (exact paths, exact counts, precise alignment, fine repeated detail, and small lettering inside a mark are rebuilt around what can be shown);
- it deepens the relation between the parts instead of adding a layer on top of them;
- it is a graphic or situational structure, never a material or an object-as-surface.
Under a fixed spine the idea may be modest: the spine at full craft plus one integrated moment. A premise that competes with the first read or the user's look is eliminated.

## 3. Explore, then build
Candidates come from reasoning over the interpretation, never from the first idea plus variations around it.
1. **Name the obvious.** Write the first-thought answer for this layer and the category's habitual answer (the prior read and, for the brand, the default territory; for the theme, the category's habitual treatment of this purpose and setting). They are the control: no candidate is built on them, except a spine candidate where the user fixed that direction.
2. **Find the foundations.** A **foundation** is the lead idea a candidate is built on: which part of the interpretation it leads with, what it claims, and what perception it establishes (from → to). The layer file says where foundations come from. List the credible ones, name the assumption each rests on and how it could be wrong, and keep the three or more most relevant that differ most. A seed from the interpretation becomes part of a foundation only when this layer writes its **yield**: what would actually be seen (a form, a situation, a relation between elements, a way of making, a color behavior, a lettering construction); a seed that can yield only a metaphor, a simulated material, or an object-as-surface is dropped or rebuilt as a graphic or situational form.
3. **Take the second thought.** For each kept foundation, write the form that comes to mind first, then at least two other ways of carrying the same truth by a different mechanism (a different form, situation, behavior, or relation). Take the one least like the obvious that is still true, relevant, and dependable: a less apparent mechanism that fits the business beats a familiar one.
4. **Divergence test (every pair).** State each idea in one sentence without style words. Candidates differ in foundation, not only in motif, color, ornament, or rendering; if one sentence implies another, or swapping their visual treatments would leave the same concept, they are variants of one candidate: rebuild one from another foundation.
5. **Develop each as a dossier** (a label is not a candidate), in plain, specific words:
   - **Idea:** one sentence naming the true thing it rests on and what it claims or does.
   - **Perception:** the interpretation items it answers, named, and the shift it establishes in the customer.
   - **Direction:** what will be seen, whole, in the layer file's fields; every adjective paired with the form that carries it.
   - **Recognizable traits:** two or three specific features that make it identifiable and distinguishable.
   - **Why this business**; **against the prior read** (raises, fixes, keeps); its **signal** (what its visible finish says on each binding dimension of the perception contract); **risk and move**.
   **Sufficiency test:** could a designer who has seen no other candidate start from the dossier with no major decision left open? **Specificity test:** every sentence would be false or meaningless for another business; adjectives that could describe any brand, and abstractions with no carrier, are cut or given their form.
6. **Engineer before judging:** run the layer's review on each and repair: plain against the bar or the user's intensity → add richness through organization; noisy → simplify or regroup; a part needing what the maker cannot deliver → rebuild around what can be shown.

## 4. Evaluate and choose
Two dimensions decide, each scored on evidence and never as a bare number.
1. **Eliminate** any candidate that contradicts a quality, a mandatory item, the conflict priority, the spine, or a constant of the layer above; that holds a false or disrespectful claim, symbol, or depiction; or whose **signal** runs against the perception contract, however attractive. The layer's **gates** eliminate regardless of conceptual strength.
2. **Relatability (R):** how accurately and strongly the candidate represents the interpreted business. The layer file names what it is measured against. Score 0–3 with a clause naming the items it carries and those it misses: **3** carries them all through specific features an informed viewer could trace back to the business; **2** carries the essential ones with minor gaps; **1** fits the category more than this business; **0** contradicts or misreads it.
3. **Distinctiveness (D):** how original, recognizable, and strategically differentiated it is. Answer four tests, each with a reason: **Category** (would the prior read or the category's habitual answer produce it?), **Transfer** (applied unchanged to dozens of unrelated businesses, would it still work?), **Peer** (is it told apart at a glance from the default territory?), **Source** (is its difference drawn from a true specific of this business, or is it only novelty?). Score **3** passes all four; **2** fails one softly; **1** fails two, or has a difference with no source; **0** is the prior. Novelty without a source scores at most 1. Attributes the user fixed are never counted against a candidate: the tests apply to what the user left open.
4. **Decision rules.** A candidate needs **2 or more on both**: safe but generic (high R, low D) and novel but misfit (high D, low R) are both out. Rank survivors by the **lower of the two scores**, then by their sum, then by Appeal (would this audience stop, smile, want it?), Memorable (recallable in one sentence), Coherent (every part follows from the idea and the layer above), and the layer's added criterion. The leader must beat the prior read on a named, visible dimension. If none qualifies, rebuild once from another foundation, then take the spine candidate, or the candidate with the best minimum after repair.
5. **Challenge** each survivor, answering in a clause: Is it appropriate for this particular business? Is the concept too obvious or predictable? Could the same direction serve dozens of unrelated businesses? Does it communicate the intended positioning accurately? Is its distinctiveness meaningful or visual novelty? Then **stretch**: if the leader scores below 3 on either dimension, build one more candidate, from the least-used foundation or by a less conventional mechanism for the leader's own, that holds R and raises D, and score it with the rest, once.
6. **Choose.** Name the runner-up (a candidate you would ship, never a weak foil) and the strongest case against the leader. The margin is one **decisive criterion**, an observable property of the finished work, not of the concept or of a business fact; if none can be stated, take the simpler and record the tie. **Engineer:** each material weakness gets a named move or an accepted cost; each key strength a place where it is amplified. Select, transplant a strength, repair, or combine as a **hybrid** (statable as one idea); a compromise needing "and also" is rejected.
7. **Record:** each candidate's idea with its R and D and their evidence clauses, the choice, runner-up, decisive criterion, what was kept from the others, obligations, the mandates addressed to the layer **answered by id** (each by a named decision, or declined with a reason), and the layer's **handoff**: what the next layer must keep (constants), may vary (flex), and must achieve.

## 5. Cards (guided mode)
Show the three best candidates, the chosen one first, in the user's language, skimmable in seconds, with no internal terms for a new user and no scores: each row gives what will be seen, the perception it builds, and its trade-off. Matters already fixed by the user go in one line "Already settled: …". The layer file gives the template and its step number.
Feedback: acknowledge in one line, rerun what it touches, show only changed rows as Before | After, ask once; after two rounds without agreement choose the best resolution, say so, continue. A question is answered in one or two plain sentences, then the confirmation line repeats. Feedback contradicting a stated quality or fact: say so kindly and offer the closest option keeping both. Feedback that changes the layer above (the business, the audience, the message): re-enter Phase 2 for what it touches; every layer below goes stale. In auto mode no cards appear.

## Gate (every layer; the layer file adds its own)
No decision belongs to another layer (boundary test, `SKILL.md`) · at least three candidates passed the divergence test and were developed as dossiers · each has its R and D with evidence, and the leader scores 2 or more on both and beat the prior read on a named visible dimension (or the fallback taken is recorded) · the challenge and, where due, the stretch were done · the leader signals the perception contract · every open decision shows its runner-up and decisive criterion · every weakness has a move or an accepted cost · every mandate addressed to the layer is answered or declined with a reason · the idea fits one sentence · the handoff is recorded.
