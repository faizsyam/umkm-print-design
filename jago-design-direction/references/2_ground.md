# Phase 2: ground the work

**Needs:** S. **Produces:** the communication problem with reading tiers, a feasibility verdict, the **appeal bar**, the **raw material** and **design truth**, the **prior read**, and tagged implications. S is not restated. **Guided:** no stop; findings lead the direction entry.
**Order:** business (A) → encounter and tiers (B) → problem (C) → feasibility (D) → appeal bar (E) → raw material and truth (F) → prior read (G) → implications → gate. A finding that changes no decision is dropped.

## A. The business as it is
Record values, personality, and positioning shown by what it does and says; credibility actually held; the owner's aspirations; the audience relationship; price tier and scale; how much departure its standing can carry. Separate fact from interpretation and aspiration from reality; downgrade readings richer than their evidence. Record traits to express and claims never to make. Never build a fictional identity from a small fact.

## B. Encounter and reading tiers
One profile per distinct context; images sharing a context share it. Start from the Phase 1 inference and test it. Derive from how the image is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' color, density, scale; competing stimuli), the audience's state (goal, motion, attention, time, distance, light, culture), the reading path. Hold the **impression** (what the viewer should feel and conclude) constant; let the setting decide the expression.
- **Reading tiers** (derived, not assumed to be three): **glance** (what a passing viewer takes in at the farthest distance), **approach**, **close**. Assign by when the viewer needs an item, never by whether it fits. Glance holds the hero and at most two short items in the order the message needs them; the name takes a slot only where recognition drives the action, otherwise the offer or occasion comes first. Reading size suited to a distance is about distance ÷ 150 for sustained lines and ÷ 115 for a passing glance (cap height, tagged assumption), used to judge feasibility only. **Core content** (what the action depends on: offers, prices, choices) keeps comfortable reading at its tier; only **reference content** (contacts, hours, fine print) may read deliberately.
- If the context cannot be established, state the most probable one and mark it provisional.

## C. The communication problem
- **Objective:** a user-stated priority fixes the primary objective; the weakest link is chosen within it and never replaces it. Record the desired response, the necessary information, the desired perception (qualities included).
- **Information groups:** by the job each does toward the objective (carry the proposition, support what is seen first, enable the offer and action, substantiate trust, give practical information), never by similar looks or the user's list order. Per group: items, tier, what it refers to, internal hierarchy, volume. Items that label, price, or explain a depicted thing belong with it.
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility, register). State both needs and the resource. **A tension between a quality and the load is resolved by structure (grouping, tiers, scale steps, a density note), never by lowering the quality.** Record the lead side only where the conflict priority (`SKILL.md`) decides, and what the other side keeps.
- **Weakest link:** attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs: the one most likely to fail here. Test the brief's own assumptions; one that undermines the objective becomes a tension.
- **Message:** one sentence: what the glance viewer takes away and does next. It serves the objective and contradicts no quality.

## D. Feasibility (one pass, only for content-heavy images)
Can this content be read at this distance in this format? Estimate once, for the most compact sensible arrangement (a hero beside its own text and equal units in columns share one band; extent = the larger): each group's need = planned lines × pitch (≈ 1.6 × cap for display lines, ≈ 2 × cap for body text) at its tier's reading size, as a share of the long side, plus ≈ 15% for gaps; add the hero's minimum extent and an overhead of 24% (structure and decoration 5, quiet 8, margins and separations 8, headroom 3). Over 100% is **dense**.
Dense is answered by structure, never by deleting mandatory content: (1) group and tier so core items lead and reference items recede; (2) simplify the hero's footprint before shrinking core content; (3) tell the user once the way out (larger format, less text, a second image); (4) carry the tiers and a density note into the brief. The verdict never becomes numbers in the brief. Dense content is also the tool's weakest ground: the picture later keeps exact text in few, calm, well-separated zones.

## E. The appeal bar
The output is judged by the audience's eye, not by the brief's logic. Establish what this audience finds beautiful and trustworthy **now**, and what finished professional work in their visual world looks like at this price tier: its depth of rendering, finish, color energy, density, lettering sophistication, composition habits.
- **Sources:** the user's references and stated tastes; your knowledge; and, when a search tool is available and the piece is image-led, one or two targeted looks at current strong work in that world (at most three queries; extract the craft level and its logic, never a look to copy; a competitor is a measure, never a model). Without tools, reason and mark provisional.
- **Record the bar:** the finish level a viewer would call professional here, as visible traits (how forms are modelled, how light behaves, how lettering is built, how the whole is composed). It is a floor for the finished image and is never set below what the user's own words imply.
- **Record what the audience's codes are for:** the signifiers that tell them "this is for me" (occasion, age, culture, category). Each is marked **meet** (carry its function through specific, well-made execution) or **depart** (only for a named gain in appeal or clarity). The apparent choice is not automatically a cliché: a signifier the audience relies on is met and made distinct through execution.

## F. Raw material and the design truth
Seeds come from the business and its world, never from the category's stock imagery.
1. **Raw material:** list the drawable specifics: what it sells and how it behaves, its character or name, materials and process, place and culture, the occasion's own habits, the audience's insider objects, what peers already claim. Test each: **true** (failure discards), **relevant** at the encounter, **depictable** (the maker can render it truthfully and attractively), **ownable** (a named peer could not use it unchanged). Write its **visual yield** in one clause: what would actually be seen (a form, a situation, a relation between elements, a color behavior, a lettering construction). A seed whose yield is only a metaphor, or a simulated material or object-as-surface, is dropped or rebuilt as a graphic or situational form. A seed from content below the glance tier serves as a supporting device, never the governing idea. Where the user fixed the look, mine only what it leaves open.
2. **Design truth:** one sentence, "[specific true thing], [productive tension]", where the tension is a contrast inside the business or the audience's moment (small and ambitious, craft and accessible price, serious and playful). It must fail the **swap test**: substituting a named peer makes it false. If nothing passes, say so: the piece then differs through the picture and its execution alone.

## G. The prior read
The maker has a strong prior. Write what it would most likely make from the user's own words alone: look, composition, density, lettering, and how it would handle each stated quality. Be honest about its strengths; it is usually polished, appealing, and well colored, and the brief must never trade those away. Then three lists:
- **Keep:** what the prior does well for this audience. It is carried by the picture, not restated.
- **Raise:** where specificity lifts the result above the prior: the hero's design and situation, the interplay of hero and content, composition logic, lettering as designed objects, depth and richness at the user's intensity, the world around the hero.
- **Fix:** the failures this brief's own features make likely (volume of exact text, digits, many small depicted items with exact counts, precise geometry or alignment, character details drifting, a hierarchy that buries the offer) and the generic defaults the prior falls to where specifics are missing. Each gets a positive remedy in the picture, or a flagged limit when the tool cannot deliver it.
Every later decision names the list it serves; a decision on none is noise. The prior is the bar to beat, not a strawman: when the user fixed the look, craft and specificity do the raising.

## Implications and gate
Write each finding as "because [finding], the picture must / should / must avoid [consequence]", tagged given, researched, or inferred.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every finding changes or confirms a named decision · the appeal bar is stated as visible traits and no code is departed from without a named gain · the design truth passes the swap test or is recorded absent · the prior read has concrete keep, raise, and fix · the objective, message, groups, and tiers are consistent, every quality-versus-load tension is answered by structure, and a dense verdict has its way out. Then append the record.
