# Phase 2: interpret the brief

**Needs:** S. **Produces:** the **interpretation**: the strategic core, the reads, the communication problem with reading tiers and feasibility, the appeal bar, the raw material, the prior read, and **mandates** addressed to Brand, Theme, and Picture. S is not restated. This phase is the foundation: brand, theme, and every element inherit its conclusions, so an error here travels to every layer. **Guided:** no stop (a fork, §3, excepted); its findings lead the first card message.
**Order:** reads (§2) → cross-read (§3) → problem, encounter, feasibility (§4) → appeal bar and category field (§5) → raw material (§6) → prior read (§7) → mandates and gate (§8). A finding that changes no decision is dropped.

## 1. How to interpret
Think as a brand strategist, creative director, and communication designer at once. Interpretation goes beyond what is written by asking what the facts *imply*, and stays disciplined by evidence:
- **Basis:** every conclusion carries its basis: given, inferred (from which facts), or researched. Aspiration above evidence is downgraded; a small fact never becomes a fictional identity.
- **Sources:** S; every link and file the user supplied, opened and read; and, when a search tool is available, at most five targeted lookups across this phase (the business's own public words and look; the audience's world; current strong work and the peers' identities, §5). Research is evidence to weigh, never a look to copy, and never overrides the user.
- **Specificity over description:** state the conclusion that would be false of a named peer or of a different customer; drop what any competent person would infer from the category alone.
- A reading too thin to support a decision is a fork (§3) or is tagged provisional with the most probable reading.

## 2. The reads
A few sentences each, with basis tags; omit what changes no decision.
1. **Business.** Identity and background (what it is, how it began, who stands behind it, what it does and how it behaves, scale); positioning (its stance against the alternatives its audience weighs, price tier, what it competes on and what it declines to); **distinctive truth**, its USP (what it does, is, or holds that is valued, true, and unclaimed by named peers); personality and values as shown by conduct, not adjectives; credibility actually held and claims never to make; the owner's aspiration and how far its standing can carry a departure.
2. **Audience.** The ideal buyer as a specific person in a specific moment, not a segment: who, in what circumstance, wanting what, wary of what; what they already believe about the category and about this business; the real motive behind the action, which often differs from the stated one; what they respect and find beautiful; the cues that say "for me" and "not for me". Where the user gave only demographics, derive the situational and motivational implications and tag them inferred.
3. **Perception and message.** The shift sought: what the audience thinks and feels now → what the business wants them to think and feel; the value it wants credited; **the message** in one sentence (what the glance viewer takes away and does next); the proof that makes it believable. The message serves the objective and contradicts no quality.
4. **Purpose and response.** The job the design does in the business's life; the response and call to action it must trigger; what stands between the viewer and that action. **Objective:** a user-stated priority fixes the primary objective; the weakest link (§4) is chosen within it and never replaces it.
5. **Atmosphere.** The state change sought in the viewer (arrives as …, leaves as …), grounded in their state at the encounter and in the business's personality. The user's stated qualities enter at their own intensity and stay binding; the derived atmosphere deepens them and never calms or narrows them.
6. **Other considerations.** Culture, timing and occasion, sensitivities, legal and channel limits: anything else that changes a decision.

## 3. The cross-read
A summary is not an interpretation. First **chain** each decisive fact: fact → what it means to this audience → what it must therefore look or feel like → the layer that decides it. A fact that stops at its meaning, with no visual consequence, has not been interpreted. Then lay the reads side by side and look for what appears only in combination: a tension between who the business is and what the audience expects; a true detail the audience would value that peers do not use; a link between facts that seem unrelated (origin, behavior, the audience's moment, the occasion); a gap between aspiration and evidence. Produce the **strategic core**:
- **Design truth:** one sentence, "[specific true thing], [productive tension]", where the tension is a contrast inside the business or the audience's moment. It must fail the **swap test**: substituting a named peer makes it false. If nothing passes, say so: the identity then rests on the name, the place, and the audience's moment, claims stay modest, and the notes invite one more detail from the user.
- **Stance:** how the business carries itself toward this audience, as conduct, one sentence, no stacked adjectives.
- **Perception shift** and **message**, sharpened by what the cross-read found.
**Five tests, per conclusion:** **Evidence** (basis stated) · **Swap** (false for a named peer or another customer) · **Obviousness** (not inferable from the category alone) · **Opposite** (its opposite does not fit the evidence equally) · **Use** (it changes a named decision at a named layer). A conclusion that fails Opposite is a **fork**: ask once, in one short message of at most two questions, each offered as two to four readings to pick from; if the user skips it, take the best-supported reading, tag it, and flag it in the notes. A conclusion that fails Obviousness or Use is deepened with the user's specifics or dropped.

## 4. Communication problem, encounter, feasibility
**Encounter and reading tiers.** One profile per distinct context; images sharing a context share it. Start from the Phase 1 inference and test it. Derive from how the image is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' color, density, scale; competing stimuli), the audience's state (goal, motion, attention, time, distance, light, culture), the reading path. Hold the **impression** (what the viewer should feel and conclude) constant; let the setting decide the expression.
- **Reading tiers** (derived, not assumed to be three): **glance** (what a passing viewer takes in at the farthest distance), **approach**, **close**. Assign by when the viewer needs an item, never by whether it fits. Glance holds the hero and at most two short items in the order the message needs them; the name or mark takes a slot only where recognition drives the action, otherwise the offer or occasion comes first. Reading size suited to a distance is about distance ÷ 150 for sustained lines and ÷ 115 for a passing glance (cap height, tagged assumption), used to judge feasibility only. **Core content** (what the action depends on: offers, prices, choices) keeps comfortable reading at its tier; only **reference content** (contacts, hours, fine print) may read deliberately.
- If the context cannot be established, state the most probable one and mark it provisional.

**The problem.**
- **Information groups:** by the job each does toward the objective (carry the proposition, support what is seen first, enable the offer and action, substantiate trust, give practical information), never by similar looks or the user's list order. Per group: items, tier, what it refers to, internal hierarchy, volume. Items that label, price, or explain a depicted thing belong with it.
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility, register). State both needs and the resource. **A tension between a quality and the load is resolved by structure (grouping, tiers, scale steps, a density note), never by lowering the quality.** Record the lead side only where the conflict priority (`SKILL.md`) decides, and what the other side keeps.
- **Weakest link:** attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs: the one most likely to fail here. Test the brief's own assumptions; one that undermines the objective becomes a tension.

**Feasibility** (one pass, only for content-heavy images). Can this content be read at this distance in this format? Estimate once, for the most compact sensible arrangement (a hero beside its own text and equal units in columns share one band; extent = the larger): each group's need = planned lines × pitch (≈ 1.6 × cap for display lines, ≈ 2 × cap for body text) at its tier's reading size, as a share of the long side, plus ≈ 15% for gaps; add the hero's minimum extent and an overhead of 24% (structure and decoration 5, quiet 8, margins and separations 8, headroom 3). Over 100% is **dense**. Dense is answered by structure, never by deleting mandatory content: (1) group and tier so core items lead and reference items recede; (2) simplify the hero's footprint before shrinking core content; (3) tell the user once the way out (larger format, less text, a second image); (4) carry the tiers and a density note into the brief. The verdict never becomes numbers in the brief. Dense content is also the tool's weakest ground: the picture later keeps exact text in few, calm, well-separated zones.

## 5. The appeal bar and the category field
The output is judged by the audience's eye, not by the brief's logic. Establish what this audience finds beautiful and trustworthy **now**, and what finished professional work in their visual world looks like at this price tier: depth of rendering, finish, color energy, density, lettering sophistication, composition habits.
- **Sources:** the user's references and stated tastes; your knowledge; and, when a search tool is available and the piece is image-led, targeted looks (within §1's limit) at current strong work in that world and at the peers' identities. Extract the craft level and its logic; a competitor is a measure, never a model. Without tools, reason and mark provisional.
- **The bar:** the finish level a viewer would call professional here, as visible traits (how forms are modelled, how light behaves, how lettering is built, how the whole is composed). It is a floor for every layer's output and is never set below what the user's own words imply.
- **The audience's codes:** the signifiers that tell them "this is for me" (occasion, age, culture, category). Each is marked **meet** (carry its function through specific, well-made execution) or **depart** (only for a named gain in appeal or clarity). A signifier the audience relies on is not automatically a cliché: it is met and made distinct through execution.
- **The category field:** how peers and the category habitually look (their marks, color behavior, lettering, imagery, composition). This is the **default territory** the identity and the theme must read as different from, recorded to be distinct from, never to be imitated.

## 6. Raw material
Seeds come from the business and its world, never from the category's stock imagery.
List the drawable specifics: what it sells and how it behaves, its name and character, materials and process, place and culture, the occasion's own habits, the audience's insider objects, what peers already claim. Test each: **true** (failure discards), **relevant** at the encounter, **depictable** (the maker can render it truthfully and attractively), **ownable** (a named peer could not use it unchanged). Write its **visual yield** in one clause: what would actually be seen (a form, a situation, a relation between elements, a color behavior, a lettering construction). A seed whose yield is only a metaphor, or a simulated material or object-as-surface, is dropped or rebuilt as a graphic or situational form. A seed from content below the glance tier serves as a supporting device, never the governing idea. Mark each seed by the layer it feeds: **identity-level** (lasting: name, origin, craft, character) feeds the brand; **occasion-level** (this design's moment, setting, offer) feeds the theme. Where the user fixed the look, mine only what it leaves open.

## 7. The prior read
The maker has a strong prior. Write what it would most likely make from the user's own words alone: how it would draw or fake the logo and identity, the look and the idea (usually a style and containers, not a theme), composition, density, lettering, and how it would handle each stated quality. Be honest about its strengths; it is usually polished, appealing, and well colored, and the brief must never trade those away. Then three lists, each item tagged **brand**, **theme**, or **picture**:
- **Keep:** what the prior does well for this audience; carried by the work, not restated.
- **Raise:** where specificity lifts the result above the prior: a mark and identity that belong to this business, a theme with a world and a logic, the hero's design and situation, the interplay of hero and content, composition logic, lettering as designed objects, depth and richness at the user's intensity.
- **Fix:** the failures this brief's own features make likely (volume of exact text, digits, many small depicted items with exact counts, precise geometry or alignment, character details drifting, a hierarchy that buries the offer, a logo that drifts or fuses with its surroundings) and the generic defaults the prior falls to where specifics are missing. Each gets a positive remedy in the work, or a flagged limit when the tool cannot deliver it.
Every later decision names the list it serves; a decision on none is noise. The prior is the bar to beat, not a strawman: when the user fixed the look, craft and specificity do the raising.

## 8. Mandates, handoff, gate
Write each finding as a numbered line, "B1 / T1 / P1: because [finding], the [layer] must / should / must avoid [consequence]", tagged given, researched, or inferred, and addressed to one layer. Each layer answers its mandates by id (`choose.md` §4), and the brief is audited against all of them (`6_compose.md` §4):
- **Brand** (lasting identity): what the identity must signal, never signal, and survive.
- **Theme** (this design): what it must do for the perception shift, the response, the atmosphere, and the encounter.
- **Picture** (execution): legibility, hierarchy, density, dependability.
**Handoff.** To Brand: stance, distinctive truth, the audience's eye and codes, the default territory, identity-level raw material, brand mandates. To Theme: perception shift, message, response, weakest link, atmosphere, encounter, groups and tiers, occasion-level raw material, theme mandates.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every read has a basis and passes the swap and obviousness tests, or is dropped · the strategic core passes its five tests, or its absence is recorded · every decisive fact has a visual consequence and every finding changes or confirms a named decision at a named layer · the appeal bar is stated as visible traits and no code is departed from without a named gain · the prior read has concrete keep, raise, and fix, tagged by layer · the objective, message, groups, and tiers are consistent, every quality-versus-load tension is answered by structure, and a dense verdict has its way out. Then append the record.
