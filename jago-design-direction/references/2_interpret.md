# Phase 2: interpret the brief

**Needs:** S. **Produces:** the **interpretation**: strategic core, reads, communication problem (content map, tiers, feasibility), appeal bar, raw material, prior read, and **mandates** for Brand, Theme, and Picture. This phase is the foundation: every layer inherits its conclusions, so an error here travels everywhere. **Guided:** no stop (a fork, §3, excepted); its findings lead the first card message.
**Order:** as numbered. A finding that changes no decision is dropped.

## 1. How to interpret
Think as brand strategist, creative director, and communication designer: ask what the facts *imply*, under evidence discipline and the tests of §3.
- **Basis:** every conclusion is tagged given, inferred (from which facts), or researched. Aspiration above evidence is downgraded; a small fact never becomes a fictional identity.
- **Sources:** S; every link and file supplied, opened and read; and, with a search tool, at most five targeted lookups in this phase (the business itself, its audience's world, peers' identities and strong current work, §5). Research is weighed, never copied, never overrides the user.
- A reading too thin to support a decision is a fork (§3) or is tagged provisional with the most probable reading.

## 2. The reads
A few sentences each, basis-tagged; omit what changes no decision.
1. **Business.** Identity and background (what it is, how it began, who stands behind it, how it behaves, scale); positioning (stance against the alternatives its audience weighs, price tier, what it competes on and declines to); **distinctive truth**, its USP (valued, true, unclaimed by named peers); personality and values as shown by conduct; credibility held and claims never to make; the owner's aspiration and how far its standing can carry a departure.
2. **Audience.** The ideal buyer as a specific person in a specific moment, not a segment: who, in what circumstance, wanting and wary of what; what they believe about the category and this business; the real motive, often different from the stated one; what they respect and find beautiful; the cues saying "for me" and "not for me". Where the decider differs from the user or the delighted, read both: what each must find, at which tier. From demographics alone, derive situational and motivational implications, tagged inferred.
3. **Perception and message.** The shift sought (what the audience thinks and feels now → what the business wants); the value to be credited; **the message** in one sentence (what the glance viewer takes away and does next); the proof that makes it believable. It serves the objective and contradicts no quality.
4. **Purpose and response.** The job the design does in the business's life; the response and call to action; what stands between viewer and action. **Objective:** a user-stated priority fixes it; the weakest link (§4) is chosen within it and never replaces it.
5. **Atmosphere.** The state change sought (arrives as …, leaves as …), grounded in the viewer's state at the encounter and the business's personality. Stated qualities stay binding at their intensity; the derived atmosphere deepens them, never calms or narrows them.
6. **Other considerations.** Culture, timing, occasion, sensitivities, legal and channel limits: anything else that changes a decision.

## 3. The cross-read
A summary is not an interpretation. First **chain** each decisive fact: fact → what it means to this audience → what it must therefore look or feel like → the layer that decides it; a fact with no visual consequence is not yet interpreted. Then read them against each other for what appears only in combination: a tension between who the business is and what the audience expects; a true detail the audience values that peers do not use; a link between seemingly unrelated facts (origin, behavior, the audience's moment, the occasion); a gap between aspiration and evidence. Produce the **strategic core**:
- **Design truth:** "[specific true thing], [productive tension]", the tension a contrast inside the business or the audience's moment; it must fail the **swap test** (a named peer makes it false). If nothing passes, say so: the identity rests on name, place, and audience moment, claims stay modest, and the notes invite one more detail.
- **Stance:** how the business carries itself toward this audience, as conduct, one sentence, no stacked adjectives.
- **Perception shift** and **message**, sharpened by the cross-read.
**Five tests, per conclusion:** **Evidence** (basis stated) · **Swap** (false for a named peer or another customer) · **Obviousness** (not inferable from the category alone) · **Opposite** (its opposite does not fit the evidence equally) · **Use** (it changes a named decision at a named layer). Failing Opposite makes a **fork**: ask once, one short message of at most two questions, each offering two to four readings; if skipped, take the best-supported reading, tag it, flag it in the notes. Failing Obviousness or Use: deepen with the user's specifics or drop. **Stated differentiators:** every strength, specification, or reason to choose the user stated is used by the core or ranked below another truth with a stated reason; none is silently dropped.

## 4. Communication problem, encounter, feasibility
**Encounter and tiers.** One profile per distinct context; images sharing a context share it. Test the Phase 1 inference; derive from how the image is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name; if unestablished, state the most probable and mark it provisional.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' color, density, scale; competing stimuli), the audience's state (goal, motion, attention, time, distance, light, culture), the reading path. Hold the **impression** (what the viewer should feel and conclude) constant; the setting decides its expression.
- **Reading tiers** (derived, not assumed three): **glance** (what a passing viewer takes in at the farthest distance), **approach**, **close**. Assign by when the viewer needs an item, never by whether it fits. Glance holds the hero and at most two short items in the order the message needs; the name or mark takes a slot only where recognition drives the action, otherwise the offer or occasion comes first. Reading size ≈ distance ÷ 150 for sustained lines, ÷ 115 for a passing glance (cap height, tagged assumption), for feasibility only. **Core content** (what the decision and action depend on: offer, choices, prices, every selling point) reads comfortably at its tier; only **reference content** (what a viewer seeks after deciding to act: contact details, hours, fine print) may read deliberately. A selling point is never reference content, however small or specification-like.

**The problem.**
- **Content map** (what belongs together, why, what role each group serves):
  1. **Units:** divide the ledger into the smallest units that each do one job: a name, its contents, its price; two claims joined by a separator; a label and its value. A unit keeps its words; dividing never alters them.
  2. **Classify** each unit by the question it answers for the viewer (what is this, why choose it, what do I get and pay, can I trust it, how do I act and where) and by whether it carries the distinctive truth or a stated differentiator: such a unit is a **selling point** whatever its type or where the user listed it, specifications included. The user's headings and adjacency are evidence of intended relationship: weigh them, override only with a stated reason.
  3. **Group** units that answer the same question about the same object and are read together. Units answering different questions never share a group for being small, low-ranked, or left over; one group per role, as many as roles need.
  4. **Rank and relate:** per group its purpose, units, tier, internal hierarchy, volume, relation to the primary message (proposition, proof, offer, action), and the depicted thing it must stay with (labels, prices, and explanations of it).
  5. **Data and labels:** for an identifier (contact channel, handle, number, address, hours) the **value** is the content; a descriptor that only names the kind of data is a **label**, a candidate for a pictogram (`5_system.md` §2); one that adds meaning (who or what it is for) is content and stays words.
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility, register); state both and the resource. **A tension between a quality and the load is resolved by structure (grouping, tiers, scale steps, a density note), never by lowering the quality.** Record the lead side only where the conflict priority (`SKILL.md`) decides, and what the other side keeps.
- **Weakest link:** attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs: the one most likely to fail here. Test the brief's own assumptions; one undermining the objective is a tension.

**Feasibility** (one pass, content-heavy images only). Can this content be read at this distance in this format? Estimate once for the most compact sensible arrangement (a hero beside its own text, and equal units in columns, share one band; extent = the larger): each group's need = planned lines × pitch (≈ 1.6 × cap for display lines, ≈ 2 × cap for body text) at its tier's reading size, as a share of the long side, plus ≈ 15% for gaps; add the hero's minimum extent and 24% overhead (structure and decoration 5, quiet 8, margins and separations 8, headroom 3). Over 100% is **dense**, answered by structure, never by deleting mandatory content: (1) group and tier so core items lead and reference items recede, and let pictograms replace labels so words carry only what needs words; (2) simplify the hero's footprint before shrinking core content; (3) tell the user once the way out (larger format, less text, a second image); (4) carry tiers and a density note into the brief. The verdict never becomes numbers in the brief.

## 5. The appeal bar and the category field
The output is judged by the audience's eye, not the brief's logic. Establish what this audience finds beautiful and trustworthy **now**, and what professional work in their visual world looks like at this price tier: rendering depth, finish, color energy, density, lettering sophistication, composition habits.
- **Sources:** the user's references and tastes; your knowledge; with a search tool and an image-led piece, targeted looks (within §1's limit) at current strong work in that world and at peers' identities. Extract craft level and logic; a competitor is a measure, never a model. Without tools, reason and mark provisional.
- **The bar:** the finish a viewer would call professional here, as visible traits (how forms are modelled, light behaves, lettering is built, the whole is composed). A floor for every layer, never set below what the user's words imply.
- **The audience's codes:** signifiers saying "this is for me" (occasion, age, culture, category), each marked **meet** (carry its function through specific, well-made execution) or **depart** (only for a named gain in appeal or clarity). A signifier the audience relies on is not automatically a cliché: meet it and make it distinct through execution.
- **The category field:** how peers and the category habitually look (marks, color behavior, lettering, imagery, composition): the **default territory** the identity and theme must read as different from, never imitate.

## 6. Raw material
Seeds come from the business and its world, never the category's stock imagery. List the drawable specifics: what it sells (each item the content names is a candidate for its own depiction) and how it behaves, name and character, materials and process, place and culture, the occasion's habits, the audience's insider objects, what peers already claim. Test each: **true**, **relevant** at the encounter, **depictable** (the maker can render it truthfully and attractively), **ownable** (a named peer could not use it unchanged). Write its **visual yield** in one clause: what would actually be seen (a form, a situation, a relation between elements, a color behavior, a lettering construction). A seed yielding only a metaphor, a simulated material, or an object-as-surface is dropped or rebuilt as a graphic or situational form. A seed from below-glance content is a supporting device, never the governing idea. Mark each seed by the layer it feeds: **identity-level** (lasting: name, origin, craft, character) feeds the brand; **occasion-level** (this design's moment, setting, offer) feeds the theme. Where the user fixed the look, mine only what it leaves open.

## 7. The prior read
The maker has a strong prior. Write what it would most likely make from the user's words alone: how it would draw or fake the logo and identity, the look and idea (usually a style and containers, not a theme), composition, density, lettering, and each stated quality. Be honest about its strengths: usually polished, appealing, well colored, and naturally turning information into pictures (channel symbols, depicted items); the brief never trades these away by fencing content into plain text. Then three lists, each item tagged **brand**, **theme**, or **picture**:
- **Keep:** what the prior does well for this audience; carried by the work, not restated.
- **Raise:** where specificity lifts the result above the prior: a mark and identity belonging to this business, a theme with a world and a logic, the hero's design and situation, hero-content interplay, composition logic, lettering as designed objects, depth and richness at the user's intensity.
- **Fix:** failures this brief's features make likely (volume of exact text, digits, many small depicted items with exact counts, precise geometry or alignment, drifting character details, a hierarchy burying the offer, a logo drifting or fusing with its surroundings) and the generic defaults it falls to where specifics are missing. Each gets a positive remedy in the work, or a flagged limit when the maker cannot deliver it.
Every later decision names the list it serves; one on none is noise. The prior is the bar to beat, not a strawman: when the user fixed the look, craft and specificity do the raising.

## 8. Mandates, handoff, gate
Write each finding as a numbered line, "B1 / T1 / P1: because [finding], the [layer] must / should / must avoid [consequence]", tagged given, researched, or inferred, and addressed to one layer. Each layer answers its mandates by id (`choose.md` §4); the brief is audited against all of them (`6_compose.md` §4):
- **Brand** (lasting identity): what it must signal, never signal, and survive.
- **Theme** (this design): what it must do for the perception shift, response, atmosphere, and encounter.
- **Picture** (execution): legibility, hierarchy, density, dependability.
**Handoff.** To Brand: stance, distinctive truth, the audience's eye and codes, default territory, identity-level raw material, brand mandates. To Theme: perception shift, message, response, weakest link, atmosphere, encounter, content map and tiers, occasion-level raw material, theme mandates.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every read has a basis and passes swap and obviousness, or is dropped · the strategic core passes its five tests, or its absence is recorded · every decisive fact has a visual consequence and every finding changes or confirms a named decision at a named layer · the appeal bar is stated as visible traits and no code is departed from without a named gain · the prior read has concrete keep, raise, and fix, tagged by layer · objective, message, content map (every unit classified by the question it answers, every stated differentiator placed as a selling point or ranked with a reason), and tiers are consistent; every quality-versus-load tension is answered by structure; a dense verdict has its way out. Then append the record.
