# Phase 3: brand design

**Needs:** S; the interpretation. **Produces:** the **brand record**: the business's lasting visual identity, an idea given form by a mark and a visual system, that the theme and every later decision inherit. The method for candidates, judging, and cards is `choose.md`; this file supplies the brand's specifics. **Guided:** brand cards (§7) and stop, unless the identity is Fixed.
**Level.** The interpretation says what the business must make people feel and believe; the brand decides **how it looks and behaves, lastingly**: its artistic direction and visual language, anchored by the mark. It answers who the business is visually across every design, year, and medium, never this piece's message, staging, placement, or content (those are the theme's). It is decided once per business: later designs inherit this record as Fixed unless the user reopens it, and an inherited brand is re-read against each new interpretation: a conflict with that perception contract is flagged for the user, never silently bent.

## 1. What the brand design is
Identity is **one idea expressed by a mark and a visual system that share one construction logic**. Decide the facets this business and its designs will use, scaled to the business (a small one needs a compact identity, a complex one more); never invent a facet for completeness. Each facet reads the ones before it and names the item it serves.
1. **Identity idea and personality:** what the brand is to its customers, in one sentence, with the personality it shows as visible behavior (the stance made seeable) and the trait-to-form map of §3.
2. **Mark system:** the central standalone mark; its lockup with the name; its behavior alone, small, in one color, and reversed. Where the business has or the brief names a **recurring figure** (mascot, character), the figure belongs to the identity and is designed here: its kind, personality, three to five features that read at distance, proportions, and what keeps it one consistent character. The theme later gives it situations.
3. **Artistic direction (the lasting style):** the **lineage** (the family of form-making: how it builds form, handles edges, models light, uses space, ranks detail), the **image-making class** it works in and its rendering stance (depth of modelling, line, light, surface), the **imagery stance** (how it depicts things, from what point of view, what it shows and never shows), and the **craft bar**: the visible traits of the best work in the audience's visual world at this tier, the floor for every later layer. The lineage is as narrow as era, region, and technique allow and carries its richness logic with its simplifications; a medium or format word (vector, 3D, photographic, flat) is a production class, never a lineage; never a living artist, studio, or protected character.
4. **Visual language:**
   - *Lettering voice:* roles (display, informational, numerals, small print), construction logic, and how name and display lettering relate to the mark.
   - *Color identity:* roles and behavior (what leads, what signals, what clears, how they relate) and the value-key tendency, not a swatch list; colors the user fixed stay fixed.
   - *Form and graphic language:* the shape and edge logic shared by every shape, container, and pictogram; ornament and line behavior.
   - *Space and layout behavior:* density tendency, rhythm, scale contrast, how it uses quiet. These are lasting habits, never one piece's layout.
5. **Signatures, constants, flex, range:** one to three recurring, drawable signatures generated by the mark's language, each with its scope; what holds in every design and what never appears (constants); what a theme may vary (flex); how far the identity stretches across media, formats, and moods.
The mark is decided whenever the design will carry the business's identifier. If only a plain-text name is supplied or requested, the lettering voice is the identifier and the rest of the identity is still decided.

## 2. The brand study
The interpretation fixed what must be felt; the study finds how visual form carries it in this audience's world. Do it once, before the brief.
- **The audience's visual world:** what work they find beautiful and trustworthy now, and what professional work at this price tier looks like: rendering depth, finish, color energy, density, lettering sophistication, composition habits.
- **The audience's codes:** the signifiers behind the interpretation's "pull" (occasion, age, culture, category), each marked **meet** (carry its function through specific, well-made execution) or **depart** (only for a named gain in appeal or clarity). A signifier the audience relies on is not a cliché by default.
- **The default territory:** how peers and the category habitually look (marks, color behavior, lettering, imagery, composition). The identity reads as plainly different, never imitating it.
- **The craft bar:** from the above, the visible traits that make work in this world professional at this tier (how forms are modelled, light behaves, lettering is built, the whole is composed). A higher bar means better making, never a dearer-looking offer: the register stays the perception contract's. Never below what the user's words imply.
- **Sources:** the user's references and tastes; knowledge; with a search tool and an image-led piece, at most three targeted looks at current strong work in that world and at peers' identities. A competitor is a measure, never a model. Without tools, reason and mark provisional.

## 3. The identity brief
Before any candidate, turn the interpretation and the study into what the identity must answer, as a short list: the **stance** it makes visible; the **distinctive truth** it rests on; the identity-level **feel profile**; what the **audience's eye** trusts and desires and the codes to meet or depart; the **perception contract** (what the identity must read as, and never read as, on each binding dimension); the **default territory** to be plainly distinct from; the **use conditions** it must survive (smallest size, one color, light and dark grounds, the range of media).
- **Trait-to-form map:** each trait of the stance, the distinctive truth, and the feel profile, paired with the form consequence the mark and language carry (shape, proportion, line behavior, color behavior, rendering); a trait with no form consequence is dropped from the brief for the identity.
- **Register translation:** this is where the contract first becomes visible. For each binding dimension, name the carriers that produce its position (finish, material richness, ornament density, restraint, scale of space, lettering polish, imagery handling) and the cues that would signal against it, so later layers answer the contract through carriers rather than adjectives.
Every brand decision names the item it serves, and every brand mandate is answered by id.

## 4. Reading a supplied identity
A logo or identity set supplied at exact or faithful fidelity makes the mark **Fixed by the asset**: no mark candidates. Reading it is the creative work of this phase. Look at it; never assume it; never redesign it. Record:
- **What it is:** the kind of mark and its relation to its lettering.
- **How it is built:** geometry, proportion, weight and stroke behavior, terminals and corners, negative space, symmetry or movement, density, color and its relations; and, from any supplied materials, the artistic direction already visible (lineage, rendering, imagery, lettering).
- **What it actually projects:** the personality seen in the form, not what the user says it is, set against the identity brief: where they agree and what it does not carry.
- **Strengths to amplify, and constraints it imposes:** what it needs around it, at what size, on which grounds.
- **What the language borrows:** the form logic, proportions, colors, and rhythm from which artistic direction, lettering voice, shape logic, signatures, and supporting graphics are **derived** (one recorded reading, no cards), so the surroundings read as the mark's family. What the mark does not carry is supplied by the surroundings, never by altering the mark; a conflict with the interpretation is recorded and, when it matters, noted for the user.
At conceptual or free fidelity the asset is read the same way and the mark becomes Steered: candidates explore within that fidelity. A supplied asset that cannot be seen is asked for once.

## 5. Candidates (Open and Steered)
Method: `choose.md` §3.
- **Foundations** come from the interpretation: each candidate leads with a different part of the business (what is distinctive, what the customer gets and why they choose it, the audience's world, the stance, the name and origin, the craft) and so builds a different customer perception. Brand options are different answers to what this business is visually to its customer, never different drawings of one answer: candidates that share an identity idea and an artistic direction are variants, however different their marks.
- **Seeds** come from the identity-level raw material and the study: the proposition itself, the name and its letters, origin, how the business behaves, its craft or process, the audience's own objects and visual world, gaps in the default territory, the founder's truth. A seed's yield (`choose.md` §3) is a form, a construction, a relation, or a way of making, never a metaphor alone and never a stock device of the category.
- **Structural axes** (how a foundation becomes an identity): the identity idea and its source; the mark's kind and its relation to the name; the lineage and image-making class (how it builds form, models light, handles edges and space); the language's construction logic (lettering, color behavior, shape and graphic devices, space behavior); the register its finish signals; the figure, if any.
- **Develop each** as the dossier in `choose.md` §3, with these fields:
  - **Identity idea:** one sentence, with the personality it makes visible.
  - **The mark, narrated:** three to five plain sentences, as seen alone on a plain field: what it is, its silhouette, its construction (geometry, proportion, weight, terminals, negative space), the one feature that makes it ownable, and how it sits with the name. Specific enough that two skilled designers would draw recognizably related marks. A recurring figure gets two or three sentences more (kind, features that read at distance, proportions).
  - **Artistic direction:** three to five sentences: lineage and what is adopted from it, rendering stance, imagery stance, and the craft traits, concrete enough that two skilled makers produce recognizably related work.
  - **Language:** lettering voice, color behavior, form logic and graphic devices, space behavior, signatures, constants and flex, each in a clause naming the identity-brief item it serves.
  - **Against the default territory:** how it is plainly distinct, and which of the audience's codes it keeps.

## 6. The brand review
Run on each candidate, and again on the chosen record (for a supplied asset it guides the surroundings and never judges the mark). Each question answers yes or names a move.
1. **Distinct:** is the identity recognizable by form and manner alone, plainly different from the peers' and the category's habitual marks and looks, not a stock device, and more than a styled name?
2. **Standalone:** does the mark work alone, with no name, in one color, and small, and does it stand clear of anything around it?
3. **Embodies:** does each trait in the trait-to-form map have its carrier in the form, and does the identity fail the swap test?
4. **One logic:** do mark, lettering, color, shapes, and imagery follow one construction logic, as a family and not a symbol set beside generic type?
5. **Generative:** can the mark's language produce lettering, shapes, supporting graphics, and signatures, and extend across the media and moods the business will use, without forcing them?
6. **Appeal and respect:** would the audience find it beautiful and trustworthy, and does it carry no unintended meaning, resemblance, or cultural reading?
7. **Dependability:** can the maker draw it consistently (mark and figure): few parts, no reliance on fine detail, exact geometry, or small lettering inside the mark?
8. **Positioning:** do the finish, material richness, ornament, and polish in the register translation signal the contract's position on every binding dimension, and nothing it rules out?
9. **Artistic direction:** **Strike** (remove medium, format, and mood words: a drawable look remains) · **Home turf** (it sits in the visual world the audience already finds appealing, at or above the craft bar) · **Coherence** (lineage, rendering, line, color, and imagery do not cancel one another).
**Gates:** Distinct, Standalone, Embodies, Dependability, Positioning, Artistic direction.

## 7. Choose, cards, record
Judge and choose by `choose.md` §4. **R** is measured against the identity brief through its trait-to-form map (stance, distinctive truth, feel profile, perception contract, the audience's eye); **D** against the default territory and the peers' identities. The added ranking criterion is **Durable** (would it still serve this business across designs, years, sizes, and moods?). Card:
```
**Step 3 of 5: brand identity**
What I learned: [one or two lines: who the business is, who it is for, what makes it distinctive, what the look must and must not signal]
| Identity | What you will see | Why / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **[name]**: [idea, ≤ 12 words] | [two or three sentences: the logo as seen alone, then the style of the artwork and lettering around it] | [the perception it builds for this business] · [weakness] |
| [name] | ... | ... |
| [name] | ... | ... |
You said "…" → carried as: …
OK, or tell me what to change?
```
**The brand record:** the identity brief answered (one line) · the identity idea and personality · the mark (narrated, or read from the asset): construction, lockup, behavior alone; the figure, if any · the artistic direction: lineage and what is adopted and left behind, image-making class and rendering stance, imagery stance, the **craft bar** as visible traits · the visual language: lettering voice, color identity, form and graphic language, space behavior · the **register translation** · signatures with scope · constants, flex, and range · a because-clause per decision · the **handoff** to Theme: constants it must keep, flex it may use, the register translation and craft bar it must meet, and what the identity needs the theme to achieve.

## Gate (adds to `choose.md`)
The candidates lead with different parts of the business, build different perceptions, and differ in identity idea or artistic direction, not only in the mark · the study and the identity brief were derived from the interpretation and every decision names its item · a supplied asset was read, not redesigned · the chosen identity passes its gates and the swap test · the mark stands alone and the language is generated from it · the artistic direction is drawable, at or above the craft bar, and decided here, not left to later layers · the register translation answers every contract position through carriers · nothing decided belongs to this piece (boundary test, `SKILL.md`) · the handoff separates constants from flex.
