# Phase 3: brand design

**Needs:** S; the interpretation. **Produces:** the **brand record**: the visual identity and design language, anchored by the mark, that the theme and every later decision inherit. The method for candidates, judging, and cards is `choose.md`; this file supplies the brand's specifics. **Guided:** brand cards (§6) and stop, unless the identity is Fixed.
The brand is the business's lasting visual identity. It is derived from who the business is (the interpretation's stance, distinctive truth, audience's eye, default territory, and brand mandates), not from the design at hand, and it is decided once per business: later designs in the conversation inherit this record as Fixed unless the user reopens it.

## 1. What the brand design is
The mark is the anchor; the rest of the identity is its language, extended. Decide only the facets this business and its designs will use, scaled to the business (a small one needs a compact identity, a complex one more); never invent a facet for completeness.
- **The mark:** the central, standalone identifier, its lockup with the name, and its behavior alone, small, in one color, and reversed.
- **Lettering voice:** how the name and display lettering are constructed.
- **Color identity:** roles and behavior (what leads, what signals, how they relate), not a swatch list.
- **Shape and form logic, and its lineage:** the construction logic that every shape, container, and supporter shares, and the family of form-making it belongs to (how it builds form, handles edges, uses space).
- **Imagery and illustration stance:** how the brand depicts things, and from what point of view.
- **Signatures (one to three):** recurring, drawable devices generated by the mark's language, each with its scope.
- **Constants and flex:** what holds in every design and what never appears; what a theme may vary.
The mark is decided whenever the design will carry the business's identifier. If only a plain-text name is supplied or requested, the lettering voice is the identifier and the language is still decided.

## 2. The identity brief
Before any candidate, turn the interpretation into what the identity must answer, as a short list: the **stance** it makes visible; the **distinctive truth** it rests on; what the **audience's eye** trusts and desires, and the codes to meet or depart; the **default territory** to be plainly distinct from; the **use conditions** it must survive (smallest size, one color, light and dark grounds, the media it will live in). Add a **trait-to-form map**: each trait of the stance and the distinctive truth, paired with the form consequence the mark and language will carry (shape, proportion, line behavior, color behavior); a trait with no form consequence is dropped from the brief for the identity. Every brand decision names the item it serves, and every brand mandate is answered by id.

## 3. Reading a supplied identity
A logo or identity set supplied at exact or faithful fidelity makes the brand **Fixed by the asset**: no candidates. Reading it is the creative work of this phase. Look at it; never assume it; never redesign it. Record:
- **What it is:** the kind of mark and its relation to its lettering.
- **How it is built:** geometry, proportion, weight and stroke behavior, terminals and corners, negative space, symmetry or movement, density, color and its relations.
- **What it actually projects:** the personality seen in the form, not what the user says it is, set against the identity brief: where they agree and what the mark does not carry.
- **Strengths to amplify, and constraints it imposes:** what it needs around it, at what size, on which grounds.
- **What the language may borrow:** the form logic, proportions, colors, and rhythm from which lettering voice, shape logic, signatures, and supporting graphics are derived, so the surroundings read as the mark's family. What the mark does not carry is supplied by the surroundings, never by altering the mark; a conflict with the interpretation is recorded and, when it matters, noted for the user.
At conceptual or free fidelity the asset is read the same way and the mark becomes Steered: candidates explore within that fidelity. A supplied asset that cannot be seen is asked for once.

## 4. Candidates (Open and Steered)
- **Seeds for the mark** come from the identity-level raw material: the name and its own letters, origin, how the business works or behaves, its craft or process, the audience's own objects, the founder's truth. A seed's yield is a form, a construction, or a relation (figure and ground, a gesture, an interplay), never a metaphor alone and never a stock device of the category.
- **Structural axes:** the mark's core idea and its source; the mark's kind and its relation to the name; the form logic and lineage (how it builds form, handles edges, uses space); the color behavior.
- **Develop each:**
  - **The mark, narrated:** three to five plain sentences, as seen alone on a plain field: what it is, its silhouette, its construction (geometry, proportion, weight, terminals, negative space), the one feature that makes it ownable, and how it sits with the name. Specific enough that two skilled designers would draw recognizably related marks.
  - **Idea:** one sentence, with the true thing and the recognition it relies on.
  - **Language:** lettering voice, color behavior, shape logic, imagery stance, signatures, constants and flex, each in a clause naming the identity-brief item it serves.
  - **Against the default territory:** how it is plainly distinct, and which of the audience's codes it keeps. **Risk and move.**

## 5. The brand review
Run on each candidate, and again on the chosen record. Each question answers yes or names a move.
1. **Distinct:** is the silhouette recognizable by form alone and plainly different from the peers' and the category's habitual marks?
2. **Standalone:** does the mark work alone, with no name, in one color, and small, and does it stand clear of anything around it?
3. **Embodies:** does each trait in the trait-to-form map have its carrier in the form, and does the mark fail the swap test?
4. **One logic:** do mark, lettering, color, and shapes follow one construction logic, as a family and not a symbol set beside generic type?
5. **Generative:** can the mark's language produce lettering, shapes, supporting graphics, and signatures without forcing them?
6. **Appeal and respect:** would the audience find it beautiful and trustworthy, and does it carry no unintended meaning, resemblance, or cultural reading?
7. **Dependability:** can the maker draw it consistently: few parts, no reliance on fine detail, exact geometry, or small lettering inside the mark?
8. **Not generic:** does it avoid the category's stock device, and is it more than a styled name?
**Gates:** Distinct, Standalone, Embodies, Dependability.

## 6. Choose, cards, record
Judge and choose by `choose.md` §4, with the added ranking criterion **Durable** (would it still serve this business across designs, years, and sizes?). Card:
```
**Step 3 of 5: brand identity**
What I learned: [one or two lines: who the business is, who it is for, what makes it distinctive]
| Identity | What you will see | Why / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **[name]**: [idea, ≤ 12 words] | [two or three sentences: the logo as seen alone, then the look around it] | [reason tied to this business] · [weakness] |
| [name] | ... | ... |
You said "…" → carried as: …
OK, or tell me what to change?
```
**The brand record:** the identity brief answered (one line) · the mark (narrated, or read from the asset): construction, lockup, behavior alone · the language: lettering voice, color identity, shape logic, imagery stance, signatures with scope · constants and flex · a because-clause per decision · the **handoff** to Theme: constants it must keep, flex it may use, what the identity needs the theme to achieve.

## Gate (adds to `choose.md`)
The identity brief was derived from the interpretation and every decision names its item · a supplied asset was read, not redesigned · the chosen mark passes the four gates and the swap test · the mark stands alone and the language is generated from it · the handoff separates constants from flex.
