# Phase 3a: envision and choose the picture

**Needs:** S; the Phase 2 record. **Produces:** the chosen picture, its runner-up and decisive criterion. Phase 3b (`4_picture_system.md`) develops it into the full direction. **Guided:** show the cards (§7) and stop for the choice; Phase 3b runs after it.

## 1. Frame
State the design problem in one sentence: "make [business] unmistakably itself and beautiful to [audience], in [context], so that they [response]", with the lead side of each tension and the intensity at which the user's qualities must show. Everything below answers that sentence.

## 2. A picture, not a device
A direction is a picture before it is a rationale. A good idea is what a viewer would tell a friend about the image: a hero in a situation, a way hero and content belong together, a world, a composition that does something. It is judged by how the finished image looks, never by how clever the premise is. An idea qualifies only if:
- the viewer need not learn its rationale for it to work;
- the maker renders it dependably (exact paths, exact counts, precise alignment, and fine repeated detail are rebuilt around what can be shown);
- it deepens the interplay of hero and content rather than adding a layer on top of them;
- it is a graphic or situational structure, never a material or an object-as-surface.
Under a fixed spine the idea may be modest: the spine realized at full craft, plus one integrated moment that links hero and content. A premise that competes with the first read or the user's look is eliminated.

## 3. Openness
Fixed: record, no cards; still derive the art direction for what the user left unsaid. Steered: explore the open remainder, at least 2 candidates, one of them the **spine candidate** (the user's stated direction at its best craft, no added premise). Open: at least 3. Volume and crowding never raise openness; they raise feasibility work. Never reduced: the prior read, the runner-up, the decisive criterion, and the judging in §6.

## 4. Build the candidates
1. **Select 2 to 4 seeds** from the raw material. A governing idea grows from the spine, the hero, or the glance message. Candidates differ on a **structural axis**: the composition architecture (what dominates, how hero and content interlock), the hero's situation, the image-making class and rendering ceiling, or how the information is organized. Color or ornament alone do not make candidates distinct. At most one stays close to the prior read.
2. **Develop each** in specialist terms (a label is not a candidate):
   - **Picture:** four to six sentences narrating the finished image whole: the first read; how the hero and the main lettering relate; the zones in plain words where each group lives; what sits in front and behind; where quiet stays open; what the ground does. A candidate that cannot be narrated is not yet a candidate.
   - **Idea:** one picture-level sentence, with **the true thing** it rests on and the recognition it relies on.
   - **Look:** lineage and the logic adopted, and the rendering ceiling (`4_picture_system.md` §2).
   - **Ownable traits:** one to three recurring, drawable devices.
   - **Against the prior read:** what it raises, what it fixes, what it keeps.
   - **Risk and move.**
3. **Engineer before judging.** Run the picture review (§5) on each candidate and repair it: where it is plain against the bar or the user's intensity, add richness through organization; where it is noisy, simplify or regroup; where the hero and text are stacked, interlock them; where a part needs what the maker cannot deliver, rebuild it around what can be shown. Express an idea as graphic structure, never as a simulated physical thing, unless that thing is the picture's subject.

## 5. The picture review
One instrument, run on a narrated picture here, on the assembled direction in `4_picture_system.md` §3, and on the brief in `5_compose_brief.md` §4. Each question answers yes or names a move.
1. **First read:** exactly one dominant element, with a decisive contrast (scale, value, chroma, or form), still dominant when squinted at from the farthest distance?
2. **Scale ladder:** do group sizes step by tier with clear differences, competing near-equals only where equality is the point?
3. **Interlock:** do the hero and the main lettering belong to one composition (overlap, shared contour, gesture or gaze leading into content) rather than sitting as stacked blocks?
4. **Depth and richness:** are the layers deliberate; is density richest at the first read, organized around it, with calm places so richness reads as richness? Does the whole, ground included, carry each user quality at its intensity?
5. **Rhythm and grouping:** are gaps between groups larger than gaps within; do repeated units share one design; is there a route from the first read to the action, led by natural cues?
6. **Color and value:** does the value key support every quality, does the strongest chroma sit on the first read, and are text-to-ground contrasts safe?
7. **Lettering as designed objects:** are the headline, name, and prices built as lockups of the same world as the hero, not generic type set beside it?
8. **Dependability:** does any element need what the maker cannot deliver reliably, and does any exclusion name something the maker would not add unprompted?

## 6. Judge and choose
1. **Eliminate** any candidate that contradicts a quality, a mandatory item, the conflict priority, or the spine. **First read, Dependability, and quality intensity gate:** a candidate failing any is out whatever its conceptual strength. If every candidate fails a gate, rebuild once from another seed, then take the spine candidate at its best craft.
2. **Compare with the prior read:** the leader must beat it on a named, visible dimension (composition architecture, hero design and situation, interplay, lettering, richness and depth, color behavior, structure of information). A candidate that differs only in adjectives, or in an idea the finished image would not show, or that a stranger could guess from the category and place, is the prior: rebuild once from another seed, or take the spine candidate.
3. **Rank the survivors** where they differ: **Appeal** (would this audience stop, smile, want it?), **Ownable** (with the name removed, does it still point to this business, and does it fail the swap test?), **Memorable** (recallable in one sentence), **Coherent** (every part follows from the idea), **Truthful** (every claim, symbol, and depiction true and respectful).
4. **Challenge once:** name the runner-up (a candidate you would ship, never a weak foil) and the strongest case against the leader. The margin is one **decisive criterion** naming an observable property of the finished image, not of the concept or of a business fact. If none can be stated, the candidates tie: take the simpler and record the tie.
5. **Engineer:** each material weakness gets a named move or an accepted cost; each key strength a place where it is amplified. Select, transplant a strength, repair, or combine as a **hybrid** (statable as one idea); a compromise needing "and also" is rejected.
6. **Record:** the picture, idea, ownable traits, runner-up, decisive criterion, what was kept from the others, obligations.

## 7. Guided entry (cards)
Show only the choice, its reason, and the alternatives, in the user's language, skimmable in seconds, with no internal terms for a new user.
```
**Step 3 of 4: creative direction**
What I learned: [one or two lines: the business's truth, the audience's eye, how it can stand out]
| Direction | What you will see | Why / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **[name]**: [idea, ≤ 12 words] | [two sentences narrating the picture, incl. the ownable trait] | [reason tied to this business] · [weakness] |
| [name] | ... | ... |
You said "…" → carried as: …
OK, or tell me what to change?
```
Two or three rows, the chosen one first; matters already fixed by the user go in one line "Already settled: …". Feedback: acknowledge in one line, rerun what it touches, show only changed rows as Before | After, ask once; after two rounds without agreement choose the best resolution, say so, continue. A question is answered in one or two plain sentences, then the confirmation line repeats. Feedback contradicting a stated quality or fact: say so kindly and offer the closest option keeping both.

## Gate (fails if any is false)
Every open decision shows its prior read, runner-up, and decisive criterion · candidates were narrated whole, run through the picture review, and differ on a structural axis · the leader passed the gates and beats the prior read on a named visible dimension · every weakness has a move or an accepted cost · the idea fits one sentence and carries one to three ownable traits. Then proceed to `4_picture_system.md`.
