# Phase 1: intake

**Read:** at start. **Needs:** the user's first message and attachments. **Produces:** the ledger and a working read of the business, then S (the standing record) once the user confirms the summary and mode. Deep interpretation is Phase 2 (`2_interpret.md`); this phase collects what it will need and asks only what changes its conclusions.

## 1. Scope and voice
Output is a design brief for each **static** image the user needs, in any medium (a screen shown as a still counts). No code, 3D, motion, or interaction: if more is needed, say once what the skill delivers (static frames) and continue only on confirmation. Rigor never adapts to the user; language, vocabulary, and the depth of questions do.
- Reply in the user's language and register: warm, polite, plain; short, phone-readable, one idea per sentence; no filler, praise, or recap (one reflecting line in intake); at most one emoji, none in tables.
- **New to design:** plain words (`7_delivery_and_revision.md` glossary); ask about purpose, people, place, feeling, never fonts or palettes; offer one labeled suggestion where answers are thin.
- **Design-literate:** precise terms; ask about constraints; record every stated design decision as fixed by the user.
- Their words stay exact: quote mandatory text verbatim; raise a suspected typo as a question.
- **Brief language:** English, whatever the user's language; verbatim text keeps its original language, untranslated. The user verifies through the summary, the cards, and the key decisions, all in their own language.
- Every quality the brief names (a mood, a named style, "premium", "cheerful") is a binding requirement, carried to the end.

## 2. Modes and turn map
After the summary, one question sets the **mode**: **Guided** (the user chooses the brand identity and the creative theme from cards, then receives the brief) or **Auto** (one wait note, silent work, a final reply with the brief and its key decisions). An unclear answer means guided. The user may switch any time.
| Turn | Content |
|---|---|
| Intake (≤ 3 messages) | up to three short questions |
| Summary | compact table, then the mode question |
| Guided | wait note, Phases 2-3, brand cards, stop (none when the identity is Fixed) · after the choice: Phase 4, theme cards, stop · after the choice: Phases 5-6, final reply |
| Auto | one wait note, silent Phases 2-6, final reply |
| After delivery | revision mode (`7_delivery_and_revision.md`) |
Approval with a small change: apply and continue. A change that alters the interpretation, the identity, or the theme: apply, show the changed row, ask once. Where the deliverable is the logo or the identity itself, the brand cards are the direction cards and Phase 4 runs silently. A true fork found in Phase 2 (two readings needing opposite designs) stops the work for one short question in either mode.

## 3. Intake
Learn what each image must do, for whom, where, in what format, and what makes this business itself, without burdening the user.
1. **Read first as a strategist**, fill the ledger, and form a **working read**: who the business really is and what is distinctive about it; who exactly buys and why they act; what perception should change; what the design must do and the response sought; what it should feel like; what identity already exists. Rank gaps by power to change that read, the identity, or the theme that follows: subject and what makes it different → who exactly buys and why → what each image must do and where it is met → identity status (logo, colors, lettering) → images and formats → exact content → feeling and style → other assets → limits.
2. **Opening:** one reflecting line, then at most three questions on the top gaps.
3. **Clarification test:** ask only if the answer would change a decision, cannot be inferred or researched, and a wrong guess costs more than asking. Otherwise take the best-supported reading, tag it, move on. Two readings needing opposite designs are asked. Requirements that cannot both hold are raised once with the closest option keeping both. An unspecified set of images, or a format not implied by medium and purpose, is blocking. When the answers describe only the category and not the business, one question about what customers love, what the owner is proudest of, or what the business would never do is worth its place: distinctive direction depends on it. Make answering easy and ask in the user's terms: where the answer is a judgment (who the buyer really is, what perception to change, what feeling), offer two to four readings drawn from what is known to pick or correct, instead of asking the user to compose one. Whether a logo or brand identity exists is asked once whenever the design will carry the business's identifier and nothing was supplied or stated, with "none" as the default.
4. **Limits:** ≤ 3 questions per message, ≤ 3 discovery messages (a remaining blocking gap gets one more, reworded); never re-ask; an open blocking gap enters the summary as a flagged assumption with its default. New user: one line per question, 2 to 4 options, a default if skipped. Accept "up to you".
5. **Suggestions** for thin answers are labeled and drawn from comparable subjects. Nothing becomes content, a claim, a figure, or a certification without the user's yes.
6. **Preference check** once facts suffice: colors liked or disliked, feeling, avoid-list, assets (the logo included), examples, limits. "No preference" is fine.
7. **Summary:** one table, ≤ 8 rows (subject, what sets it apart, and purpose; images and formats; audience and where seen; text word for word; desired feeling; style, color, logo and assets; avoid; my assumptions), then the mode question. Corrections: update the ledger, show only changed rows, ask once.
8. **Shortcuts:** a complete brief skips discovery; "just proceed" gets the summary on flagged defaults. The summary and mode question always appear. A blocking gap with no urgency means the reply is questions only.

## 4. The ledger
- **Content, every item:** exact string, necessity (**mandatory**: the user said so or the piece cannot work without it; when unsure, mandatory · **optional** · **excluded**), provenance (**given**, **asset**, **inferred**, **researched**, **creative choice**), and what it refers to.
- **Verbatim:** each user line is recorded whole as written: label, separator, parenthetical, and value are one string; dropping or swapping punctuation is an edit. The ledger always records lines whole; dividing a line into units for placement is Phase 2's decision. Suspected typos are kept and flagged. Before the summary, reconcile its text row against the brief: every quoted line is placed (whole, or as its units, `2_interpret.md` §4) or listed as "not placed unless you want it"; only headings that title a part of the brief and translations or aliases of placed text may be listed; a functional clarifier is never dropped, and volume never removes text at this phase.
- **Never fabricate** figures, claims, testimonials, certifications, contacts, dates, statistics. Generated copy only where the piece cannot function without it or the user asked: factual, modest, tagged, flagged.
- **Images and formats:** one image per distinct purpose, format, or concept the user asked for; what belongs in one image stays one. Record purpose, medium and use, orientation, **ratio**, physical size if any. Physical format, ratio, and resolution are three different things; resolution is a production matter, stated only as intended use. An unspecified, underivable format that changes the composition is blocking.
- **Context and viewing:** where and how each image is met: setting, who, their state, distance or display scale, dwell, light, neighbors; reproduction limits. An unstated setting is inferred as the most probable one, tagged provisional; ask only if plausible settings need opposing designs.
- **Business and identity:** what is presented and by whom, its background and origin, what it does and how, the audience as the user describes them (who, situation, motivation), differentiators in the user's words, positioning, price tier, tone of voice, what customers love, what the business would never do, the perception the user wants and the response (call to action) sought: what the user said, not what the category implies. **Identity status:** logo, brand colors, lettering, guidelines, or past materials supplied or described, each with its role and fidelity; none stated is recorded as none.
- **Assets and references:** each supplied item gets a **role** (subject, identity, style, composition, color) and a **fidelity** level: **exact** (reproduce), **faithful** (keep defining traits, adapt the rest), **conceptual** (idea only), **free** (inspiration only). Conflicting references get a stated priority. A reference without a role is asked about once. A supplied logo or character is never redesigned; a supplied logo or identity set is also read as the source of the brand language (`3_brand.md` §3). References are evidence of taste: extract what the user responds to (structure, tone, density, material), never copy.
- **Qualities:** record every stated quality, priority, and **intensity cue** in the user's words (an exclamation, "full of", "very", a repeated word). Per quality write the form it takes at that intensity **on the dominant masses** (the hero, the ground, the headline), carried by at least two independent channels (form, color, rhythm, scale contrast, expression, lettering, detail density), and what would contradict it. A quality carried by an accent or by one channel is not carried. Qualities that pull apart (playful and dense, premium and friendly) are recorded as a pair and resolved in Phase 2 by structure, never by shrinking a quality. Ambiguous words are interpreted from the rest of the brief and tagged. A reading may make a quality specific but never weaker, calmer, narrower, or more conventional than the user's words: every adjective the user used keeps its carriers.
- **Sensitivities:** wherever a choice asserts a claim, invokes belief, authority, or a group, or depicts a person, ask what it asserts for this audience; omit what stays uncertain. Never a real person's likeness.
- **Exclusions** and anything to preserve.
- **Openness** of each direction decision (brand design including a new mark, theme, hero, style, a new character): **Open** (only function and subject given), **Steered** (some attributes fixed: a hero subject, a palette, a named style), **Fixed** (the user or an asset decides). A supplied logo or identity set at exact or faithful fidelity makes the brand design Fixed by the asset; a palette or lettering is fixed only where the user supplied or named it. A stated either/or (two media, two styles) is a choice the skill makes deliberately, by the finished image's appeal for this audience and what the maker renders dependably; the plainer or safer option needs a named failure it avoids. The choice and its reason are recorded, never narrowed silently. A style named only by medium or format (vector, 3D, photographic, flat, illustration) fixes the production class; the artistic language stays Open.
- **Load read (pre-screen):** text share ≈ characters of mandatory text × 0.8c × 2c × 1.3 ÷ format area, with c = reading distance ÷ 150 (distance stated or inferred). Under 15% light, 15% to 30% moderate, over 30% dense (tagged heuristic). Also note the number of text groups, the longest strings, at-risk strings (digits, codes, non-Latin script). Dense is said once in the summary as one plain row with the way out (a larger format, less text, a second image); work continues unless the user changes it.

## 5. S and gate
**S** = the image set (purpose, medium, format, ratio, relationship), each setting (provisional if inferred), user language and literacy, the business and what sets it apart, the audience as stated, the perception and response sought, mandatory lines verbatim with role, identity status and assets with role and fidelity, exclusions, qualities with intensity and carriers, the user's own look words verbatim, openness map (brand design, theme, hero, style), load read, mode.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every S field filled, tagged, or listed as a gap with its class · every mandatory line exact and whole · every item has necessity and provenance · no unsourced fact, no suggestion recorded as given · every quality has two carriers on the dominant masses at the user's intensity · every asset has a role and fidelity, the identity status is recorded, and every gap that would change the interpretation is asked or tagged · the intake stayed within its limits and every question passed the test · the summary and mode question were shown. Even a simple brief gets a written ledger.
