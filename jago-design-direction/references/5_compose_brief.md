# Phase 4: compose the brief

**Needs:** S, the Phase 2 record, the direction record. **Produces:** one English design brief per image, then the final reply (`6_delivery_and_revision.md`). Composing is translation: if it needs a new idea, return to Phase 3.
The **maker** produces the image from the brief alone, with no access to the analysis, the alternatives, or the reasoning. The brief carries everything that gives the design its identity and quality, stated as decisions, and nothing the maker would decide as well.

## 1. Sort every decision by owner
Walk S and the direction record once. Each decision lands in one class and is stated once.
| Class | What belongs | In the brief |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** (user) | facts, exact text, assets with fidelity, named preferences, palette, format, exclusions, what must be preserved | **Requirements**: precise, "must", exact quotes |
| **Decided** (skill) | the idea as a visual result, art direction, hero, signature moves with scope, color roles, lettering voice, hierarchy and grouping relations, supporting visuals, boundaries, a new mark's device | **Direction**: firm statements of what the image is and does |
| **Open** (maker) | placement and composition detail, sizes, spacing, type pairing, lighting setup, decorative and finish detail | omitted; never listed, never handed back |
**Necessity test** (every Decided item): if left open, would the maker's likely choice harm the objective, a stated quality, the truth of the content, or the quality or distinctiveness of the image? If not, omit it. If yes, state the least that prevents the harm: the intent and the traits that make it recognizable. A skill decision is never worded as the user's requirement.
A matter only the user can resolve is asked in Phase 1 or settled by a flagged default: stated firmly in the brief, flagged in the reply's notes. The brief never carries an unresolved item.

## 2. Anatomy
Outcome first, requirements second, direction third. Plain English headings; include only what applies.
```
Request       Create a [kind of image] for [business] to [purpose]. [Orientation, ratio]; [where and how it is seen, from what distance].
Text          Each mandatory line whole, in quotes, with role and tier (reads at distance / while approaching / up close).
              "Use exactly this text. Add, translate, or correct nothing, and write no other text."
Context       Audience and the response the image must trigger; the user's qualities at their own intensity (two sentences at most).
Requirements  Assets and fidelity; palette; stated styles and hero traits; exclusions; what to preserve.
Direction     Idea as its visual result (one sentence) · art direction (lineage, construction, line, color and rendering, surface, light)
              · hero · color roles · lettering voice · reading order and groups · signature moves with scope · supporting visuals.
Avoid         Short list of costly, likely failures, as positive opposites where possible.
Close         "Decide every unstated detail with craft judgment. Ask no questions; invent no facts, text, or decoration without a purpose."
```
- Direction leads with the idea and the art direction, so the maker can restate the idea in one sentence and render the look consistently.
- **Core** (Request to Requirements) is always present. **Precision** (reference map, fidelity, tiers, density note) appears only when the task needs control.
- A simple, complete request needs only a few sentences plus the direction's essentials.

## 3. Writing rules
1. **Voice:** English; the user's request to the maker, in natural prose with short lists; no process commentary; a reason only as one clause where a reading could go wrong.
2. **Decisive:** state decisions as facts about the image. No "prefer", "ideally", "try", "could", "may", "consider", "if possible", "adapt if better". A non-decision is omitted. Conditionals only for real branches.
3. **State the look, not the metaphor.** Convert every concept into its visual consequence. Never ask the image to be an object or a material; a figurative premise can be rendered literally. Name a material or finish only when it is wanted in the picture.
4. **Positive wording.** Describe what success looks like; keep a short list of real exclusions and of likely, costly failures (extra text, invented marks, the generic look the boundaries name).
5. **Relations, not placement.** State the order of attention, groups by job, what belongs with the hero, where space stays open. No coordinates, regions, millimeters, pixels, or layout percentages. Permitted numbers: format and ratio, viewing distances, counts that matter, relative statements.
6. **Verbatim block:** every mandatory line whole, punctuation included. Line breaks are the maker's; the skill splits a string only where the user did.
7. **Density note** (dense verdict only): "Text-heavy. Group it into a few clear clusters, keep every line, keep [core items] comfortably readable from [distance], let [reference items] be smaller, never distort letters to fit." No sizes.
8. **References and assets:** a reference map (purpose, what to take, which wins on conflict) and each asset's fidelity; a supplied logo or character is reproduced, never redesigned.
9. **Specificity:** depicted things by their true specifics, not category names; art direction by lineage and construction traits, never by medium words or mood adjectives alone. Color by named hue with role and intensity.
10. **Once only:** one statement per decision, no restating the user's text outside the verbatim block, no stacked adjectives, no defensive negations unless the failure is likely. A quality appears once, at the user's intensity.
11. **Length follows need:** typically 200 to 350 words besides the verbatim block; hard cap 450. Over the cap, cut in this order: Decided items failing the necessity test, then secondary context; never Fixed.
12. **Several images:** one standalone brief each; the shared direction is repeated word for word; each names its own format and purpose and never refers to another brief.
13. **Editing a supplied image:** state what changes, what must stay identical, where, and how the change integrates.

## 4. Review before delivery (once)
1. **Baseline check:** write in two sentences what the maker would produce from the user's words alone. Every addition in the brief must improve that result (a specific lineage and axes, a designed hero, a signature move, a content structure); a sentence that would not change the image is cut. A brief that is the baseline with more adjectives returns to Phase 3.
2. **Render forecast:** read the brief as the maker. For each phrase about material, finish, object, light, or metaphor, state what will literally appear; rewrite or remove any that yields unintended texture, an object or mockup look, or a clash with another phrase or a stated quality (matte against glossy, clean against textured).
3. **Meaning preserved:** every ledger requirement is present at its priority and the user's intensity; none weakened; nothing Decided reads as the user's requirement.
4. **Necessity:** each Decided sentence survives the test in §1; the rest is cut.
5. **Coherence:** no contradiction, no competing adjectives, no decision stated twice; exactly one first read; hierarchy claims match the relations stated.
6. **Verbatim:** every string matches the ledger's whole line, punctuation included; no extra text is invited.
7. **Truth:** no invented fact, claim, contact, date, or mark; no placeholder.
8. **Form:** English; no hedges; no list of open decisions; no layout numbers; standalone; within the length rule; tiers and density note consistent with the verdict.
A failure returns to the earliest responsible decision (a missing requirement to Phase 1, a weak direction to Phase 3).

## 5. Level of prescription
The default is Fixed and Decided as sorted above. After a result is reviewed (`6_delivery_and_revision.md`), move one notch at a time and only on evidence: **tighten** a trait that was lost (state it more explicitly), or **relax** items that stiffened the result (drop Decided items that did not earn their place, least consequential first). Record the setting.
