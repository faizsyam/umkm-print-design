# Phase 4: compose the brief

**Needs:** S, the Phase 2 record, the direction record. **Produces:** one design brief per image, then the final reply (`6_delivery_and_revision.md`). Composing is translation: if it needs a new idea, return to Phase 3.
The **maker** is whoever produces the image and receives the brief alone. It has no access to the analysis, the alternatives, or the reasoning, so the brief must carry everything that gives the design its identity, and nothing the maker would decide as well.

## 1. Sort every decision by owner
Walk S and the direction record once. Each decision lands in one class and is stated once, in that class's form.
| Class | What belongs | Stated as |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | the user's facts, exact text, assets (with fidelity), named preferences, format, exclusions, what must be preserved | a requirement, precise and complete: "must", exact quotes |
| **Directed** | the idea, signature moves, visual language, hero truth, hierarchy and grouping relations, boundaries, a new mark's device | intent plus the defining traits, labeled as decided direction: "follow this idea; the execution is yours" |
| **Recommended** | color roles, lettering voice, balance, supporting-visual stance | a preference with latitude: "prefer…; adapt if a better solution serves the same aim" |
| **Open** | placement and composition detail, sizes, spacing, type pairing, lighting setup, decorative treatment and count, finish detail | named in one line, or omitted |
**Necessity test** (every Directed and Recommended item): if this were left open, would the maker's likely choice harm the objective, a stated quality, the truth of the content, or the distinctiveness of the design? If not, delegate it. If yes, state the least that prevents the harm: the intent and the traits that make it recognizable, never an exhaustive description. A skill proposal is never worded as the user's requirement: the user can tell what they decided from what was proposed.

## 2. Anatomy
Outcome first, constraints second, direction third. Use natural headings in the user's language; include only what applies.
```
[Request]    Create a [kind of image] for [business] to [purpose]. Format: [orientation, ratio]; [where it is used].
[Must appear] The exact text, in quotes, each line with its role and reading tier. Nothing else is written.
[Context]    Who it is for, where and from what distance it is seen, what surrounds it, what it must achieve.
[Impression] What it should feel and communicate, in the user's qualities; add what each looks like only where it could be misread.
[Fixed]      Assets and their fidelity; reference roles and priorities; exclusions; what to preserve.
[Direction]  The idea and why it suits this business (two sentences); the signature moves; the visual language (traits);
             the hero; color logic; lettering voice; what leads and how content groups; supporting visuals allowed;
             what the piece must never become, as positives.
[Open]       What the maker decides: composition and placement, sizes, spacing, type pairing, lighting, decorative detail.
[How to work] Understand the objective before choosing visuals. Explicit requirements outrank the decided direction, which outranks
             defaults; anything open is yours. Invent no facts, text, or decoration without a purpose. Do not ask questions: decide, then make.
```
- The Direction block leads with the idea and the signature moves, so the maker can restate the idea in one sentence and apply the moves everywhere.
- **Core** (Request to Fixed) is always present. **Precision** (reference map, fidelity, tiers, grouping, density note) appears only when the task needs control. A Fixed direction decision moves to the Fixed section.
- A simple, complete request needs only a few sentences plus the direction's essentials.

## 3. Writing rules
1. **Voice:** the user's request to the maker, in natural prose with short lists; no commentary about the process, no rationale beyond the two sentences in the Direction block and one clause where a reading could go wrong.
2. **Intent before execution.** A quality carries a visible form only where the word is ambiguous; never stack adjectives.
3. **Positive wording.** Describe what success looks like; keep a short list of real exclusions and of failures that are likely and costly (extra text, invented marks, the generic look the boundaries name).
4. **Relations, not placement.** State the order of attention (first read, second, third), groups by job, what belongs with the hero, where space stays open. No coordinates, regions, millimeters, pixels, or layout percentages. Permitted numbers: the format and ratio, viewing distances, counts that matter (exactly two offers), and relative statements ("prices read second, after the headline").
5. **Verbatim block:** every mandatory line whole, in quotes, with role and tier (reads at distance / read while approaching / read up close), then "use exactly this text; add, translate, or correct nothing". Line breaks are the maker's; the skill splits a string only where the user did.
6. **Density note** (only for a dense verdict): "This is text-heavy. Group it into a few clear clusters, keep every line, keep [core items] comfortably readable from [distance], let [reference items] be smaller, and never distort letters to fit." No sizes.
7. **References and assets:** a reference map (what each is for, what to take, which wins on conflict) and each asset's fidelity; a supplied logo or character is reproduced, never redesigned.
8. **Specificity:** depicted things by their true specifics, not category names; lineage and traits, not medium words. Color by named hue with its role and intensity, never a hue list alone.
9. **Length follows need.** The brief grows through verbatim content and real constraints, not through added direction. Typically 250 to 500 words besides the verbatim block; beyond about 650, cut Recommended first, then Directed, never Fixed.
10. **Several images:** one standalone brief each; the shared direction is repeated word for word; each names its own format and purpose and never refers to another brief.
11. **Editing a supplied image:** state what changes, what must stay identical, where, and how the change integrates.

## 4. Review before delivery (once)
1. **Baseline check:** write in two sentences what the maker would produce from the user's words alone. The brief must visibly change at least three things (form logic, color behavior, composition logic, imagery mode, lettering voice, information structure) and name the signature moves. If the brief reads like the baseline with more adjectives, return to Phase 3.
2. **Meaning preserved:** every ledger requirement is present with its priority; none downgraded; nothing Directed or Recommended reads as the user's requirement.
3. **Necessity:** each Directed and Recommended sentence survives the test in §1; the rest is cut or moved to Open.
4. **Coherence:** no contradiction, no competing adjectives, no decision stated twice; exactly one first read; every claim about hierarchy matches the relations stated.
5. **Verbatim:** every string matches the ledger's whole line, punctuation included; no extra text is invited.
6. **Truth:** no invented fact, claim, contact, date, or mark; no placeholder.
7. **Form:** no layout numbers; standalone; within the length rule; the reading tiers and the density note are consistent with the verdict.
A failure returns to the earliest responsible decision (a missing requirement to Phase 1, a weak direction to Phase 3).

## 5. Level of prescription
The default is Fixed, Directed, and Recommended as sorted above. After a result is reviewed (`6_delivery_and_revision.md`), move one notch at a time and only on evidence: **tighten** a trait that was lost (state it more explicitly), or **relax** items that stiffened the result (drop Recommended, then Directed items that did not earn their place). Record the setting.
