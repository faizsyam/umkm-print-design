# Phase 3b: the direction as a system

**Needs:** the chosen idea and its record; S; the Phase 2 record. **Produces:** the **direction record**: idea, art direction (forces, lineage, axes), hero, signature moves, color logic, lettering voice, hierarchy and grouping relations, supporting-visual stance, identity decisions, boundaries, point of tension, and how each tension is carried.

## 1. The chain
A distinctive direction is one logic seen everywhere. Develop the parts in this order; each states in one clause **because of what** it is so, and each reads the ones before it. A part that cannot name its reason is a default and is replaced.
**idea → art direction → hero and imagery → signature moves → color logic → lettering voice → hierarchy and grouping → supporting visuals → identity → boundaries**
The idea sets the premise; the forces in §2 set the language; the idea is realized through the language, never the reverse. Where they conflict, re-express or drop the idea before bending the language. The art direction governs the rest: color follows the rendering mode, lettering shares the carrier's construction logic, composition follows how the lineage treats space, identity follows its form language. Test the chain once at the end: pick any part and predict the next from the idea and the parts before it; if the prediction fails, the part does not belong. The direction states what it fixes; the maker decides what it does not (§4).

## 2. The parts
- **Art direction** (the most consequential decision for any image-led piece; derived, never merely named):
  1. *Carrier:* what the eye reads first (a character, a photograph, a pattern, the lettering). Its language is built first; everything else conforms to it.
  2. *Forces:* the audience's visual world (what they already love, trust, and read fluently); the business's tier and promise (craft level, price signal); each mood quality turned into form consequences (shape, proportion, rhythm, contrast, saturation, line behavior); the carrier's job (what it must make the viewer feel or understand); the reproduction (scale, distance, process, what the making tool renders dependably).
  3. *Lineage:* the specific tradition, discipline, or school, as narrow as era, region, and technique allow, whose internal logic answers those forces. State what is adopted from its logic (how it simplifies, builds form, uses color and space, treats detail) and what is left behind. One lineage leads; a second may govern one axis under a stated rule; a pile of unrelated looks fails. A medium or format word (vector, 3D, photographic, flat) is a production class, not a lineage. Never a living artist, studio, or protected character; a lineage is used with its logic understood, not as a surface.
  4. *Axes:* form construction (geometry, proportion, simplification, silhouette); line (presence, weight behavior, terminals, color); color and rendering (flat, tonal, or modeled; shadow logic; saturation and temperature relations; the **value key**); surface (graphic marks only; no simulated material unless that material is the picture's subject); light; detail by rank (the carrier fullest, the rest stepping down).
  5. *Tests:* **Strike** (remove medium and format words and bare mood adjectives; a drawable look remains) · **Kinship** (two skilled makers given the paragraph would produce recognizably related work) · **Coherence** (every axis follows from the lineage's logic; none cancels another) · **Home turf** (it sits in the visual world the audience already finds appealing, at the craft level the tier requires) · **Mood** (each mood quality has a form-level carrier) · **Tool** (the maker can render it dependably; uncontrollable fine detail sits only where an error is harmless; a concretely described rich look usually renders well, so complexity alone never justifies the plainer choice).
- **Hero and imagery:** what the hero truly is, down to its specific features; the depiction mode (photographic, illustrated, constructed, typographic, abstract) follows what must be true: where the viewer must see exactly what is offered, a mode that stylizes it away is ineligible. A hero is chosen only when one subject deserves primary attention and can be depicted truthfully; otherwise there is none, or a small equal set. A figure (mascot, character) is decided at identity level: kind, character, three to five distinctive features, proportions, consistency across images; its proportions, simplification, and rendering are the art direction's output, not separate choices. A supplied figure is never redesigned, and a real likeness is never used. Depict the specific thing, never a generic glyph interchangeable between subjects.
- **Signature moves (one to three):** recurring devices that make the piece recognizable and ownable, derived from the idea and the true specifics, not from the category's stock devices. Each is a graphic device (a form logic, a color behavior, a lettering treatment, a structural gesture, a use of the content's own shape), never a simulated material or object-as-surface, and each states its scope: where it appears and where it never does (never across mandatory text or the first read unless that is the move). A move applied everywhere is a filter, not a move. Everything else supports the moves and stays quieter. More than three dilute the identity.
- **Color logic:** state what color does here (organize, signal, separate, evoke, depart), then roles and rough proportions (a dominant field, a supporting field, an accent, an ink for text), harmony, temperature, and the **value key**: the light or dark balance by area, ground included. Dark masses make a low-key piece however light the accents, so the key must support every quality. The strongest chroma goes to the first read; area is not prominence. Colors the user named are fixed; text color is chosen for legibility; hex only for supplied brand colors.
- **Lettering voice:** what the lettering says about this subject, built from the art direction's logic and the lettering traditions in the pool, never the category's stock face. Roles that must differ (display, informational, numerals, small print) differ by more than size: construction, weight, width, posture, case, spacing. Items of one role share one treatment whatever their length. Letters are never compressed, stretched, or slanted to fit. No font names unless a brand requires one.
- **Hierarchy and grouping** (the one composition decision made beforehand): exactly one first read at glance, then the order; groups formed by job and referent, with what labels, prices, or explains a depicted thing kept with it; what belongs with the hero; where quiet stays open and what it separates; the balance stance and its counterweight; the reading route. Relations only, never placement. When content is dense, relief comes from structure and grouping, never from ornament.
- **Supporting visuals:** every element carries a purpose (its function, what is lost without it, its effect); two with one function merge. Enrich before adding: deepen the hero and lettering, then link major elements, then decided atmosphere, then small supporters. Detect defaults: an element present only because pieces of this kind have it is replaced by a specific one or removed; ornament that merely restates the category needs a reason rooted in the idea. Count resources by kind, never by theme: dense content allows up to 2 supporter families (pattern or texture, atmosphere effects, edge ornament, dividers, spot illustration), 3 container designs, 3 rendering-treatment kinds (outline, shadow, gradient, texture, highlight, dimension); moderate 4, 4, 3; light 6, 5, 4. A stated quality of energy, richness, abundance, or warmth raises every cap one level and is then expressed through designed, purposeful elements; plainness that contradicts a stated quality is a failure, not a decision. Exceed the caps only by naming the deficit that earns it and the family dropped to pay for it.
- **Containers and grouping devices:** the lightest device first (spacing and alignment, then a rule or tonal step, then a container); a container needs a reason the lighter ones fail. One shape language (corner and edge geometry, material impression) shared by all containers, rules, and supporters; containers holding different roles differ at a glance; equal items share one design. The default form of the category is the baseline: it wins only by naming the criterion the alternatives failed.
- **Identity:** a supplied logo or wordmark is exact. A new one is decided as an **idea**: an ownable, grounded device tying the name to the business's truth (never a styled name alone), weighed against one alternative; its construction is left to the maker, its letters stay complete and in order, and it is distinct from the surrounding lettering by form, color role, scale, or isolation. A device that puts a mandatory string's accuracy at risk sits beside the letters, not in them. If the hero or idea already fixes the form, only the device is open.
- **Boundaries:** what the piece must never become: failures the user would also call failures (muddy, cluttered, generic, off-mood), taken from the baseline's weaknesses and common defaults, never an element or treatment the user's own words could welcome; each later stated as a positive opposite.
- **Point of tension:** at most one deliberate break from the piece's own system, only where it lowers reading effort or raises delight; none when the idea already supplies the unexpected or the qualities call for calm.
- **Tensions:** confirm each lead side against the assembled direction and record what the other side keeps.

## 3. Direction check (once)
Do the parts reinforce one another, and do accepted costs compound? Is the chain unbroken? Does the direction answer the weakest link in the real setting, hold with the name removed, and beat the baseline in the finished image, each departure naming its gain? Does every quality have a visible carrier at the user's intensity, and does any decision signal its opposite? Does anything read as a literal material or object where a graphic form was meant? Can the content hierarchy be carried? Is there exactly one first read? Are the signature moves present in the hero, the lettering, and the supporting system, within their scopes? Does anything fixed here belong to the maker (§4)? A conflict returns to the responsible part; two cycles at most.

## 4. What the direction leaves to the maker
Placement and composition detail, sizes, spacing, type pairing within the voice, lighting setup, the position and count of decorative or supporting elements, finish detail below the signature moves. The direction fixes intent and defining traits, never a specification.

## Gate (fails if any is false)
Every part names its reason and the chain holds · the art direction names its carrier, forces, lineage with adopted logic, and axes, and passes its six tests · one to three signature moves recur within their scopes · the hero depicts only what is true · the value key supports every quality · exactly one first read · supporting resources are within the caps, counted by kind, with no default fill and no plainness against a quality · every quality is carried and every tension carried · the direction beats the baseline in the finished image · nothing is fixed that belongs to the maker. Then append the record.
