# Phase 2 (continued): problem, audience's pull, raw material, prior read, mandates

**Needs:** the reads and strategic core (`2_interpret.md`). **Produces:** the rest of the interpretation: the communication problem (encounter, tiers, content map, feasibility), the audience's pull and perception bar, the raw material, the prior read, and the mandates, handoff, and gate for the whole phase. Section numbers continue from `2_interpret.md`.

## 4. Communication problem, encounter, feasibility
**Encounter and tiers.** One profile per distinct context; images sharing a context share it. Test the Phase 1 inference; derive from how the image is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name; if unestablished, state the most probable and mark it provisional.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' color, density, scale; competing stimuli), the audience's state (goal, motion, attention, time, distance, light, culture), the reading path. Hold the **impression** (what the viewer should feel and conclude) constant; the setting sets its expression. These are facts about the encounter; how the concept answers them is Theme's context fit (`4_theme.md` §2).
- **Reading tiers** (derived, not assumed three): **glance** (what a passing viewer takes in at the farthest distance), **approach**, **close**. Assign by when the viewer needs an item, never by whether it fits. Glance holds the hero and at most two short items in the order the message needs; the name or mark takes a slot only where recognition drives the action, otherwise the offer or occasion comes first. Reading size ≈ distance ÷ 150 for sustained lines, ÷ 115 for a passing glance (cap height, tagged assumption), for feasibility only. **Core content** (what the decision and action depend on: offer, choices, prices, every selling point) reads comfortably at its tier; only **reference content** (what a viewer seeks after deciding to act: contact details, hours, fine print) may read deliberately. A selling point is never reference content, however small or specification-like.

**The problem.**
- **Content map** (what belongs together, why, what role each group serves):
  1. **Units:** divide the ledger into the smallest units that each do one job: a name, its contents, its price; two claims joined by a separator; a label and its value. A unit keeps its words; dividing never alters them.
  2. **Classify** each unit by the question it answers for the viewer (what is this, why choose it, what do I get and pay, can I trust it, how do I act and where) and by whether it carries the distinctive truth or a stated differentiator: such a unit is a **selling point** whatever its type or where the user listed it, specifications included. The user's headings and adjacency are evidence of intended relationship: weigh them, override only with a stated reason.
  3. **Group** units that answer the same question about the same object and are read together. Units answering different questions never share a group for being small, low-ranked, or left over; one group per role, as many as roles need.
  4. **Rank and relate:** per group its purpose, units, internal hierarchy, volume, relation to the primary message (proposition, proof, offer, action), the depicted thing it must stay with, its **tier** (when it is read) and its **rank** (how much the objective depends on it: primary, supporting, or reference; equal where purposes are equal). Tier is not rank.
  5. **Data and labels:** an identifier (contact channel, handle, number, address, hours) divides into its **value** and any **clarifier** adding meaning (who or what it is for), both words, and its **label**, which names only the kind of data and is a candidate for a pictogram (`5_elements.md` §3).
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility, register); state both and the resource. **A tension between a quality and the load is resolved by structure (grouping, tiers, scale steps, a density note), never by lowering the quality.** Record the lead side only where the conflict priority (`SKILL.md`) decides, and what the other side keeps.
- **Weakest link:** attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs: the one most likely to fail here. Test the brief's own assumptions; one undermining the objective is a tension.

**Feasibility** (one pass, content-heavy images only). Can this content be read at this distance in this format? Estimate once for the most compact arrangement (a hero beside its own text, and equal units in columns, share one band; extent = the larger): each group's need = planned lines × pitch (≈ 1.6 × cap for display lines, ≈ 2 × cap for body text) at its tier's reading size, as a share of the long side, plus ≈ 15% for gaps; add the hero's minimum extent and 24% overhead (structure and decoration 5, quiet 8, margins and separations 8, headroom 3). Over 100% is **dense**, answered by structure, never by deleting mandatory content: (1) group and tier so core items lead and reference items recede, and let pictograms replace labels so words carry only what needs words; (2) simplify the hero's footprint before shrinking core content; (3) tell the user once the way out (larger format, less text, a second image); (4) carry tiers and a density note into the brief. The verdict never becomes numbers in the brief.

## 5. The audience's pull and the category's stance
The output is judged by the audience's eye, not the brief's logic. Establish, in perception terms, what this audience is drawn to and trusts **now**, and what peers make customers feel at this price tier. How that looks is studied and decided by Brand (`3_brand.md` §2); nothing here names a style, technique, or form.
- **Sources:** the user's references and tastes, recorded as stated; your knowledge; the lookups of `2_interpret.md` §1. Without tools, reason and mark provisional.
- **The perception bar:** the level of finish and sophistication this audience expects of an offer at this tier, as impressions a viewer would report ("made by someone who cares", "current and generous"). A floor for every layer, never below what the user's words imply. A higher bar means better making, never a dearer-looking offer: the register stays the perception contract's. Brand translates the bar into visible craft traits.
- **The audience's pull:** the meanings that say "this is for me" (occasion, age, culture, category), each stated as what it must make the viewer feel or recognize and marked **meet** (honor its function through specific, well-made execution) or **depart** (only for a named gain in appeal or clarity). A signal the audience relies on is not a cliché by default.
- **The category's stance:** what peers habitually promise and how they make customers feel: the default stance this business must be told apart from. Brand studies how that stance looks, as the default territory.

## 6. Raw material
Seeds come from the business and its world, never the category's stock imagery. List the drawable specifics: what it sells (each item the content names is a candidate for its own depiction) and how it behaves, name and character, the proposition itself (what the customer gets and why they choose it), materials and process, place and culture, the occasion's habits, the audience's insider objects, what peers claim. Test each: **true**, **relevant** at the encounter, **showable** (a static image can show it truthfully; judged by its kind, not its look), **ownable** (a named peer could not use it unchanged). Write its **significance** in one clause: what it tells about the business or the occasion and what it could do for the perception, never what it would look like; the layer that uses a seed writes its yield (`choose.md` §3). A seed from below-glance content is a supporting device, never the governing idea. Mark each seed by the layer it feeds: **identity-level** (lasting: name, origin, craft, character) feeds the brand; **occasion-level** (this design's moment, setting, offer) feeds the theme. Where the look is fixed, mine only what it leaves open.

## 7. The prior read
The maker has a strong prior. Write what it would most likely make from the user's words alone: how it would draw or fake the logo and identity, the look and idea (usually a style and containers, not a theme), composition, density, lettering, the register it would default to, and each stated quality. The prior read is a prediction used as a baseline, never a decision: the look it predicts is not adopted or fixed here. Be honest about its strengths: usually polished, appealing, well colored, and naturally turning information into pictures (channel symbols, depicted items); the brief never trades these away by fencing content into plain text. Then three lists, each tagged **brand**, **theme**, or **picture**:
- **Keep:** what the prior does well for this audience; carried by the work, not restated.
- **Raise** (names the dimension to lift and why, never the means; the owning layer chooses it): where specificity lifts the result above the prior: a mark and identity belonging to this business, a theme with a world and a logic, the hero's design and situation, hero-content interplay, composition logic, lettering as designed objects, depth and richness at the user's intensity.
- **Fix:** failures this brief's features make likely (volume of exact text, digits, many small depicted items with exact counts, precise geometry or alignment, drifting character details, a hierarchy burying the offer, a logo drifting or fusing with its surroundings) and the generic defaults it falls to where specifics are missing. Each gets a positive remedy in the work, or a flagged limit when the maker cannot deliver it.
Every later decision names the list it serves; one on none is noise. The prior is the bar to beat, not a strawman: when the user fixed the look, craft and specificity do the raising.

## 8. Mandates, handoff, gate
Write each finding as a numbered line, "B1 / T1 / P1: because [finding], the [layer] must / should / must avoid [outcome], shown when [a check on the finished design: what a viewer reads, feels, or does]", tagged given, researched, inferred, or open, and addressed to one layer. A mandate states a **result and how to see it, never the means**: no subject, motif, shape, style, technique, or device (those are the layer's own answer). A mandate naming a means is rewritten as the result the means was meant to achieve, or dropped. Each perception-contract entry yields at least one brand and one theme mandate; identity-level feel entries yield brand mandates and occasion-level ones theme mandates; every mandate states a consequence checkable on a finished design, never a bare quality. Each layer answers its mandates by id (`choose.md` §4); the brief is audited against all (`6_compose.md` §4):
- **Brand** (lasting identity): what it must signal, never signal, and survive.
- **Theme** (this design): what it must do for the perception shift, response, atmosphere, and encounter.
- **Picture** (execution): legibility, hierarchy, density, dependability.
**Handoff.** To Brand: stance, distinctive truth, identity-level feel profile, perception contract, the audience's pull and perception bar, the category's stance, identity-level raw material, the user's stated look words, brand mandates. To Theme: perception shift, message, occasion-level feel profile, perception contract, response, weakest link, encounter facts, content map and tiers, occasion-level raw material, theme mandates.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** every read has a basis and passes swap and obviousness, or is dropped · the strategic core passes its five tests, or its absence is recorded · every decisive fact has a perceptual consequence and every finding changes or confirms a named decision at a named layer · the feel profile is stated as calibrated positions with viewer reads, tagged by layer · the perception contract names, per binding dimension, a target position and the readings to avoid as viewer reads, and every signal conflict is resolved by subject versus register, forked, or flagged · the perception bar and the audience's pull are stated as impressions and meanings, each code marked meet or depart · **no conclusion, contract entry, or mandate names a style, form, motif, technique, or device (boundary test, `SKILL.md`)** · the prior read has concrete keep, raise, and fix, tagged by layer · objective, message, content map (every unit classified, every group ranked, every stated differentiator placed as a selling point or ranked with a reason), and tiers are consistent; every quality-versus-load tension is answered by structure; a dense verdict has its way out. Then append the record.
