# Phase 3: design plan

**Needs:** S; Phase 2 Forward. **Produces (Forward):** brand design and theme; core idea; style (7 dimensions, balance type included); hero with hosting capacity and footprint (share of format, edge behavior); principles (space intent, imagery/decoration stance with supporter budget and treatment vocabulary, integration, mark role, layering, tone and positioning, density, character carriers and links, boundaries, point of tension, figure stance); how each tension is carried; the **seed bank** (each surveyed territory: name, traits, one-line lineage); obligations; flags. The quality carrier map goes into S.
**Order:** plan criteria → brand design/theme → survey → core idea → style → hero (each by the full protocol, each a criterion for the next; no style card before the core idea is chosen) → principles and tension carry → direction check → gate. Each decision has ≥ 1 card from survey, inventory, or departure dimensions. Check renderability and fit throughout.
**Guided entry rows:** main idea, style, what is seen first, open space and decoration, logo role; plus one line on how each stated quality is carried.

## Plan criteria (set before any card)
1. **Space budget:** one table of single numbers summing to 100, an envelope Phase 5 re-derives. Rows: each information group (its load-table row, wraps included); structure and decoration; **quiet** (floor ≈ 8%, with jobs and where it lives); margin and separations (≈ 6-10%); headroom (≥ 3%); the hero group. Fill in that order: the **hero row is the residual**, what its message needs (from glance parts) and never more than what is left. Atmosphere the style's world calls for is paid from the structure-and-decoration row and the hero's surround, never counted as quiet. If the residual is below the hero's minimum share, the brief is dense: pre-apply ladder rungs now (compact arrangement, reference content to a nearer stage, simpler hero lockup), keep core content at its floors, record the volume risk. Derive per deliverable at its own ratio, from the format, role floors, and implied positioning. Do not size strings or draw layouts here. A hero card whose footprint exceeds its row is dropped at the screen. The budget narrows only through the ladder.
2. **Tension leads:** per open Phase 2 tension, the side that leads (conflict priority, user priority, the problem) and the minimum the other keeps.
3. **Carry targets:** the message and the qualities the core idea must carry.
Criteria constrain candidates; revise one only when a card shows it infeasible, and record it.

## A. Brand design and theme
Compare two ways to express the brand's impression here, then state **brand design** (enduring; fixed assets bind it; for a new brand propose with restraint proportional to evidence) and **theme** (this piece's idea) separately. The theme must not contradict the brand design and must leave room for a specific core idea.

## B. Territory survey
From knowledge (counts by openness): historical and movement lineages in design, illustration, lettering, print; regional and vernacular traditions; craft, material, production traditions; contemporary practice in other categories and regions; the subject's own world. One table row per territory: defining traits (depiction, technique, line/form, material/surface, ornament, framing, light, chroma, type, composition); lineage and conditions; true to this subject vs imported whole; behavior at glance and approach here; reproduction and renderability demands. Record traits, not labels. Query only for a territory with uncertain traits (one query, one page); mark unverified provisional. Verify or omit loaded territories.

## C. Core idea
Relates something true about the subject to something the audience recognizes, suggesting a visual structure; a mood word or category is not one. Cards differ on a structural axis. **Fields:** idea in one sentence; source (territory, inventory, departure, weakest link, differentiator, name/origin); the true thing and the recognition; visual structure; behavior per stage; risks (renderability, capacity); compatibility with each quality. **Five properties** every leader shows: specific, visual, generative (theme, hero, style, layout follow), truthful, functional (reads at glance without decoding).

## D. Style
One synthesis across seven dimensions in specialist terms: (1) **visual language** (form and line, corner/edge geometry as the root of shape language, abstraction, geometric/organic); (2) **influences** by lineage and borrowed traits (never a living artist, studio, or protected character/brand); (3) **compositional behavior** (axis, alignment, grid, figure-ground, planes, balance type, tension, rhythm); (4) **material character and visual world** (surface, process evoked, decorative vocabulary and where it lives, or a decision that it holds none); (5) **image-making technique** as a specific language per image type, derived from this brand, never a medium or category name (illustration: lineage, proportions, linework, rendering, texture, detail, color treatment, stylization; photography: framing, light, grade, finish, retouch; mixed: how combined); (6) **typographic expression** (voice and lineage, classification, stroke contrast, proportion, spacing, display vs informational contrast, alignment); (7) **light, atmosphere, chromatic stance** (light direction and quality, tonal key, value structure, temperature and harmony; Phase 4's palette must realize it).
Card: one entry per dimension, territories drawn on, core idea realized, quality compatibility. For each **open** dimension of the leading cards (a dimension the brief fixes is recorded as fixed) compare ≥ 2 options and repair conflicts. Renderability is judged on what must be exact (text, counted forms, mark geometry), never as a reason to pick the plainest language: a concretely described rich style usually renders well.
**Per-zone application:** one language, fully drawn in illustration and ground, calm where information is read; texture that harms text stays out of text zones and does not disqualify a lineage.
**Specificity test:** strike medium/category words (flat, vector, sticker, kawaii, cartoon, pop) and bare mood adjectives (cute, playful, friendly, modern, minimal, clean) from dimensions 1, 4, 5; what remains must still describe a distinct drawable look. Relabeling does not pass.

## E. Hero
Options: symbolic marks, objects, products, in-use scenes, places, figures (supplied/wanted), illustration, photography, abstract forms, typography-led, an equal set, or none. Choose a hero only when one subject deserves primary attention and a renderable depiction exists; otherwise no hero or a small equal set. A figure leads only as a designed character (Phase 4), never stock or a real likeness; this card fixes its role, depiction mode, pose family. Depiction follows what must be true (where the viewer must see exactly what is sold, a mode that stylizes it away is ineligible). A standalone logo request: the mark is the hero.
**Card:** (1) what it is down to true specifics, with depiction mode and technique; (2) what it truthfully represents; (3) stage at which it registers; (4) which mark roles it permits; (5) renderability (avoid real people, complex hands, crowds, fine faces); (6) **footprint** against the budget: share of format and edge behavior (cropped by trim, inside margin), including any text it hosts at that text's floors; a card over the budget's hero row is dropped after one line; (7) quality compatibility; (8) purpose line; (9) **hosting capacity:** what its form can carry (text along or over its contour, callouts, an overlapping tag, surrounding supporters, interlocking groups); which related groups or supporters would reinforce it, at what scale and edge behavior; which parts may sit in front of or behind text; for a circular, arched, or other non-rectangular host, the text capacity by geometry, solved once: a block centered in a circle needs diameter ≥ √(w² + h²) + 2 × clearance (w widest line, h block height); a stack narrowing toward top and bottom is tested line by line against the chord 2√(r² − y²) at each line's farther edge (y from center). The host is sized from its text at the floors, never the text from the host; a host that cannot hold its text inside the footprint is dropped.
The hero's scale, crop, and edge must express the message, not merely fit, and is never enlarged into room the plan holds as quiet or assigns to other roles.

## F. Governing principles (each a decision; compared = ≥ 2 options with one line of strength/weakness vs idea, style, hero, qualities)
**Compared in every plan**
- **Space intent:** the budget as defined (quiet with jobs, atmosphere decided from the style's world, breathing room per group, role ranges). Space intent is a floor for quiet: unneeded area stays quiet or carries decided atmosphere. **Load budget test:** with every information group at its load-table row, the structure row, the quiet floor, and the margin row, the hero's minimum share must still fit with ≥ 3% headroom, per deliverable at its ratio; fitting only with everything at minimum does not fit. On failure the plan is dense: state the pre-applied rungs (smaller hero lockup, compact arrangement, reference content to a nearer stage, fewer supporter families, lighter containers), record volume risk, lower quiet below its floor only when the ladder is exhausted. Core content never shrinks.
- **Imagery and decoration stance and supporter budget:** evaluate each supporter family for what it adds to this piece's world and information; record which are open (job and where it lives: atmosphere on information-free ground, edges, beside the information it serves) or closed (reason), the share they may take, their shared form language, and the **treatment vocabulary**, graded by rank (hero fullest, rest step down; one recipe on everything flattens hierarchy). An open family is a permission, never an obligation, inside the density caps. Plainness is a decision weighed against a developed treatment; it holds when character carriers keep the piece specific, and a piece plain only because its world was never evaluated is underdeveloped. Options differ in how much world is built and where (hero's surround, ground, edges, rendering depth), so dense information is relieved by richness outside its reading ground. Heavy text raises the need for relief, never licenses clutter. A whole family is excluded only for a stated reason.
- **Integration stance:** whether the hero is composed with related content (integrated/attached) or stands alone. Presumption to test: content that labels, explains, prices, or substantiates the hero belongs with it (supplied or true material only). Exception: a hero that carries the message alone or would be diluted by coupling (competing for its edge, hiding its form, shrinking). A standalone hero is a stated decision. Integration regroups content and never enlarges its text.
- **Mark role:** leads, anchors, or signs off, from the roles the hero permits, with a purpose line; fixes the weight Phases 4-5 give it.
- **Density and rhythm** (with space intent): density per category (information, imagery, structure/containers, decoration/surface, rendering treatments), bounded by the budget and the hero's need to dominate, no category rivaling hero or information; content beyond it is triaged now by the ladder. Higher density demands stricter grouping, never more decoration. Rhythm: the glance stage needs a clear first beat. **Density caps** (families and designs, not instances; defaults from the Phase 2 load class, exceeded only by naming the deficit that earns it and the family dropped to pay for it):

| Load class | Supporter and ornament families (beyond hero, its companions, the mark) | Container designs (beyond the hero's own form) | Rendering treatments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | ≤ 6 | ≤ 5 | ≤ 4 |
| Moderate | ≤ 4 | ≤ 4 | ≤ 3 |
| Dense | ≤ 2 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 |
A family is a kind of non-text resource (pattern band, atmosphere effect set such as embers and steam, edge ornament, dividers, spot illustration); its instances are one family. Ornament beside text counts first. The cap is the load tally's pass line.

**Compared when open (direct when steered)**
- **Layering stance:** 1 to 3 planes, what each carries, how strongly expressed; compare options including a single plane; more planes only for a named gain. Flat styles gain richness from tonal steps, quiet surface detail, overlap of flat shapes. Overlap passes the function test (`9_place_and_check.md`); a plane's added prominence counts against the weight ceiling.
- **Tone and positioning:** personality and register as checkable qualities (formality, warmth, assertiveness); a tone signaling the opposite of a stated quality is eliminated. Positioning from economical to premium with its cues (restraint or abundance, finish, scale, space, price visibility); honest to the offer, since one contradicting cue undoes the rest.

**Decided directly**
- **Character and connection:** name the ownable carriers (from idea, inventory, survey) and how major elements link; look first inside elements already needed. Required at every density. Test: with the name covered, could this belong to another subject of its kind at this density?
- **Boundaries:** what the design must never become (from apparent options, neighbors' look, the model's own, brief, production, culture), each later realized as a decided opposite trait.
- **Point of tension:** at most one deliberate break from the plan's own system, carried only by an element allowed to lead, compatible with every quality, lowering reading effort or raising delight at glance; none when the idea, hero, or departure already supplies the unexpected or qualities call for calm.
- **Figure stance** (only if supplied/wanted): roles identify / lead / demonstrate / guide / carry mood; each answers a need no other element answers; one figure serves all roles; subordinate to required information except as hero or mark.
- **Quality carriers:** assign each quality's carriers to plan decisions; a quality with none is a gap.
**Carry tensions** one by one: confirm the lead side against the assembled plan and record what the other side keeps; a lead the plan cannot honor returns to the decision that broke it.

## G. Direction check (once) and gate
Read the plan as one system: do brand design, theme, idea, style, hero, principles reinforce each other and do accepted costs compound? Does it answer the weakest link in the real setting and hold at glance with the name or product removed and in another setting? Does each quality have a carrier and does any decision signal its opposite? Space and load: contiguous quiet with a job, could any element go, is every plainness a decision, does one category rival the hero or information; do volume, space intent, medium, renderability fit (load budget test)? Conflicts return to the responsible decision.
**Gate (fails if any is false):** survey meets the openness minimum, no direction derives from a competitor, no territory used as surface without its lineage understood · every card field filled in specialist terms, no apparent option won without a decisive criterion, leaders passed the three tests, every weakness has a move or accepted cost · idea fits one sentence with five properties; style passes the specificity and swap tests; no style dropped on renderability or load alone without naming what fails · hero has a stated stage and reach, depicts only what is sold, obeyed the hero rule, has reinforcing content decided or a reason to stand alone; a figure exists only if supplied/wanted · plan criteria set before the first card · compared principles show options; space intent passed the load budget test; stance evaluates each family and names the vocabulary and caps · carriers and links named; every quality has carriers; every tension carried. Then the hand-off check; append D.
