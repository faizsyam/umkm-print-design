# Phase 5a: frame, stages, groups, composition, space plan

**Per deliverable.** **Needs:** S (mandatory content, this deliverable's format and ratio, reproduction limits, exclusions, carrier map); Phases 2-4 Forward. **Goal:** an Execution Specification: a composition whose hierarchy, space, scale, and placement are decided for its format, realizing the plan and system. Files 8 to 10 are read together at phase entry.
**Guided entry rows** (Phase 5): reading order, text attached to the image, arrangement, open space (rows only for what differs between deliverables); the entry ends with the table, no confirmation.

## Process (each step fixes what the next carries unchanged)
1. **Frame:** confirm the message (reword only if triage or volume changes what a glance viewer can take away; record it); read the format (§A); assign every item a stage (§B); profile groups (§C). No sizes yet.
2. **Needs:** each group's space need (§D).
3. **Compose:** choose the spatial strategy (§E). The chosen card is the **composition sketch**, kept as a region table; hierarchy, route, and balance are decided here and later steps edit rows, never re-derive.
4. **Measure:** the space plan on the sketch, once (§F), in shares of the format (physical units end with Phase 2's floors).
5. **Specify and verify:** `9_place_and_check.md`.

## A. Format
The format (proportion, orientation, display scale) is a design input, not a container: it fixes which regions exist for hero, text, quiet, how far apart glance and approach content sit, the reading path, where weight balances. Compose for this format; never scale or crop another deliverable's composition. A size change at the same ratio and encounter changes nothing; a changed ratio, orientation, or encounter changes reach, regions, path and is recomposed. Extreme proportions change the strategy path: decide it explicitly.

## B. Stage assignment
Assign by when the viewer needs an item, not by how important the requester feels it is. Stages come from the profile; only glance content is sized for the widest reach, and glance holds only what a passing viewer needs (hero, identity, at most a few words). Everything else goes to the stage where the viewer has chosen to read it. A glance item that cannot meet its floor beside the hero's minimum share moves to approach; a role is never left below its floor. **Split an item at its glance part:** the glance part takes the glance floor, the remainder its own stage's. **Core content** (offers, prices, choices) is assigned to the nearest stage where it is read and keeps comfortable readability there; only **reference content** (contacts, hours, fine print) takes the deliberate-reading floor. Record each item's stage and tier. Visibility needs guide priority; they do not entitle every element to be large.

## C. Relationship map and grouping
Group by job and referent, not only attention order (attention-only grouping yields a picture with captions; referent-only yields a field of labels). Start from the Phase 2 groups; confirm or refine for this format.
**Apply the stance.** The Phase 3 integration stance stands unless a named failure: the hero cannot host an item it couples; more items would be integrated than stay readable; coupling hides or crowds the hero's identifying form. Where the hero carries the message alone, its text stays separate by stage. On a named failure return to Phase 3 and amend the stance.
**Profile every group before any region exists:** items and volume; internal hierarchy (lead, supporting, fine details each hold rank); referent and coupling mode; Phase 4 resources (supporters, container form, copy treatments); stage and tier hence text scale; arrangement form. Supplementary info (contact, social, utility) is one or a few compact groups, never a row or container per item by default.

| Mode | Use when | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated | item names, describes, quantifies, substantiates, or dramatizes the hero or a visible part, is short, and the hero can host it | callout with pointer to the named part; label on the object; text on the hero's contour or an arc; price/evidence tag overlapping the object; headline emerging from the depicted thing; supporters around the hero's edge; a group interlocking with its silhouette |
| Attached | concerns the hero as a whole, or the hero cannot host it | same group: shares the hero's field, axis, or rail; nearer the hero than any other group |
| Separate | self-standing: identity unit, action, contact, anything no depicted thing grounds | own place by stage (corner of the hero's field, an edge, free ground) |

**Rules.** *Most specific referent:* a claim substantiating a depicted thing refers to that thing; parallel items stay distinct statements placed by their own referents (own container only where the container ladder requires) and gather by shared job otherwise. *Hero stays dominant:* integrated/attached text is subordinate; the hero group reads as one object at glance; the resting field around the hero may host its text if isolation survives; text squeezed into a band beside open ground that could host it is a misallocation. *Balance and clarity:* at most five integrated labels; one rail or radial rhythm, one short uncrossed pointer style ending on the named part, text on quiet ground, never across detailed hero parts. *Verbatim:* integration changes position and form, never wording or order; split a string only if each fragment is verbatim, in order, and reads alone. *Group count:* count perceptual groups at glance; units around a shared referent read as one; many equal bands make a list. *Reliability:* only forms the model renders reliably (one short line on one smooth arc, straight pointers, few labels); if doubtful, attach. *Row guidance:* a fine leader or shared rail only where label and value sit far apart.
**Arrangement form per group:** the Phase 4 container stands unless a named failure (overflows, hides the hero, breaks equivalence); the arrangement inside is decided here, changing the container only if no arrangement fits. Choose from function, relations (sequence, comparison, parallel, label/value), length, neighbors, room: stacked, run-in, compact horizontal run, columns/grid, table, clustered/radiating around the referent, floating at the hero's edge, staggered run, or set in a container shape; separators, markers, icons only where they do a job. A long list or several comparable items is tested against columns, groups, grid before one stacked column; a single column is chosen like any form, never the default. Across columns keep logical grouping, reading sequence (column-first or row-first), shared rails, one item form. Compact forms are the first means of making room, ahead of shrinking type or space. One arrangement repeated across groups of different function is a default, not a decision; consistency comes from the shared system. **Equivalent units** (rows, cards) share outer dimensions and one internal structure (padding, rails, treatment); the longest member sets the size; spare room sits at the end of a field, never centered or stretched; a lone outlier is widened, rewrapped, repositioned, or the group restructured instead of inflating all.
**Tests:** *referent* (each item nearest what it refers to; two coupled items that could trade places are only allocated); *caption* (where the stance couples, covering the text must not leave a picture with captions).

## D. Scale and space need
- **Size follows stage, not format.** A larger format changes absolute area and glance distance, not the reach of approach and close content, so those tiers take a smaller share as the format grows. Area this frees goes to the composition (stronger hero, isolation, resting fields, generous separation, decided atmosphere), never extra items or enlarged lower tiers. Confirm each stage is reachable; unreachable content moves to glance, is demoted out, or is a volume risk.
- **Floors by role** come from the Phase 2 profile and are never re-derived; supporting and reference roles stay at their own floors. Internal hierarchy shows first by weight, contrast, case, position, and by size only as one decisive step; size above a floor needs a stated rank reason. If the format cannot carry a tier at its reach, the content moves to a nearer stage, is demoted out, or becomes a volume risk before any layout is drawn.
- **Space need per group** = its load-table area (text at role floors; independent of wraps) plus the breathing room its arrangement needs (spacing steps, §F) and the open ground it needs to read as a unit. Nothing is re-estimated, so the need holds for every strategy card. Isolation raises glance visibility more reliably than size; express scale as proportions and steps between tiers.

## E. Spatial strategy (Direction decision, full protocol)
Compare ≥ 3 structurally different strategies (different organizing logic, not a different hero position). Write each first as a one-line sketch (logic, order of group regions along the governing axis, where free ground sits) and **screen** it (field 10); complete cards only for survivors; one survivor is chosen without manufacturing others and recorded as fixed by fit. Each is a composition built outward from the groups; none starts from equal or conventional zones; groups may interlock with, overlap, surround, or stand apart from the hero. Unless the stance is a hero alone, at least one card couples the hero's related content. **Card fields:** (1) where the eye enters and travels (route), rank order with the lever holding each rank, behavior at the ratio; (2) alignments, grid logic, arrangement form per group; (3) how hero, unit, and groups relate and the shape free ground takes; (4) balance type and counterweight source, tension, density, rhythm; (5) how free ground is planned (quiet and atmosphere amounts, placement, proportions), never the remainder; (6) edge behavior and plane order; (7) behavior per stage, fit with and departure from neighbors; (8) where supporters and designed copy sit; (9) coupling and resulting group count; (10) **fit screen, run first:** each group's need against its region's area, the region's width against the longest unbreakable string, with headroom; a non-rectangular host by its geometry (`5_plan.md` E); a strategy fitting only by compression is eliminated; first-rank footprint must visibly exceed second-rank (a lead hero is sized before the text around it, never the reverse).
Judge with the protocol lenses; density, scale, finish are relative to surroundings; no stock layout without compelling reason.
**Region table (the composition sketch):** one row per region along the governing axis: groups held, width and axis shares of the format, rank, mass class (1-3) and side (balance is read from these), overlap or clearance step to neighbors. **Row 1 is the hero's Phase 3 footprint**, changed only through the ladder; the headline stack's footprint and clearance follow. Every size, text heights included, is a share of the format (floors carried as shares). Each supporter is a row, anchored to a host edge or side with a count, never coordinates. If capacity later fails (`9_place_and_check.md` capacity), take the next surviving card, re-tested; write a new set only if none survives, then the cause is upstream (ladder from rung 6, or Phase 3's budget).

## F. Space plan (once, on the sketch)
Proportions of the full format at this ratio (groups along one dimension as shares of it, summed with separations); honors the space intent and planned free ground.
- **Axis ledger, computed once:** for each group in order: extent = space need ÷ the width its region gives it (wraps recorded here) plus padding, line spacing, gaps at their steps; plus separations, margins, and placed quiet; totaled against the axis and the area as a column summing to 100% with headroom shown. Adjust rows until it sums; never reconcile in prose. A shortfall takes the fit ladder once, as a batch. Later steps read these figures.
- **Area shares:** start from need (a floor), bounded by rank (a ceiling: hero group and first-rank message largest, identity unit per mark role, then supporting, then utility). Where need exceeds what rank allows, the ladder resolves it, not a squeezed zone. Shares plus free ground, margin, separations account for the whole format with headroom; nothing is left to become filler.
- **Order of reservation:** composition has fixed where quiet separates and rests; set proportions first (outer margin, separations, isolation), then the regions the longest, densest core text needs (width and line count at its floor; it cannot compress), then other groups, and last the hero group up to its Phase 3 footprint in what remains, so no group's region includes its own separation and the hero never takes from a need. Fitting inside a region is never the test of adequacy; the room left around the items is.
- **Spacing steps** (three named steps per group, from u = line height of that group's reading text, i.e. body or smallest informational text, never display): **S = 0.5u** (gaps between items in a group) · **M = 1u** (container padding on every side, more for a first- or second-rank claim so the text visibly floats; units of a group) · **L = 2u** (between groups, isolation around hero and unit; the larger neighbor's u). Line spacing: tight for display, ≈ 1.33 to 1.5 × text size for sustained text. Outer margin = larger of L and the production's safe margin plus a visible gutter. Each pair of neighbors takes a step by name; beyond the steps, space comes from what content and rank leave free. Steps sit inside every group's extent. If the ledger does not close with them, change composition, arrangement, or content tiers (ladder); the steps do not change. Every level scales with format, viewing distance, density; denser content needs clearer separation.
- **Distribution:** quiet goes where content is dense and groups must be told apart, along the dimension the content runs; breathing room is local (each group's perimeter, between neighbors, inside each container); open area in one band while other zones are tight is not breathing room.
- **Top-down sizing:** format → groups (shares) → elements (what remains after padding and separation) → text tiers. An element exceeds its place only by taking from a lower-ranked group, never from quiet, margin, or separation.
- **Residual area** stays free ground: quiet where separation, isolation, rest is needed; atmosphere where decided; plain where plainness is decided; never enlarging elements or stretching content.
- **If content does not fit:** the ladder, quiet narrowing last.

## Gate (fails if any is false)
Stage assignment recorded (glance parts split, core vs reference) and every group profiled (items with internal ranks, coupling, arrangement, scale, need) · no deliverable derived by resizing another's composition · each need derived once, before the strategy; strategy chosen by screening structurally different sketches, composed around the needs · only glance content sized for the widest reach; every role at or above its floor for a stated rank reason · the space plan states shares per group (between need and rank ceiling) that with free ground account for the whole format with headroom, spacing steps at their floors, relationship gaps, atmosphere fields · every quiet area has a stated job and is contiguous, free of content, undecorated.
