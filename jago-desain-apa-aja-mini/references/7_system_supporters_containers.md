# Phase 4b: supporters, figures, containers, ground, system review

**Skip supporters (D) only when** the brief fixes a plain, text-only piece or supplies all artwork and no figure is wanted; record it as the plainness decision.
**Needs:** the system so far; Phase 3 hero, stance, figure stance, supporter budget, treatment vocabulary, style dims 1, 4, 5, density caps; S qualities; inventory and seed bank.

## D. Visual supporters
No supporter is a default; a piece needing none is valid, and a user's exclusion of imagery stands. Equally, text-only is a defect wherever information has a job no text does (identify, categorize, reinforce, help interpret).
**Step 1: text-only baseline.** Imagine only required text, identity unit, and a plain ground. Name deficits as concrete observations across: visual interest and variety; evidence of the offer (use, sensory, appetite cues); mood, surface, material beyond type and color; scanning aids (entry points, markers, category cues, rhythm along lists); balance; brand personality and cultural grounding; the theme's visible world (style dim 4 and inventory); reading comfort of dense text; qualities type and color cannot carry alone. A deficit is **real** when its absence would weaken a quality, the message, the reading, or balance; when the piece at its density would read as blank or category-generic (character deficit); or when the stance opens decoration and the theme's world is unrealized without reason (world deficit). Generous quiet is not a deficit. Scale/form/density variety is read from the whole system, never answered by a supporter with no deficit.
**Information-job pass.** For each group, find a job an image does better than words (identify at a glance, tell parallel items apart, categorize, show use or outcome, tie a label to a depicted part, speed the scan). Where words or position already do it, no visual. A supporter doing a real information job is not dropped because it would not fit a container width assumed now: its cost raises its group's space need.
**Order of remedy: enrich before add.** (1) deepen main elements (finish, crop, surface that rewards a closer look); (2) link major elements; (3) atmosphere and surface in the free ground assigned to it and at edges; (4) small supporters (simple icons, spot forms, dividers, an ownable recurring motif); (5) typographic and edge detail. Each stays below the information it serves and inside the structure-and-decoration line. The count of families stays inside the **density caps**: in a dense plan a world deficit is met by the hero's own depth and at most one atmosphere family, and a remedy beyond the cap enters only by naming the family dropped to pay for it. From step 3 on, judge against the lighter alternative (nothing, smaller, spacing) at its cost, in both directions; where the stance opened the family, plainness needs its reason. A family repeated across items is one system with its quantity fixed. Where the allowance cannot hold a remedy, the deficit is accepted rather than met by consuming quiet.
**Counterweight provision.** Unless the stance is strictly symmetric, name a deployable counterweight from what the system already has (ground tonal step, echo of a motif or the hero, a large quiet tonal shape) before creating any new element; quiet, scalable, low-intensity, in the shape language; Phase 5 sets scale and position from the structure-and-decoration line, never the quiet. A piece that later measures unbalanced with none returns here.
**Step 2: weigh ≥ 2 families per real deficit** and adapt the chosen one to the style; deficits in one group share one comparison. Line quality, weight, density, finish, form language come from style dims 1, 4, 5, so it reads as drawn by one hand; illustrated supporters use the style's image language, never the medium default. Draw forms from inventory and seed bank; the category's obvious signifiers are the benchmark. **Representation follows the job:** pictogram/icon for fast recognition at small size; spot or full illustration for character or specifics; photography for proof, appetite, scale, place where a true depiction exists; annotation where a relation needs showing; or none. A supporter depicts the specific thing, not a generic glyph interchangeable between subjects. Where a recognizable symbol says what an item is, adjacent text carries only what it cannot (handle, number, address, time).

**Figures** (mascot, character, person): supplied → fixed asset; wanted (or accepted after a labeled suggestion) → created here; otherwise absent. One role per appearance: **identify** (part of the unit, per mark role, reads as silhouette and one color) · **lead** (the hero, within hosting capacity, one clear pose) · **demonstrate** (subordinate to the offer, simple interaction) · **guide** (gesture or gaze ends on the target, covers nothing, below the information it points to) · **carry mood** (reduced, below required information, in the system's line quality). One figure, one design, identical everywhere. A created figure that leads or identifies is developed like a mark (seeds from true traits, three candidates differing in construction/silhouette/personality, scrutiny for generic, cliché, one-color silhouette, expression range, small size, renderability, one revision); the card states form class and construction, proportions, silhouette, signature trait, personality and pose language, linework and rendering per style dim 5, palette, what it wears or carries. Fictional, non-identifiable, never a real likeness or implied customer/endorsement; check dress, features, gesture for cultural fit; only forms the generator holds reliably. A figure in the mark recurs elsewhere only for a purpose the mark does not serve.
**Step 3: supporter card:** purpose line (loss = the deficit answered; function distinct from every other element); family and form; relation to hero and unit; scale tier, region, plane; quantity, weight ceiling, space cost, renderability. Shared form language, line quality, finish; few micro-details.

## D2. Containers and shape language
The model's default is a rounded pill, solid or outlined: the benchmark in every piece, winning only by naming the criterion the alternatives failed.
1. **Purpose first:** separate groups, bind items, host text over uneven ground, flag one fact, lift a rank, direct the eye, close or anchor an edge, carry material, mark an information role, integrate a group with the hero, counterweight. No purpose, no form.
2. **Lightest device:** spacing and alignment → rule or tonal step → bounded container. A container needs a stated reason lighter rungs fail. Group before containing; one container serves a group, not each member.
3. **One shape language** from Phase 3 corner/edge geometry and the subject's material, craft, or origin, shared by every container, rule, supporter (mark and hero may differ by decision). Fix: geometry class (rectilinear, faceted, curvilinear, organic, hand-made, arched/circular); corner treatment (square, chamfered, notched, scalloped, cut, radiused at a stated proportion); edge quality and ornament (clean, deckled, torn, stitched); line behavior; material impression from the subject's world. Rounding is one option, chosen only when style and subject call for softness. The language constrains geometry, not how many forms, fills, or scales are used.
4. **Form by job within the language:** containers holding different information roles or ranks differ at a glance in form, fill, or scale (lightest lever first: a tonal or scale step before a new form); same role and rank share them everywhere; one design across roles asserts equal status and erases hierarchy. A container holds one unit of meaning. Forms: panel, band, ribbon/banner, tag, ticket/tab, badge/seal, arch/window, frame, brackets, rule/underline, notched/cut edge, offset plate, layered paper, tonal field, image mask. A container may take its form from the hero or the group it holds (following a contour, overlapping the edge, hanging from the depicted thing). Open grouping with no container is a standing option.
5. **Fill mode** by function and rank: solid, outline, tonal field, textured/patterned, image-filled, edge-only, depth (offset plate, shadow), none. Higher ranks take higher-contrast modes; one mode per role; solid-or-outline is a decision only after the others were weighed.
6. **Compare once** inside the system decision (container language for all groups; a group separately only as an Escalation): apparent form, ≥ 2 alternatives differing in rung, geometry, fill, or material (open grouping included); decisive criterion; renderability (straight, geometric, simply notched edges hold; fine torn or intricate cut edges do not); text fit. State the container's job, form, fill, and padding as a relation to its text (`8_frame_and_space.md`, spacing steps), never an adjective. How content is arranged inside is Phase 5's.
7. **Cost:** every container counts against the allowance and weight ceiling and never creates a rival of the same rank; its weight follows its content's rank and is counted with the content; a container never outweighs its text or matches a higher-rank container. More containers than the stage's tiers can parse fragment the piece: merge into one panel with internal rules or tonal fields, or return to spacing.

## E. Ground, unifying devices, other systems (each only where needed)
- **Ground:** the base field that unifies, separates from surroundings, keeps contrast with content. **Surface character** is decided, not defaulted: realize the style's material character and visual world and the baseline deficits, choosing among uniform, tonal, textured, patterned, ornamented by what each adds. A uniform ground carries a reason. Any surface stays below the information behind it: contrast and detail fall inside text zones; open fields carry only tonal change or grain too fine to read as pattern; the denser the information, the calmer the ground; it never softens the edge of text or hero. **Layered ground** (more than one quiet step) where the layering stance calls; in a flat style it is the main enrichment. **Atmosphere fields** are decided with the composition, in free ground assigned to atmosphere, in the shape language and palette, receding under information.
- **Scrim:** translucent tonal layer behind a text zone, only when content must sit on an uneven field and a quiet ground cannot be planned.
- **Edge behavior:** which elements run off the trim (ground, hero, large blocks, surface) and which stay inside; mandatory content, the unit, fine borders stay inside the safe margin.
- **Zone transitions:** a plain edge, or a boundary in the shape language where the transition should feel directed.
- **Other systems** (shapes, framing, material effects, image treatments, information graphics): only what improves the design, each with a purpose line. Lighting and shadow are decisions (direction and softness) or absent. Every rendering treatment comes from the Phase 3 vocabulary, applied by role.

## F. System review (once) and gate
Judge the system as one read: **realization** (each addition's purpose line names the commitment it realizes; an addition realizing none is removed; a commitment realized by nothing is a gap; every quality has a visible carrier) · **allowance and load** (costs as classes fit the structure-and-decoration line with quiet intact; families, container designs, treatments within the density caps; no category dominates unless hero or information; over → remove or merge before shrinking, lowest rank first) · **removal and distinctness** (an addition whose loss costs nothing is removed unless a second purpose earns it; each beats its lighter alternative; two with one function merge) · **weight and plane** (no decoration outranks the information it serves at any stage, forward plane included; one plane per element, cues agree) · **single voice** (line quality, finish, abstraction, light, treatments read as one designer; vocabulary applied uniformly by role) · **additive and subtraction** (re-read the baseline: close each real gap by the remedy order, never filler; plainness is a named decision passing the character test; would removing one more thing improve it?) · **connection and character** (every major element links to another; with the name covered ≥ 2 ownable carriers from different levers remain).
**Gate (fails if any is false):** the system exists in drawable terms and no look-shaping attribute is left to the model's apparent value · every addition has a purpose line tracing to a plan commitment and every commitment is realized · supporters: text-only baseline, information-job pass, world read were run; plainness recorded as a decision; each supporter names its deficit and stays below the information; families not dismissed without comparison; figures name origin and role, share one design; no invented level, rating, or trust mark · containers: purpose line and rung named, one shape language, no two roles sharing one design, apparent form only by criterion, material and layering decided, open grouping weighed · ground: surface decided against the baseline, uniform with a reason, no visible pattern in a field held as quiet, atmosphere placed by the composition · a counterweight exists unless symmetric; treatments within vocabulary; shadow or glow only by decision.
