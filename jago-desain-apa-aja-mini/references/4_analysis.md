# Phase 2: analysis

**Needs:** S; Phase 1 research questions, tensions, flags. **Produces (Forward):** Communication Problem (primary objective, weakest link, message, information groups, load table, tensions with lead side); Environment Profile (encounter, medium reading, stages with floors, departure/fit); audience encounter; differentiation; Visual source inventory; tagged design implications; flags. S is not restated. **Guided:** no stop; findings lead the Phase 3 entry (weakest link; context and where the piece departs; how it stands out or "fixed by evidence").

**Process:** triage research → read the brand (A) and research (F) → Environment Profile (C) and audience (D) → differentiation (B) and inventory (G) → Communication Problem (E) → design implications (discard any finding yielding none; carry Phase 1 quality translations forward as given implications) → gate.

## A. The subject as it is
Record values, personality, positioning shown by what it does and says; credibility actually held (origin, skill, history, proof supplied); the commissioner's aspirations; the audience relationship. Separate fact from interpretation and aspiration from reality; downgrade readings richer than their evidence; record traits to express and claims not to make.

## B. Differentiation
List candidate differentiators from specifics, process, materials, origin, scale, behavior, offer logic, location, people, name, signature item, ritual, and what each peer already claims. Test each: **true** (failure discards), **relevant** at the encounter, **distinctive** (not shared by dozens of peers), **visualizable** (a failure may still shape tone or hierarchy). Choose a strategy; if none is strong, choose execution-led (the piece differs through composition and making) and say so. Record options with test results; the choice must fail the swap test (a named peer substituted makes it false).

## C. Environment Profile (governing parameter)
One per distinct context; deliverables sharing a context share it. Start from the Phase 1 inference and test it. Derive each part from how the deliverable is made, shown, and reached, never from the medium's name.
- **Encounter:** what surrounds it (neighbors' colors, density, scale, order or clutter, finish; competing stimuli; setting); the audience's state (goal, passive/active, moving/still, attention, time, distance or display scale, light, culture); the reading path.
- **Encounter stages:** derived, not assumed to be three. Per stage: what can be perceived, attention and time, the decision, reach. Record each stage's reading distance (where that content is read, not the range's extreme), engagement (passing, approaching, deliberate), and **once** the floors for its role classes: comfortable readability and deliberate reading, each the larger of role floor and raster floor, in mm and as % of format height, with the render scale assumed. A stated viewing range gives its far end to glance content only; glance holds only what a passing viewer needs. Later phases read these and never recompute.
- **Medium reading:** imposes / relies on / affords; record only what changes a decision. Keep a convention where departing costs the audience effort or credibility; depart where it is the apparent option and the purpose gains.
- **Impression vs expression:** hold the impression (what the viewer should feel and conclude, from desired perception and qualities) constant; let the setting decide the expression (palette, density, scale, form).
- **Departure and fit:** find where neighbors are uniform (color, density, scale, order, finish, form); choose the **departure** dimensions (distinctness is relative and can lie in either direction; more intensity is not departure) and record the **fit** dimensions (conventions relied on, credibility cues). The occasion, culture, and atmosphere the brief names are fit dimensions: depart from their apparent signifiers by specificity and craft, never by omitting them. Departure is a decision with a reason.
- If the context cannot be established, state the most probable and mark it provisional.

## D. Audience encounter
Cultural references, behavior, motivations, decision considerations, trusted register, trust cues present or lacking; what the audience does and must conclude per stage. Drop stereotypes and unsupported psychology.

## E. Communication Problem
- **Objectives:** a user-stated priority fixes the primary objective; the weakest link is chosen within it and never replaces it. Record desired response, necessary information, desired perception (qualities included).
- **Information groups:** by the job each group does toward the objective and weakest link (lenses, never a fixed list: carry the proposition, support what is seen first, enable the offer and action, substantiate trust, help evaluation, give practical/contact info, relate further offerings). Per group: items, stage, referent, internal hierarchy, volume. Formed by shared job, not similar looks or the user's list order.
- **Load table (once):** one row per group; a name or phrase whose parts do different jobs gets one row per part (glance part at the glance floor, remainder at its own stage's). Each row: characters, floor, area share of the format = characters × floor cap × ≈ 0.8 width factor × ≈ 2× cap line pitch × 1.3 (wraps, item gaps). Add the total and the hero's **minimum share** (smallest hero carrying the message at the glance floor, from glance parts only). This is the only text-area estimate in the workflow: Phase 3 builds the budget from it, Phase 5 reads space needs from it. A group sits at glance only if its text at the glance floor fits beside the hero minimum; otherwise it goes to approach here. The brief is **dense** when rows + hero minimum + structure-and-decoration (≈ 5%) + quiet floor (≈ 8%) + 3% headroom exceed 100%: state it once with the way out; Phase 3 pre-applies fit-ladder rungs.
- **Tensions:** pairs of needs competing for one resource (glance attention, space, legibility distance, budget, register); state both needs and the resource. Where the conflict priority or the user's priority decides, record the lead side; Phase 3 decides the rest.
- **Weakest link:** the link (attention → comprehension → credibility → action, those the purpose needs) most likely to fail here, weighing cost. Test the brief's own assumptions (deliverables asked, audience assumed, success measure); one that undermines the objective becomes a tension.
- **Message:** one sentence: what the glance viewer takes away and does next, serving the primary objective, contradicting no quality. Phase 3 builds the idea to carry it; Phase 5 confirms it after triage.

## F. Research
A question, not a phase: run only for a question that names a Direction decision it could change and that the brief and knowledge cannot answer. At most three targeted queries and one page read per question, stop at the first sufficient answer, independent questions together; no trend or category surveys (trend articles and listicles are not evidence of visual traits). None needed → record "no research needed". Prefer authoritative, first-hand sources; reviews show perception, not fact; confirm a found subject is the right one; competitor visuals are a baseline to differ from, never a model. Without research tools: reason from the brief, mark conclusions provisional, avoid choices depending on unverified specifics. Record findings with provenance and the decision each informs.

## G. Cultural grounding and Visual source inventory
Identify the cultures, languages, religions, commercial conventions actually relevant (a national label names many aesthetics); preserve the user's register; use regional or traditional references only where the subject truly connects. Mine seven dimensions: practices and rituals; materials and tools with their surface, pattern, ornament traditions; how things are made, handled, shared; spatial and sensory daily details; language, its play and lettering traditions; geography and seasons; the subject's history. Screen each specific: **true**, **respectful** (no tokenism), **meaning verified** (omit a loaded symbol if uncertain), **workable** (can be seen, drawn, rendered at distance, depicted truthfully). A figure the user wants comes from the same specifics, never a stock mascot. Record the inventory: (1) apparent baseline, (2) deeper true specifics (≥ 1 beyond baseline), (3) the workable ones.

## Gate (fails if any is false)
Every finding changes or confirms a named decision and every implication cites a tagged finding · differentiation passes the swap test and shows its options (or "fixed by evidence") · the profile fills every part for each deliverable and passes the context-swap test; departure names its dimension and visible difference, differs in more than size or brightness, contradicts no quality · primary objective follows the user's priority · the inventory holds ≥ 1 non-emblem source (object, material, craft, place, practice), every symbol verified, no competitor as source, every audience claim cited · the message is one takeaway serving the objective · groups follow objective and content with job, stage, referents, hierarchy · load table split by glance part, floors from the profile.
