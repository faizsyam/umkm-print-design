# Phase 4a: color, typography, identity unit, copy treatment

**Phase 4 goal:** one design system (palette with value key, type roles, shape and container language, supporter policy, ground) derived from the Phase 3 direction, plus the identity unit and any created lead figure. Additions each carry a purpose line, weight ceiling, and space-cost class (small/medium/large, never dimensions). Everything else is Execution, specified in the Phase 6 prompt.
**Needs:** S (content, fixed assets, exclusions, reproduction limits, qualities, carrier map); Phase 3 Forward (style dims 6-7, boundaries, tone, seed bank, obligations); Phase 2 (surroundings, light, stages, medium, inventory).
**Order:** inventory fixed assets and limits → color (A) and type (B) as provisional constraints for the mark → mark and lockup (C; adjust A/B in this phase if it exposes a conflict) → copy treatment by content role (D) → supporters, containers, ground (`7_system_supporters_containers.md`) → system review and gate.
**Exploration:** Direction protocol with extended development for a new mark/lockup and a created figure; everything else is **one compact system comparison** (color + type + container language), copy treatments and supporters derived from it. Execution items are not compared.
**Guided entry rows** (Phase 4): color, lettering, logo and name, emphasized text, supporting visuals; for the logo: the idea in plain words, what it looks like, how name and symbol join (or "supplied, unchanged").

## A. Color
**Inputs:** style dim 7, space budget, hero, mark role, message (which items rank first/second for color; final ranks are Phase 5's), fixed brand colors and reproduction limits (ink count, substrate, finish), surrounding color field, ambient light.
**Options differ in logic, not hue:** what color does here (organize, signal, separate, evoke, depart). Per option: roles (dominant, supporting, accent; number of hues and area proportion), harmony, value structure, chroma, temperature, contrast per stage, behavior under reproduction limits and real light, verified cultural associations, quality compatibility. Monochrome, dual-tone, restrained multicolor are all valid when justified.
**Value-key check (mandatory):** state the key from area classes (light, mid, dark), counting ground and every large band; dark masses make a low-key piece however light the accents. Eliminate any option whose key contradicts a quality; achieve departure through hue, chroma, or form inside a compatible key. No contrast ratios unless a quality or reproduction limit depends on one. Describe colors by named hues with qualifiers and roles; hex only for supplied brand colors.
**Distribution:** area is not prominence. Per role record area share, intensity (chroma, value contrast, temperature), and place; a small saturated accent outweighs a large muted field. Strongest chroma and contrast go to first-rank levers (the accent sits on what ranks first or second, never spread across many items). Large areas stay restrained unless a saturated dominant field is the departure (then everything on it is calmed). No utility item takes a high-intensity role; pattern, ornament, atmosphere take calm palette colors.

## B. Typography
Typography carries personality and world as much as color; decided per role, never one family on a size ladder.
- **Voice and character:** name what the lettering says about this subject (from lettering lineages in the territories and inventory, never the category's stock face); give each role its construction: classification, stroke contrast, proportion, x-height, width, terminals, case, posture. Weight, width, proportion are instruments, never left at regular.
- **Roles and pairing:** roles come from content (display, supporting, informational, numerals/prices, small print, promotional/action). State what they share (a construction trait making one family of voices) and what contrasts. Expressive display lettering is a treatment of the display role, not an extra family. One family only when weight, posture, case, width, or spacing alone separates roles; otherwise at most two families with variants (what the model renders reliably).
- **Texture, spacing, orientation:** per role letter-, word-, line-spacing, measure and line count, alignment, orientation (level by default; rotation needs a reason). Tracking and leading are character, never defaults; they feed space needs.
- **Differentiation by purpose:** each role is styled to its success measure. Roles in one group differ at a glance by ≥ 1 contrast other than scale (classification, weight, width, posture, case, spacing, color); scale ranks, it does not differentiate. Headline, tagline, description, promo message, supporting info, call to action never inherit one treatment at different sizes. Equivalent items share one style whatever their length. Keep roles few but distinguishable. The wordmark's lettering differs from informational type in construction, weight, or treatment. Dense text raises rendering risk: plan structure. Describe type by traits; name a font only for a stated brand requirement.
- **Readability vs expression:** each role is legible at its stage's size and real light (Phase 2 floors); the more expressive the voice, the fewer roles carry it.
- **Letterform proportions:** width class, posture, baseline decided per role. Informational text keeps natural proportions and a level baseline. Fit is achieved by container size, breaks, tier size, or the ladder, never by compressing, stretching, or slanting; a role chosen for a narrow or tilted form gets room for it.

## C. Identity unit: mark, wordmark, lockup
A basic, generic, or forgettable identifier fails even if it passes every test.
**Hard rules**
- A new identifier is never only a styled name: it carries a personality device (transformed lettering, integrated symbol, distinctive silhouette or container, symbol beside the name, or a combination), never decoration added to look designed.
- A supplied logo (plain wordmark included) is used as given unless the user asks otherwise; an explicit plain-text request governs. Supplied lockup: decide only role, region, clear space, relation to the system. Mark plus separate name: leave both unaltered, decide arrangement, spacing, alignment, color roles. A supplied mascot is never redrawn; decide its role. A tagline is copy unless supplied inside a lockup.
- **The device never costs the name:** every letter stays complete, in order, recognizable; a symbol that replaces, merges with, or distorts a letter is allowed only where a first-time viewer still reads the exact name at once at its stage. An emblem never borrows the look of an official seal.
**Inheritance:** when the core idea or hero already fixes the form class or construction (the hero is the mark's container), development runs on the open remainder: candidates differ in device and lockup, never a new construction, and a candidate contradicting the fixed form is not written. Scrutiny keeps the tests that can still fail (generic, name legibility, device necessity, renderability).
**Extended development** (also the method for a created lead figure, where a candidate is a figure design and the lockup test becomes silhouette and expression range): (1) **mine seeds** (count by openness) from the name's meaning/sound/shape/initials, the product's defining truth, process, origin, the audience's ritual, inventory, seed bank, environment; include seeds beyond the apparent; keep only true, ownable, workable ones. (2) **Three developed candidates** (seed and device, construction family, lockup arrangement/proportion/shared logic, small-size behavior), differing in source or construction, at most one apparent. Families: enclosed, contained, integrated, emblematic, open. (3) **Scrutiny:** generic (survives a competitor's name?), story, memory (one sentence, redrawable), silhouette and reduction (one solid color, small), device necessity (without it, a styled name?), name legibility, cliché, renderability. (4) **One genuine revision** after critique, then choose with runner-up and decisive criterion. (5) **Winner tests:** lockup, separation from hero/lettering/decoration, text-neighborhood (differs from the densest adjacent text on ≥ 2 distinction mechanisms); if still basic, return to mining naming the weakness. (6) **Generation preparation:** models render simple geometric clearly described forms far better than intricate ones; simplify the execution, never the idea; define geometry, key shapes, relationships, colors concretely.
**Lockup integration** (one unit, not two objects): decide **arrangement** (beside, stacked, one container, mark integrated into lettering) against stage and region; **shared construction logic** (a module from the mark that the lettering echoes, spacing from a measurable unit, shared axis, optical balance); **fixed scale proportion** and **one color role scheme**; which element leads; **name hierarchy:** a name's words take size from their job: the distinctive word carries the glance part and glance floor, category or descriptive words step to the next stage's floor, so the wordmark is two sizes at most; it stays one size only when the glance floor still fits the hero's footprint; spelling and order never change. **Separability:** the mark works alone; the wordmark alone keeps the construction logic; they share logic but not form class. **Tests:** unit (one shared outline or container, or one construction module); disconnection (cover each: does the other stay recognizably the same identity?).
**Distinction from type** (judge the whole unit as a relationship in the composition; dense layouts usually need ≥ 2): (1) form class (silhouette, mass), (2) mass and texture (compact solid vs even linear text), (3) color role the body text does not share, (4) scale step sized to the role, not enlargement compensating weakness, (5) isolation (protected clear space), (6) containment or fixed anchor region. In dense layouts resolve distinction by composition, never endless enlargement; weight follows the mark role.
**Identity forms:** the full unit identifies the source once per view (a second identifier is removed unless it serves a purpose the unit cannot); symbol-only appears only where it can be linked to the name; name-only exists only for a supplied wordmark; weight follows mark role.

## D. Content roles and copy treatment
Every text item is an element (items of one role, rank, stage may share a purpose line). Phase 4 fixes **grade, device, and color role** per content role; line breaks and counts are planned in Phase 5 (planned wrap) and executed in Phase 6.
- **Designed** when the role carries voice, persuasion, memory, or the transaction (hooks, slogans, taglines, headline claims, prices, sizes, offers) or the item's rank is first or second at any stage. Scale alone is not treatment. Graphic lettering with ≥ 1 device from the style's lettering lineage: rhythm-setting breaks; scale/weight contrast; key-word emphasis; baseline or alignment play; a container; a fitted motif or underline; a distinct color role; text following the arc of the hero; a tag overlapping the hero; text emerging from or carried by the depicted thing; text passing in front of/behind part of the hero with every letterform whole.
- **Set:** informational and functional items (descriptions, lists, addresses, contacts) in the informational face with the role's case, spacing, weight, color role, alignment decided (never default text); hierarchy by size, weight, spacing, grouping.
- **Reassurance claims** that matter at approach: each independent claim keeps its own statement and sits by its referent; claims serving one purpose form one compact group (arrangement in Phase 5), a container only where the container ladder requires, never a trust mark. They merge only if the viewer should read one claim. A claim substantiating a depicted thing is placed with that thing.
- **Rules:** rank is provisional (message, role, hero, mark role); Phase 5 owns final rank and, if it promotes an item to first or second, returns here for that item only. A ranked item left plain needs the plain setting recorded as the style's choice and holds rank through other levers. A voice-bearing line in the informational face on bare ground is under-served. Designing changes form only, never words. Designed treatments cost space and attention: no more than the tiers can carry, one lettering logic, none competing with the identity unit. Write each as a concise spec: letterform character, emphasis, container, color role, relation to neighbors. Treatments follow the system decision (compared only as an Escalation); test fit to purpose at the item's stage within its rank.

## Gate (this file's part)
Color, type, mark each show their comparison (apparent option named, options, decisive criterion), compared against the profile's surroundings and light; value key supports every quality; color intensity follows rank · type has a voice from this subject; roles in one group differ on a named attribute other than scale; same-role items share one style; each role has width class, posture, baseline, spacing, alignment · mark: fixed asset untouched or the development recorded; personality device present; unit and disconnection tests passed; exact letters readable; text-neighborhood passed with named mechanisms; no second identifier; originality tests passed; geometry describable in a few concrete shapes · each text item's grade matches its role and provisional rank; no first/second-rank text left as scaled plain type without a recorded style reason; roles sharing a group differ by more than size.
