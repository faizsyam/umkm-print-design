## 1. Process

Per deliverable, with the shared decisions as inputs. Files 10 to 14 are read together at phase entry; the work runs in this order, and each step fixes what the next carries unchanged.

1. **Frame** (this file): confirm the message from Phase 2 (reword only if triage or volume changes what the glance viewer can take away, and record it); read the format (B9); assign every item to a stage (B1); profile the groups with their referents, coupling modes, internal ranks, and arrangement forms (A2). No sizes yet.
2. **Needs** (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` B3, B4): each group's space need, read from the Phase 2 load table and completed with the breathing room its arrangement needs.
3. **Compose** (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` C): decide the spatial strategy. The chosen card is the **composition sketch**, kept as a region table (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` C): regions in order along the governing axis with their shares, the hero's and headline stack's footprints and clearance, where the free ground sits, the rank order with its levers, the reading route, and the balance relation with its counterweight. Hierarchy, route, and balance are decided here; later steps carry the table and edit its rows, never re-derive it.
4. **Measure** (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` B2): the space plan on the sketch, computed once: each group's extent from its need and the width its region gives it, wraps recorded, separations and margins in a few graded steps, totals against the axis and the area, headroom or shortfall, the fit ladder applied once as a batch to any shortfall. These are the sizes later steps use.
5. **Specify** (`12_hierarchy_and_element_placement.md`; backgrounds, reproduction conditions, and the set in `14_backgrounds_review_and_gate.md` E): every element and text item inside the sketch's regions, its levers set to deliver the sketch's ranks, with planes, overlaps, and relationships. An item promoted to first or second rank with no treatment decision takes the Phase 4 treatment check (B2 in `06_identity_unit_and_copy_treatment.md`).
6. **Verify once** (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md`; `14_backgrounds_review_and_gate.md` F): one pass over the specified composition: rank order, route, and balance against the sketch; load; capacity for the strings in doubt, then the size freeze; proportion, fill, and zones; the composed-whole reads. A failure returns to the earliest step that owns it (cap: two cycles). Fit edits and verification do not interleave: close the ledger first, then verify after the last fit edit; a later fit edit that changes no region order, rank, or side does not reopen it. Gate.

## 2. Guidance

### B9 and B1. Format and stage assignment (items 9 and 1 of section B)

Visibility requirements guide prioritization; they do not entitle every element to be large.

9. **Format.** The deliverable's format (proportion, orientation, display scale) is a design input, not a container. It fixes which regions exist for the hero group, text, and quiet space, how far apart glance and approach content sit, the reading path, and where weight can balance.
   - Compose for this format; never derive it by scaling or cropping another deliverable's composition. Across the set what transfers is decisions (identity, style, color, type, element system, shared hero treatment), not layout.
   - A size change at the same ratio and encounter changes nothing; a changed ratio, orientation, or encounter changes reach, regions, and path and is recomposed.
   - Extreme proportions change the strategy path (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` C): decide it explicitly.

1. **Stage assignment.** Assign by when the viewer needs each item, not by how important the requester feels it is. Stages come from the Environment Profile; only glance-stage content is sized for the widest reach, and glance holds only what a passing viewer needs (the hero, the identity, at most a few words) because exposure time bounds it; everything else is assigned to the stage at which the viewer has chosen to read it. Record each item's stage and tier.

### A2. Relationship map and grouping

Group content by the job it does together and by what it refers to, not only by when it is read: grouping by attention order alone yields a picture with captions, and grouping by referent alone yields a field of separate labels. The Phase 2 information groups are the starting point; confirm or refine them for this format and these items.

**Apply the stance.** The Phase 3 integration stance stands unless a named failure appears:
- the hero cannot host an item the stance couples to it;
- more items would be integrated than stay readable (below);
- coupling would hide or crowd the hero's identifying form.

Where the hero carries the message alone, or coupling would dilute it, its text stays separate by stage and the hero keeps its isolation (the modes below then apply only to what is still coupled). On a named failure, return to Phase 3 and amend the stated stance.

**Profile every group** before any region exists: its items and volume; its internal hierarchy (a lead item, supporting items, and fine details each hold their own rank inside the group, so related items read as one visual unit without sharing prominence); its referent and coupling mode; the visual resources Phase 4 gave it (supporters, container form, copy treatments); its stage and legibility tier, hence the scale of its text; and its arrangement form (below). Its **space need** is computed next (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md`, B4). Supplementary information (secondary, utility, contact, social) is profiled the same way: one or a few compact groups, never a row or container per item by default.

**Map every text item** from its referent and content role: what it refers to, whether the hero's form can host it, and its mode.

| Mode | Use when | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated | The item names, describes, quantifies, substantiates, or dramatizes the hero or a visible part, is short, and the hero can host it | Callout with a pointer to the named part; label on or against the object; text following the hero's contour or an arc over it; a price, offer, or evidence tag overlapping or abutting the object; a headline emerging from the depicted thing; supporting elements floating over or surrounding the hero's edge; a group interlocking with its silhouette |
| Attached | The item concerns the hero as a whole, or the hero cannot host it | Same group: shares the hero's field, axis, or rail; nearer the hero than any other group |
| Separate | Self-standing: identity unit, action and contact, anything about the subject as a whole that no depicted thing grounds | Its own place in the composition, by stage (a corner of the hero's field, an edge, or free ground, as its function and the composition ask; not necessarily a zone) |

**Rules**
- **Most specific referent.** An item refers to the most specific thing it is about or evidence of: a claim that substantiates a depicted thing refers to that thing, not to the subject. Parallel items (several items doing the same job) stay distinct statements, each placed by its own referent where the referents differ (in its own container only where `08_containers_and_ground.md`, D2's ladder requires one), and gather into one group by their shared job where they do not. They merge into one statement only when the viewer should read them as a single claim.
- **Hero stays dominant.** Integrated and attached text is subordinate in scale, weight, and contrast; the hero group reads as one object at glance, its details resolving at approach. The resting field around the hero can host the group's text, provided the isolation the hero needs and the field's open character survive. Text squeezed into a separate band beside open ground that could host it without losing either is a misallocation.
- **Balance and clarity.** Integrated items counterbalance the hero's composition (one-sided only when the asymmetry is the plan). Integrate only as many items as stay readable at approach (at most five labels): one rail or radial rhythm, one short uncrossed pointer style ending on the part named, text on quiet ground, never across detailed parts of the hero.
- **Verbatim.** Integration changes position and form, never wording or order. Split a string across labels only if each fragment is verbatim, in order, and reads alone; otherwise attach it whole. A group title stays with its items.
- **Group count.** Count perceptual groups at glance. Merging items that share a referent or a job lowers the count, and units arranged around a shared referent read as one group; many equal separate bands make a list.
- **Reliability and isolation.** Use only forms the model renders reliably (one short line on one smooth arc, straight pointers, few labels). An integrated form that overlaps or wraps the hero needs a clean isolated hero edge. If doubtful, attach.
- **Row guidance.** Where a label and value sit far apart, a fine leader or shared rail ties the pair; where proximity already does, add nothing.
- **Arrangement form per group.**
  - **Phase 4 pairing stands.** Where Phase 4 fixed a container with a provisional arrangement form for the group, that pairing stands unless a named failure (it overflows its room, hides the hero, or breaks equivalence); then change the arrangement first and the container only if the arrangement cannot fix it.
  - **Choose** each remaining group's form from its function, its relations (sequence, comparison, parallel items, label and value), content length, neighbors, and the room the composition gives it: stacked, inline or run-in, a compact horizontal run, columns or grid, table, clustered or radiating around its referent, floating or overlapping at the hero's edge, a staggered or asymmetric run, or set into a container shape, with separators, markers, or icons only where they do a job (`07_visual_supporters_and_figures.md`, D). Supplementary information takes the most compact form its relations allow; where a recognizable symbol says what an item is, its text carries only the data (`07_visual_supporters_and_figures.md`, D, representation).
  - **Fit to region and quantity:** a long list, or several comparable items, is tested against columns, groups, or a grid before a single stacked column, and a short run in a wide band against inline form, so neither vertical length nor line length stretches without need. A single stacked column is chosen like any other form, never as the unexamined default.
  - **Across columns or sections,** preserve logical grouping, reading sequence (stated as column-first or row-first to suit the reading path), alignment on shared rails, and one item form.
  - **Compactness:** compact forms are a first means of making room, ahead of shrinking type or space, but only where they keep clarity and the separation of the space plan.
  - **Consistency:** one arrangement repeated across groups of different function is a default, not a decision. Consistency comes from the shared system (alignment logic, spacing rhythm, shape language, type roles). Each zone and sub-composition is decided from its own function, then reconciled with the whole.
  - **Equivalent units stay balanced inside.** Cards, rows, or panels of the same role presented as a set of repeated containers share outer dimensions and one internal structure: the same padding, the same alignment rails (title, body, price, and similar fields start and align at the same positions), the same typographic treatment. Differences in content length are absorbed by that structure: shorter content leaves its spare room at the end of its field, consistently, and is never centered, stretched, or enlarged to fill. The longest member sets the unit's size; if one outlier would force every unit far larger than the rest need, widen or rewrap the unit, reposition the outlier, or restructure the group (A2 form) instead of inflating all.

**Tests**
- *Referent:* each item sits nearest what it refers to. If two coupled items could trade places without changing meaning, they are only allocated, not integrated.
- *Caption:* where the stance couples, covering the text must not leave a picture with captions beneath it.
