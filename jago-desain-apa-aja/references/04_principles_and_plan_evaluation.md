## 2. Guidance (continued from `03_core_idea_style_and_hero.md`)

### F. Governing principles (the stance sheet)

Each is a decision, not a default.
- **Compared** principles: two or more options, one line of strength and weakness each, judged against the core idea, style, hero, and quality requirements (compact comparison).
- **Decided directly:** a one-line basis.
- The depth table in `scope_roles_and_depth.md` section 6 says which are compared.

**Compared at every depth**
- **Space intent.**
  - **Definition:** the **space budget**: the share of the format that stays quiet and its job (separate glance content, rest the eye, signal positioning), where it lives (distributed among the zones that need separating, never parked as one empty band), the breathing room each zone keeps, and the rough share each occupying role may take. Only open ground counts as quiet (`terms_design_system.md`, Space intent); decorated ground is occupied.
  - **Derived from** format size, proportion, and orientation, encounter stages and their reach, content volume and text load, and positioning: the plan-criteria budget, confirmed or narrowed once the hero's space cost is known.
  - **Scale:** larger media raise space intent or the strength of the main idea, not element count. Space intent is a floor: area the content does not need stays quiet and is never spent on enlargement.
  - **Load budget test:** total, at rough area shares, everything the plan will make occupy the format: the hero group at the size its message needs; the identity unit per its role; every text group at its legibility minimum with its line spacing and padding (the longest and densest mandatory strings first, since they cannot compress); and the containers, supporters, and surface the plan intends. The total must fit the non-quiet share with headroom. A plan that fits only when every item sits at its minimum and every gap at its tightest does not fit. On failure, simplify the plan by the fit ladder now (reduce hero group size, supporter families, or container weight; compact the grouping; change the integration stance) and lower the space intent only when the ladder is exhausted, recording a volume risk.
- **Imagery stance and supporter budget.** Which supporter families are open, the share of the format they may take (from the structure-and-decoration line of the space budget), their shared form language, and the **treatment vocabulary**: the few rendering treatments (outline, shadow, gradient, texture, highlight, dimensional effect) that the one primary visual language admits, each named with its job and the roles it applies to, and no others.
  - An open family is a permission, never an obligation.
  - Supporters rank below the hero, identity unit, and required information they serve.
  - Heavy text raises the need for relief, never licenses clutter.
  - A whole family is excluded only for a stated reason (brief, style, production, culture).
  - A family used across many items is one system, and its cumulative footprint counts as a block.
- **Integration stance.** Whether the hero is composed with related content and supporters (integrated or attached) or stands alone in separate zones.
  - Presumption to test, not assume: content that labels, explains, prices, or substantiates the hero, and supporters that clarify its value or context, belong with it, drawn only from supplied or true material.
  - Exception: a hero that carries the message alone, or one that coupling would dilute (competing for its edge, hiding its form, shrinking it). There, separate zones with isolation are stronger.
  - A standalone hero is a stated decision with its reason, never the unconsidered default.
  - Decide from the core idea, hosting capacity, and content inventory.
  - Integration regroups content and never enlarges it: coupled items sit inside the hero group's share of the budget.
- **Mark role.** Whether the identity unit leads, anchors, or signs off, against the Communication Problem, chosen from the roles the hero permits, with its purpose line. It fixes the weight Phases 4 and 5 give it.
- **Density and rhythm.**
  - Density: the plan's visual-load level, stated per category (information, imagery, structure and containers, decoration and surface, rendering treatments), bounded by the space budget and the hero's need to dominate; no one category may rival the hero or the required information. Content beyond it is triaged now by the fit ladder, not shrunk. Higher density demands stricter grouping, never more decoration.
  - Rhythm: the glance stage needs a clear first beat; uniform loudness or quiet is no rhythm.

**Compared at Full depth (direct at Focused)**
- **Layering stance.** One to three planes, what each carries (hero, information, supporters, base field), and how strongly the order is expressed. Plane order ranks content, integrates it with the hero, and adds richness.
  - Compare options with a single plane among them; more planes only for a named gain.
  - A flat style keeps one picture plane and gains richness from tonal steps, quiet surface detail, and overlap of flat shapes.
  - Overlap is allowed wherever it passes the function test (`12_hierarchy_and_element_placement.md`, A). Layering adds no element beyond space intent, and a plane's added prominence counts against the weight ceiling.
- **Tone and positioning.**
  - Tone: personality and register as checkable qualities (formality, warmth, assertiveness), governing typographic stance and the hero's manner. A tone signaling the opposite of a stated quality is eliminated.
  - Positioning: where the offer sits from economical to premium, and the cues that signal it together (restraint or abundance, finish, scale, space, price visibility). It is honest to the offer; one contradicting cue undoes the rest.

**Decided directly**
- **Character and connection.** Name the ownable character carriers the plan relies on, each drawn from the core idea, inventory, or survey, and how major elements will be linked. Look first for carriers inside elements the plan already needs (the hero's depth, lettering character, color logic) before adding any new element. Required at every density: the sparser or calmer the plan, the more each element must carry. Quality compatibility is not enough; the plan must also pass "with the name covered, could this belong to any other subject of its kind at this density?"
- **Boundaries.** What the design must never become, as qualities and treatments, drawn from the apparent options and neighbors' look (the model's included), then from brief, production, and culture. Each is realized later as a decided opposite trait, not a bare exclusion.
- **Point of tension.** At most one deliberate break from the plan's own coherent system (scale, contrast, placement, or concept). It is carried only by an element allowed to lead, is compatible with every quality requirement, and lowers reading effort or raises delight at the glance stage. None is the answer when the core idea, hero, or departure already supplies the unexpected, or when style and qualities call for calm.
- **Figure stance** (only when a figure is supplied or wanted). Roles: identify as part of the mark, lead as hero, demonstrate, guide, carry mood. Each role answers a need no other element answers; one figure serves all roles; it is subordinate to required information except as hero or mark. An unmentioned figure is offered once as a suggestion where a real deficit calls for it, and never enters unconfirmed.
- **Quality carriers.** Assign each quality requirement's carriers to plan decisions (color and value key, style, hero, density, tone). A requirement with none is a gap.

**Carry the Phase 2 tensions** one by one: confirm the lead side set in the plan criteria against the assembled plan and record what the other side keeps. A lead the plan cannot honor returns to the decision that broke it.

### G. Assembled-plan evaluation and stress tests

Check the plan as one system:
- Do brand design, theme, idea, style, hero, and principles reinforce one another?
- Does the plan answer the weakest link in the real setting?
- Do content volume, space intent, medium, budget, and renderability fit together?
- Do accepted costs compound?

Return to the responsible decision on a conflict (two cycles per issue). Then run and record:
1. **Swap / category:** with a competitor's name, or the product removed, does it still work unchanged? If so it is generic or stereotyped.
2. **Context-swap:** is it equally right in another setting? Then it ignores the environment.
3. **Distance and attention:** does the first impression hold at the glance stage in the real setting?
4. **Contradiction scan** (`quality_gates.md` section 1): do all plan decisions agree?
5. **Ambition:** does it fit content, budget, and format?
6. **Brief-quality** (`quality_gates.md` section 1), against the carrier map: does each stated quality have a carrier at the glance stage, and does any decision signal its opposite? This run is also the gate's quality check.
7. **Crowding and restraint:** squint the assembled plan. Is there contiguous, undecorated quiet ground with a job, and does the load budget hold with headroom? Could any element, treatment, or supporter be removed so the plan reads better? Does one category (decoration, containers, imagery) rival the hero or the required information?
