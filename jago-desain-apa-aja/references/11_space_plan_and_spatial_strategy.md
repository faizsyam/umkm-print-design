# Phase 5: Design Execution, part 2 of 5: group scale and space need, spatial strategy, space plan

Derive each group's scale and space need from its stage, choose the spatial strategy by the full protocol as a composition built around the groups with the free ground planned in it, then quantify the chosen composition as the space plan.

**Inputs needed:** Stage assignment, group profiles, and relationship map from `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`; Phase 3 space intent, space budget (free ground as quiet share and any atmosphere, role needs), density level, balance type, hero hosting capacity, integration stance; Phase 4 element system; Phase 2 encounter stages with reading distances; S format and ratio.

## 2. Guidance (continued from `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`)

Order of work in this file: B3 and B4 (scale and space need), then C (strategy), then B2 (space plan). The section labels are kept for reference from other files.

### B3, B4, and B6. Size follows stage, legibility minimum and space need, space as visibility (items 3, 4, and 6 of section B)

3. **Size follows stage, not format.**
   - A larger format changes how much absolute area exists and, for a physical piece, how far the glance viewer stands; it does not change the reach at which approach and close content is read.
   - Each role's floor comes from its own stage's reach, material, light, and contrast, and from the role's job (comfortable readability or deliberate reading).
   - Only glance content must be understood at the widest reach, and only it is sized for it. Approach and close tiers are sized for their nearer reach and take a smaller share of the format as the format grows, because the audience that wants them comes closer.
   - Confirm each stage is reachable (Phase 2). Content whose stage is unreachable moves to glance, is demoted out, or is recorded as a volume risk.
   - Area this frees goes to the composition (a stronger hero, isolation, resting fields, more generous separation at every level, atmosphere where the style's world calls for it), never to extra items or enlarged lower tiers.

4. **Legibility is a minimum by role, from its own stage; readability and prominence are separate.** Each role takes the floor its job needs from the Phase 2 Environment Profile (comfortable readability for what is read at once or in long runs, nearer the limit for reference and deliberate reading) and never re-derives it; supporting and reference roles stay at their own floors and do not borrow the lead's size. Size beyond a floor buys emphasis, which works only when most elements lack it, and every role above its floor needs a stated rank reason. If the format cannot physically carry a tier's content at its reach, the content moves to a nearer stage, is demoted out, or is recorded as a volume risk before any layout is drawn.
   - **Scale and space need per group.** Each group's text roles are set at their role floors, with internal hierarchy shown first by weight, contrast, case, and position and by size only as one decisive step where the rank needs it, never enlarged to fill room or to feel safe. The group's **space need** (`terms_design_system.md`) is the area that content takes at that scale with the breathing room inside it (line spacing, gaps between items, padding where contained) and the open ground it needs around it to read as one unit, taken from the Phase 2 load table (text area at the role floors, which does not depend on where it wraps) plus the breathing room its arrangement form needs at the B2 floors; nothing is re-estimated, so the need holds for every strategy card. The physical format enters here: the same text height is a smaller fraction of a larger piece, so on a large format approach and close groups need a smaller share of area while the glance message and the hero group do not automatically grow; on a small format the same groups may not fit at their reach, and the fit ladder decides before any layout is drawn.

6. **Space as visibility and scale as relationships.** Isolation raises glance visibility more reliably than size. Express scale as proportions and steps between tiers.

### C. Spatial strategy

Compare at least three structurally different strategies (different organizing logic, not a different position of the same arrangement). Write each first as a one-line sketch (its organizing logic, the order of the groups' regions along the governing axis, where the free ground sits) and screen it (field 10); complete the card fields only for survivors. One survivor is chosen without manufacturing others and recorded as fixed by fit. Each is a composition built outward from the groups: how the hero, the groups with their space needs, and the free ground (quiet and atmosphere) relate. Each card states how the groups are arranged and where the free ground sits; cards that arrange the groups the same way and differ only in the hero's position are one card. None starts from a partition of the format into equal or conventional zones; groups may interlock with, overlap, surround, or stand apart from the hero as their function asks. They differ in how content is distributed, not only where the hero sits: single or multiple columns, grid, bands, horizontal or vertical runs, clusters or orbits around the hero, staggered or asymmetric arrangements, chosen from content volume, relations, hierarchy, and the format. **Card fields:**
1. Where the eye enters and how it travels (the reading route over the regions), with the rank order of the groups and the lever that holds each rank; behavior at the aspect ratio (extreme ratios change the path: decide it explicitly).
2. How groups relate: alignments, grid logic, and the arrangement form of each group.
3. How hero, identity unit, and groups relate (interlock, overlap, surround, stand apart) and the shape the free ground takes: where quiet sits and what it separates or frames, where atmosphere fills, figure-ground.
4. Balance type and where its counterweight comes from, tension, density, rhythm.
5. How the free ground is planned: the amount, placement, and proportions of quiet and atmosphere as part of the arrangement, never as the remainder.
6. Edge behavior and plane order.
7. Behavior at each stage, fit with and departure from neighbors.
8. Where supporters and designed copy sit and how they relieve, group, and balance text areas.
9. Coupling: what content and supporters are integrated, attached, separate, and the resulting group count.
10. Fit (the screen, run first): each group's space need against the area its region gives it, and the region's width against the group's longest unbreakable string, with headroom; a strategy that fits only by compression is eliminated, and a non-rectangular host is screened by its geometry (`03_core_idea_style_and_hero.md` E). Screen dominance too: the first-rank element's footprint exceeds the second-rank element's by a visible margin, read from the region table (a lead mascot or hero is sized before the text around it, never the reverse).

Judge with the protocol lenses. Density, scale, and finish are judged relative to the surroundings. No universal grid, fixed zone count, or stock layout without compelling reason. Unless the stance is a hero carrying the message alone, at least one card couples the hero's related content to it; message coherence outweighs tidy zoning.

The chosen card is the **composition sketch**, recorded as a region table: one row per region along the governing axis: the groups it holds, width and axis shares of the format, rank, mass class (1 to 3) and side (balance is read from these), and overlap or clearance step with its neighbors. The first row is the hero's footprint from Phase 3, which changes only through the ladder; the headline stack's footprint and clearance follow. Every size, text heights included, is a share of the format (the Phase 2 floors carried as shares), and each supporter is a row too, anchored to a host edge or side with a count, never to coordinates. The space plan fills its extents and the ledger totals its axis column; later steps edit rows and re-total, never re-derive, and moving a region returns here, naming the failed check. If capacity later fails (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md` B7), the next strategy is the next surviving card, re-tested; a new set is written only when none survives, and then the cause is upstream (the fit ladder from rung 6, or Phase 3's budget).

### B2. Space plan (item 2 of section B)

2. **Space plan** after the strategy and before placing anything, as proportions of the full format at this deliverable's ratio (for groups arranged along one dimension, as shares of that dimension, summed with their separations): the chosen composition made quantitative. It honors the Phase 3 space intent and the free ground the strategy planned. It contains the **axis ledger**, computed once on the sketch: for each group in order along the governing axis, extent = space need ÷ the width its region gives it (wraps recorded here), plus its padding, line spacing, and gaps, and the separations and margins, all at their floors, plus the quiet the composition placed on this axis, totaled against the axis and the area, with headroom or shortfall stated (`terms_design_system.md`, Governing axis). State extents as shares of the format's height and width (physical units end with Phase 2's floors), as a column that sums to the axis with the remainder shown as headroom; adjust rows until it sums, never reconcile a total in prose. A shortfall takes the fit ladder once, as a batch (`terms_design_system.md`, Applying the ladder). Later steps read these figures and do not recompute them.
   - **Area shares:** each group's or zone's portion starts from its space need (converted to an extent on the governing axis) and is bounded by its rank. Need is a floor: a share is never smaller than its content needs to be read comfortably at its scale. Rank is a ceiling: a share is never larger than its rank earns (hero group and first-rank message largest, the identity unit per its mark role, supporting then utility information progressively smaller), so area agrees with rank before any content is placed. Where need exceeds what rank allows, the fit ladder (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md` B5), not a squeezed zone, resolves it. The shares, with the free ground, margin, and separations, account for the whole format with headroom (nothing is left unassigned to become filler).
   - **Order of reservation:** the composition has already fixed where quiet separates and rests; the plan sets its proportions first (outer margin, separations between zones and groups, isolation), then the regions the longest and densest mandatory text needs (its width and line count at the legibility minimum, since it cannot compress), then the other groups, and last the hero group, up to its Phase 3 footprint, in what remains, so a group's region never includes its own separation and the hero never takes from a group's need. Fitting inside its region is never the test of adequacy; the room left around its items is.
   - **Spacing as a graded system,** each level distinct from the next:
     - an outer margin scaling with the format;
     - separations between zones and groups, clearly larger than the gaps inside a group (space is the first separator, ahead of rules and boxes);
     - isolation zones around glance content, hero, and identity unit;
     - internal spacing within each group (container padding, gaps between items, line spacing) scaled to the group's text size and reading distance;
     - relationship gaps: each pair of neighbors takes the step its relationship calls for (within a unit, between units of a group, between groups, between zones), so no gap is incidental;
     - resting fields placed to support the eye's path;
     - atmosphere fields, where decided: the free ground assigned to decoration, placed by the composition (framing, edging, or occupying ground between groups that the quiet does not need) and kept clear of the ground information is read on.
   - **Few steps.** Use the fewest spacing steps that tell the levels apart (usually three: small, medium, large); each pair of neighbors takes a step by name, never its own figure.
   - **Spacing follows type.** Three named steps, set once per group from u, the line height of that group's reading text (its body or smallest informational text; display text does not set it): **S = 0.5u** (gaps between items inside a group), **M = 1u** (container padding on every side, more for a first- or second-rank claim so its text visibly floats; spacing between units of a group), **L = 2u** (separation between groups and isolation around the hero and the unit, taking the larger neighbor's u). Line spacing follows the text's job (tight for display, about one and a third to one and a half times the text size for sustained text). The outer margin is the larger of L and the production's safe margin (Phase 2 medium reading) plus a visible gutter. Each pair of neighbors takes a step by name; space beyond the steps is allocated from what content and rank leave free, never by scaling every gap with the largest type. The steps sit inside every group's extent in the ledger, so a group's size is its text plus its breathing room and a container is sized to hold both. If the ledger does not close with them, the composition, arrangement, or content tiers change (the fit ladder); the steps do not.
   - **Scaling:** every level scales with the format's size and ratio, the viewing distance, and content density (denser content needs clearer separation between groups, not less).
   - **Distribution follows the format and the content.** Quiet is placed where content is dense and groups must be told apart, along the dimension the content is arranged in (the path the eye travels), at the proportions of this format's orientation and ratio. Open area parked in one band while other zones are tight is not breathing room: breathing room is local, judged at each group's perimeter, between neighbors, and inside each container.
   - **Top-down sizing.** Sizes derive in one direction: format, then groups and zones (area shares), then the elements in each (what remains after the group's padding and separation), then text tiers (steps between tiers). An element's wish for size never sets its group's share; an element may exceed its place only by taking from a lower-ranked group, never from quiet, margin, or separation.
   - **Residual area:** area that rank, legibility, and grouping do not call for stays free ground: quiet where separation, isolation, or rest is needed, atmosphere where the plan decided it, plain ground where plainness is the decision. It is never spent on enlarging elements or stretching content across it.
   - **If content does not fit,** take the fit ladder (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md` B5) in order; the quiet share narrows only at its last rung.

## Quality checks

**Gate checks for this step (fails if any is false):**
- Each group's space need was derived once, as an area, before the strategy, and the strategy was chosen by screening structurally different sketches (full cards for the survivors), composed around those needs with the free ground planned in them.
- Only glance content is sized for the widest reach, and lower tiers are sized for their own stage's reach.
- Every text role sits at its role floor or above it for a stated rank reason; supporting and reference roles sit at their own floors.
- The space plan is stated with proportions: area shares (each between its group's space need and its rank's ceiling) that with the free ground account for the whole format with headroom, graded spacing levels at their type-derived floors, relationship gaps, and any atmosphere fields included, and realized.
- Every quiet area has a stated job and is contiguous, free of content, and undecorated (`terms_design_system.md`, Quiet).
