# Phase 5: Design Execution, part 2 of 5: space plan, size and legibility, spatial strategy

Decide the space plan before anything is placed, derive legibility minimums from each stage, and choose the spatial strategy by the full protocol.

**Read this when:** after `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md` (stages, coupling modes, and arrangement forms decided for this deliverable).

**Inputs needed:** Stage assignment and relationship map from `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`; Phase 3 space intent, space budget (quiet share, role allocations), density level, balance type, hero hosting capacity, integration stance; Phase 4 element system; Phase 2 encounter stages with reading distances; S format and ratio.

## 2. Guidance (continued from `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`)

### B2 to B4 and B6. Space plan, size follows stage, legibility minimum, space as visibility (items 2, 3, 4, and 6 of section B)

2. **Space plan** before placing anything, as proportions of the full format at this deliverable's ratio. It honors the Phase 3 space intent.
   - **Area shares:** each zone's portion of the format follows its communication role and rank (hero group and first-rank message largest, the identity unit per its mark role, supporting then utility information progressively smaller), so area agrees with rank before any content is placed. The shares are a budget that, with the quiet share, margin, and separations, accounts for the whole format with headroom (nothing is left unassigned to become filler); each share is a ceiling its content is fitted inside, never a floor it may grow past.
   - **Order of reservation:** the quiet share, outer margin, and separations are set first, then the regions the longest and densest mandatory text needs (its width and line count at the legibility minimum, since it cannot compress), then the other zones in what remains, so the whole format is accounted for and a zone's region never includes its own separation. Fitting inside its region is never the test of adequacy; the room left around its items is.
   - **Spacing as a graded system,** each level distinct from the next:
     - an outer margin scaling with the format;
     - separations between zones and groups, clearly larger than the gaps inside a zone (space is the first separator, ahead of rules and boxes);
     - isolation zones around glance content, hero, and identity unit;
     - internal spacing within each zone (container padding, gaps between items, line spacing) scaled to the zone's text size and reading distance;
     - relationship gaps: each pair of neighbors takes the step its relationship calls for (within a unit, between units of a group, between groups, between zones), so no gap is incidental;
     - resting fields placed to support the eye's path.
   - **Scaling:** every level scales with the format's size and ratio, the viewing distance, and content density (denser content needs clearer separation between groups, not less).
   - **Distribution follows the format and the content.** Quiet is placed where content is dense and groups must be told apart, along the dimension the content is arranged in (the path the eye travels), at the proportions of this format's orientation and ratio. Open area parked in one band while other zones are tight is not breathing room: breathing room is local, judged at each zone's perimeter, between neighbors, and inside each container.
   - **Top-down sizing.** Sizes derive in one direction: format, then zones (area shares), then the elements in each zone (what remains after the zone's padding and separation), then text tiers (steps between tiers). An element's wish for size never sets its zone's share; an element may exceed its place only by taking from a lower-ranked zone, never from quiet, margin, or separation.
   - **Residual area:** area that rank, legibility, and grouping do not call for stays quiet; it is never spent on enlarging elements or stretching content across it.
   - **If content does not fit,** take the fit ladder (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md` B5) in order; the quiet share narrows only at its last rung.

3. **Size follows stage, not format.**
   - A larger format changes how much absolute area exists and, for a physical piece, how far the glance viewer stands; it does not change the reach at which approach and close content is read.
   - Each tier's legibility minimum comes from its own stage's reach, material, light, and contrast.
   - Only glance content must be understood at the widest reach, and only it is sized for it. Approach and close tiers are sized for their nearer reach and take a smaller share of the format as the format grows, because the audience that wants them comes closer.
   - Confirm each stage is reachable (Phase 2). Content whose stage is unreachable moves to glance, is demoted out, or is recorded as a volume risk.
   - Area this frees goes to the space plan (a stronger hero, isolation, resting fields, more generous separation at every level), never to extra items or enlarged lower tiers.

4. **Legibility is a minimum** per tier, from its own stage. Size beyond it buys emphasis, which works only when most elements lack it. Derive it as `terms_brief_and_context.md` (Legibility minimum) describes, and express it as a fraction of the format's governing dimension: that fraction is the tier's floor in the load and capacity checks. If the format cannot physically carry a tier's content at its reach, the content moves to a nearer stage, is demoted out, or is recorded as a volume risk before any layout is drawn.

6. **Space as visibility and scale as relationships.** Isolation raises glance visibility more reliably than size. Express scale as proportions and steps between tiers.

### C. Spatial strategy

Compare at least three structurally different strategies (different organizing logic, not a different position of the same arrangement). They differ in how content is distributed, not only where the hero sits: single or multiple columns, grid, bands, horizontal or vertical runs, clusters around the hero, chosen from content volume, relations, hierarchy, and the format. **Card fields:**
1. Where the eye enters and how it travels, including behavior at the aspect ratio (extreme ratios change the path: decide it explicitly).
2. How groups relate: zones, alignments, grid logic, and the arrangement form of each group.
3. How hero, identity unit, and information divide the format; figure-ground.
4. Balance type and where its counterweight comes from, tension, density, rhythm.
5. How the space plan is realized.
6. Edge behavior and plane order.
7. Behavior at each stage, fit with and departure from neighbors.
8. Where supporters and designed copy sit and how they relieve, group, and balance text areas.
9. Coupling: what content and supporters are integrated, attached, separate, and the resulting group count.
10. Fit: each region's estimated load against its share at legibility minimums, with headroom; a strategy that fits only by compression is eliminated.

Judge with the protocol lenses. Density, scale, and finish are judged relative to the surroundings. No universal grid, fixed zone count, or stock layout without compelling reason. Unless the stance is a hero carrying the message alone, at least one card couples the hero's related content to it; message coherence outweighs tidy zoning.
