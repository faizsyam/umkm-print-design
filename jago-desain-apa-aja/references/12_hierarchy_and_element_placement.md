# Phase 5: Design Execution, part 3 of 5: hierarchy, planes, and implementing elements

Rank every element, verify the attention sequence, decide planes and overlap, and specify role, scale, weight, color, treatment, plane, position, and relationships of every element and text item.

**Read this when:** after `11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` (space plan and strategy decided); this is step 5 of the Phase 5 process.

**Inputs needed:** Everything from `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md` and `11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md`; Phase 4 element system with purpose lines and weight ceilings; Phase 3 mark role, layering stance, tone and positioning (price visibility, scale, space cues), density, point of tension; Phase 4 counterweight.

## 2. Guidance (continued from `11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md`)

### A. Visual hierarchy and planes

Decide the order in which the audience should notice, interpret, and use the information, with text and imagery ranked together. The number of levels follows the piece, not a standard.

- **Explicit rank.**
  - Record the attention hierarchy per stage as numbered ranks (first, second, third …), each with the levers it holds.
  - Rank follows function: what the piece's job needs the viewer to get first ranks first, as the purpose lines state. Each element's levers are then set to deliver its stated effect at its stage.
  - Exactly one element is first at glance. Every element holds a rank or is removed.
  - Elements sharing a rank share their weight levers (scale, contrast, isolation, position) so they read as a set, not as rivals. Form follows content role (Phase 4), so same-rank items of different roles differ in form, not in weight.
- **Importance versus prominence.** Informational importance (what the viewer must have, mandatory status, volume) is not visual prominence (what is noticed first).
  - Prominence is the combined effect of allocated area, scale, value contrast, chroma, weight, isolation, and position on the reading path, judged for each element as drawn: its text together with its container, fill, edge, shadow, and surrounding treatment. It follows rank. A zone's area share, a text mass's weight, a container's mass, and a utility item's emphasis never exceed what its rank earns.
  - Secondary and utility information stays findable by position, grouping, and a legible tier, not by size or weight. Brand identity, hero, essential information, and utility details each hold the prominence their role earns: utility details (contacts, addresses, fine print) take the lowest prominence that stays findable and legible, and essential information (prices, hours, the action) is clear by position, grouping, and legibility, never by competing with the hero or the first-rank message.
  - A large volume of secondary text is contained by compact arrangement and by weight and contrast kept to what legibility needs, so its area mass does not outrank the hero group or the first-rank message.
- **Rank separation.**
  - Each rank leads the next on at least two levers, one of them primary (size, value contrast, isolation, position), so the step is decisive. A step that is only slight is a hedge: merge the ranks or separate them.
  - Levers are scarce: the strongest value contrast, heaviest weight, largest scale, and accent color each belong to the first rank only, because a lever spent on many elements ranks none. The first rank holds exactly one element; a hero group (the hero with its coupled text) or an equal image set chosen as the hero counts as one element. First-rank pairing is not allowed.
  - Spacing groups: distance within a group is clearly less than between groups.
- **Verification.** Predict the attention sequence from the placed composition itself, not from recorded intent.
  1. Order the elements at each stage by combined salience (allocated area, size, value contrast, chroma, weight, isolation, position on the reading path, and the container or treatment each carries, using the A3 weight estimates in `13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md`).
  2. Blur or squint the same composition.
  3. The predicted order must equal the recorded order. An inversion is remedied on the lightest lever; an element pulling more than its rank is lowered on its strongest lever; one whose effect does not register is handled by Fulfillment. A secondary element whose compound (text plus container) lands in a higher rank's salience band is lowered on its container first.
- **Hierarchy, reading sequence, and placement agree.** Three things are recorded and kept consistent: the attention hierarchy (what is noticed first, by rank), the reading sequence (the order in which the viewer takes content in, following the audience's reading path and the content's own structure), and the placement (the region each item occupies). The first-rank element is noticed first wherever it sits; the sequence then runs from it as a route along the reading path through the placed regions, without doubling back or running against the script direction, and each step lands on an adjacent region. An item whose rank puts it early in attention but whose placement puts it late on the path (or the reverse) is repositioned or re-ranked. The specification states the sequence as a route over placed regions, and Phase 6 repeats it as that route, so no ordering statement contradicts a position statement.
- **Fulfillment.** For each element, name what limits its stated effect at its stage (a neighbor taking its levers or pull, placement off the viewer's path, too little contrast, isolation, or scale for its stage's reach, a relationship that hides it) and remove that limit by the lightest means: reposition, isolate, separate levers, link, resize within rank. An element that cannot reach its effect within its rank and weight ceiling is re-ranked, re-treated (back to Phase 4 for that element), or removed. One carrying more weight than its function earns is lowered.
- **Who leads.** The hero and identity unit (per mark role) lead; evidence imagery and designed hooks follow; guides, structure, and ground stay below the required information they serve, each within its Phase 4 weight ceiling.
  - The unit's distinction comes from Phase 4 mechanisms, not size alone.
  - Its scale follows its mark role relative to the hero and the first-rank message: it stays recognizable at the stage where recognition is needed, neither dominating a hero it should anchor beneath nor shrinking below recognition.
- **Connection.** Every major element is linked to at least one other by a stated link (shared axis, overlap, shared color or shape, echoed motif, connector, proximity group), so the piece reads as one composition. Links never outrank what they connect.
- **Planes** (skip the first two rules for a single-plane stance):
  - Plane order agrees with rank.
  - The hero and lead content take the nearest plane that serves them; supporters sit on or behind what they serve.
  - An element forward for another reason is held to its weight ceiling through the other means.
  - Each plane differs from its neighbor by more than one layer cue, and one element's cues agree.
  - Only cues that survive the glance stage's widest reach and the reproduction method carry the glance stage.
- **Overlap principle.** Overlap is a depth device with four purposes: depth and rank (the front reads as nearer), integration (text and hero read as one object), connection (linking major elements), and richness.
  - **Use** it when it serves one of them and the covered element keeps its **essential part**: for text every letterform whole, for the mark the whole mark, for the hero or product the identifying silhouette and the features that prove the offer, for information whatever the viewer needs at its stage. Cover only inessential parts (redundant body of an image, ground, decoration, bleed).
  - **Do not overlap** when it hides an essential part, when the covering item lacks contrast against what it covers, when the render cannot hold it reliably, when the amount is a graze or near-touch that reads as an accident, or when looks are the only reason.
  - *Function test:* after the overlap, can each element still do its job at its stage?
  - **Clearance.** Elements not meant to overlap keep clear ground between them at the step their relationship calls for in the graded spacing system (text from its container's edge, group from group, element from the trim and from the hero's edge). A gap too small to read as either a decision to separate or a decision to overlap is a defect: widen it to the next step, or overlap decisively under the function test.

### D. Implementing elements

For each element specify role, scale, weight, color, treatment, plane, position, and the decision it traces to, plus its **relationships** to its nearest neighbors: the rail or axis it aligns to, the gap step it takes from the graded system, its relative position (beneath, beside, in front of), and its overlap or clearance status. Related elements read as related by proximity and alignment, unrelated ones are separated by the larger step, and no relationship is left to the generator.

- **Equivalent items** (entries of a list, parallel claims, repeated labels, prices) share one typographic treatment (size, weight, width class, posture, case, color role, line spacing) in whatever zone they sit, adjacent or apart.
  - Size encodes role and rank, never length. Text length never sets prominence: a shorter entry is not enlarged to fill its width, and a longer one is not reduced or compressed to fit.
  - Length is absorbed by layout (available width, column distribution, line wrapping, alignment, spacing, arrangement form, A2 in `10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`). The longest entry sets the group's fit, and the group's width, columns, or container change before any entry's type.
  - Variation is deliberate only where entries differ in importance, semantic role, emphasis, or purpose, recorded with its reason. Items that sit together but differ in role keep their differences.
- Type sizes follow encounter stages. Prices, calls to action, and contact details are functional text, clear at their own stage, not enlarged by default.
- The identity unit claims its own region with protected clear space measured from the unit.
- Specify where every mandatory text item sits and in what typographic role. Integrated items name the part they point to or follow, their side or path, and the pointer style; attached items name the group they join.
- Designed copy keeps its Phase 4 treatment (breaks, emphasis, container, color role) and never reverts to plain text in layout.
- Place supporters where they answer their deficit:
  - markers and glyphs align with the items they label, at one size;
  - pointers end on what they name and never cross;
  - a path carries only a given sequence;
  - borders and frames stay inside the safe margin unless they bleed deliberately;
  - a block or band holds one group;
  - a tag attaches to the one fact it flags without competing with the hero;
  - a scrim sits behind only the text zone it serves.
  - atmosphere fields sit where the strategy placed them, below the information in contrast and detail, and never behind required text at a strength that competes with it;
- A supplied asset the generator cannot reproduce faithfully (scannable code, supplied photograph, or logo file that cannot be attached) gets a **reserved asset panel**: its own region at the size it needs (a code sized to scan at its use distance, with quiet zone), inside the space plan, with isolation and a quiet frame in the system's colors. Never an invented stand-in.
- Figures are placed by Phase 4 role: lead = the hero; demonstrate = within the hero group, subordinate to the offer; guide = gesture or gaze ending on the target, covering no information; mood = supporter scale; in the mark = inside the unit's region. One design everywhere; a supplied figure is never redrawn.

## Quality checks

**Gate checks for this step (fails if any is false):**
- Ranks are recorded per stage with one first at glance, separated on two or more levers, with no lever spent on many elements; the predicted attention sequence equals the recorded ranks; the reading sequence is recorded as a route and agrees with ranks and placement.
- The unit has its own region.
- Designed copy and supporters are placed as specified.
- Every pointer ends on its referent; no figure covers information.
- Every major element is linked; every overlap passes the function test.
