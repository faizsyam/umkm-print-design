# Glossary part 2: copy, supporters, containers, identity, space and layering, and the full fit ladder (Phases 3 to 6)

One meaning per term for the design system (purpose line, treatment grade, supporters, containers, identity, space, layering, load) and the full fit ladder.

**Read this when:** on entering Phase 3; reopen a row or the fit ladder whenever a term or the order in which things give way is in doubt (Phases 3 to 5).

**Inputs needed:** `terms_brief_and_context.md` already read.

## Terms

One meaning per term in every file. Only terms used in more than one file are here; single-file terms are defined where used.

## Qualities, copy, supporters

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equivalent items | Text items of the same content role, rank, and function, wherever they sit (list entries, parallel claims, repeated labels, prices). They share one typographic treatment: size encodes role and rank, never text length, and layout absorbs length (width, wrapping, columns, grouping). Equivalent units presented as repeated containers (cards, rows, panels) also share outer dimensions and internal structure. Equivalence follows meaning and rank, not proximity |
| Content role | The function a text item serves for the audience: what the audience needs from it, and the success measure that tells whether it served. Roles are derived from the piece's purpose and the set is open. A role fixes the item's treatment grade and its place in the reading order |
| Purpose line | The three-part statement every element carries, made where the element is designed and read where it is placed. **Function:** what it does toward the piece's job (an objective, the message, or the weakest link) at a named encounter stage. **Loss:** what the message or viewer loses if it is absent; if nothing, it is removed or merged. **Effect:** what the viewer perceives, understands, feels, or does because of it, observable at that stage, with the success measure of its content role where it has one. Items of one role, rank, and stage may share a line; two elements with the same function merge toward the one that serves the hierarchy better. Phase 6 never receives it; the Execution Specification carries its consequences |
| Treatment grade | **Designed** (built as a graphic with its own scale play, emphasis, rhythm, container, or motif) or **set** (informational typesetting in the informational face, with the role's case, spacing, weight, color role, and alignment decided, never default text), by content role |
| Text-only baseline | The piece imagined with only its required text, identity unit, and a plain uniform ground; the reference for finding visual deficits and for reading what the piece's world would add |
| Supporter family | A class of non-text visual resource: icons and symbols; subject, in-use, or place imagery; figures; spot illustration; image treatment or set; pattern or texture; border or frame; divider; pointer, connector, or direction cue; shape block, band, tag, or edge shape; lettering-as-graphic. A tag or badge never imitates a regulated or trust mark |
| Referent | What a content item is about: the subject, the offer, the hero, a specific depicted part, or an action |
| Coupling mode | **integrated** (on, around, or pointing into the thing described), **attached** (same group, sharing its field, axis, or rail), **separate** (its own zone) |
| Hero group | The hero plus the content and supporters coupled to it, read as one object at the glance stage; its text is always subordinate to the hero |
| Rank / lever | **Rank:** an element's place in the stated order of attention at an encounter stage (first, second, third …). **Levers** are the means that create it: size, allocated area, position, color, value contrast, weight, shape or container, spacing and isolation, plane and overlap, detail density, alignment, typographic style, edge contact |
| Character carrier / link | **Character carrier:** a quiet, ownable feature that keeps a piece interesting and specific to its subject at its density (depth within a main element, a recurring signature motif, subtle surface, small iconography, typographic detail, edge or container character). **Link:** a stated tie between two major elements (shared axis, overlap, shared color or shape, echoed motif, connector, proximity group) |
| Visual weight / balance | **Visual weight:** an element's pull on the eye, roughly area share × intensity, raised by leverage (distance from center). **Balance:** the weight centroid of the composed piece sits where the chosen balance type puts it (`13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md`, A3). A **counterweight** is a quiet carrier that gains weight from area and distance, not intensity |
| Weight ceiling | The most weight (scale, contrast, color intensity, isolation, plane prominence) an element may take. Every element's ceiling sits below the required information it serves; only the hero, the identity unit (per mark role), and a designed hook or transaction lead may exceed it |
| Regulated or trust mark | Any mark whose presence a viewer would take as proof: official authorization, certification, third-party endorsement, an institution's or authority's identity, or a rating. Used only as supplied, never invented or imitated. A decorative badge form is acceptable; one built to read as an official seal is not |
| Container / shape language / form | **Container:** any form that holds, separates, flags, or frames content (panel, band, tag, badge, border, cut shape). **Shape language:** the family likeness shared by every container, rule, and supporter (geometry class, corner treatment, edge quality, line behavior), derived from the style and the subject, never from the model's default. **Form:** the specific container type, chosen per function and information role within that language. Shared language never means one form. Form includes material impression (the physical thing the container evokes, drawn from the subject's own world), edge ornament, and layering against the group or hero it holds. A container's **weight** (fill contrast, edge, padding, shadow) follows its content's rank and counts toward the prominence of the element as drawn |
| Letterform proportions | A text role's width class, posture, and baseline, decided per role. Fitting text into space never alters them |

## Identity

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wordmark / logo mark / lockup / identity unit | **Wordmark:** the name in lettering. **Logo mark:** a distinctive form that can stand for the brand without the name. **Lockup:** how they combine. **Identity unit:** the lockup designed as one object with shared construction logic and a defined spatial relationship, distinct from everything else on the piece |
| Styled name | The name in an attractive typeface with no further identity device. Not an identity |
| Personality device | An ownable, conceptually grounded feature specific to the subject, readable at small size, without which the identifier collapses to plain lettering |
| Form class | The kind of visual construction a shape belongs to. A mark reads as a mark when its form class differs from the text around it |
| Mark role | Whether the identity unit **leads** the hierarchy, **anchors** it, or **signs off** |
| Figure | A mascot, brand character, or depicted person: supplied, or created only when the user wants one. Each appearance holds one role (identify, lead, demonstrate, guide, carry mood) and one shared design |
| Identity forms | The full unit (the one identifier in a view), a symbol-only form (only where the viewer can link it to the name), a supplied name-only wordmark |

## Space and layering

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Space intent | Phase 3: the piece's provisional **space budget**, an envelope the plan stays inside and Phase 5 re-derives from the real groups and composition. **Free ground** is everything that carries no information, in two kinds. **Quiet** is genuinely open ground: contiguous, free of content, calm (uniform, tonal, or grain too fine to read as pattern at the stage's reach), large enough to read as a shape of its own, and holding a job (separating groups, isolating glance content or the hero, resting the eye, signaling positioning). **Atmosphere** is decorated ground (pattern, ornament, motif, illustration, textured or layered fields) that carries the piece's world; it is occupied by definition, never counts toward the quiet share, and exists by decision, plain ground being the other decision. The budget states the quiet share as a range with its jobs and where it tends to live; whether the style's world calls for atmosphere and roughly how much; the breathing room each group keeps; and the rough share each occupying role takes (hero group, identity unit, information groups, structure and decoration), as proportions derived from the Phase 1 load class and the Phase 2 groups' volumes and not from rank alone; Phase 5 sizes them. With the free ground these account for the whole format, with headroom, scaled to format size, orientation, and viewing distance. Leftover slivers between elements are neither quiet nor atmosphere. Space intent may differ per deliverable where formats differ enough to change it. Phase 4 applies the structure-and-decoration line (containers, supporters, counterweights, surface detail, atmosphere fields) as the **element allowance**: every element has a space cost, stated as a class (small, medium, large) and never as dimensions (its footprint plus the open ground it needs around it to read as designed), the total fits that line, and elements are removed or merged before any is shrunk |
| Space plan | Phase 5, after the spatial strategy: the chosen composition made quantitative as proportions of the format (area shares between each group's space need and its rank's ceiling, margin, separations, isolation, internal spacing, relationship gaps, resting and atmosphere fields), accounting for the whole format with headroom; it contains the axis ledger (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` B2) |
| Space need | Phase 5, per group: the area its content takes at its stage's legibility minimums plus the steps its internal hierarchy needs, with the breathing room inside it (line spacing, gaps between items, padding where contained) and the open ground around it that lets it read as one unit. Computed once, as an area share of the format (character count at the Phase 2 tier floor, with its breathing room), before any region is drawn: text area does not depend on where it wraps, so the need holds for every strategy card; the same text height is a smaller fraction of a larger format |
| Plane / layering | **Planes:** front, middle, ground (a piece may use one). **Layering** assigns elements to planes with **layer cues** (overlap, relative scale, contrast and detail, tonal and temperature step, focus, light and shadow, surface texture). Plane order is a hierarchy means and a way to integrate content with the hero. A flat style keeps cues in the picture plane: tonal steps, surface detail, overlap of flat shapes |
| Visual load | The cumulative demand on attention and space of everything drawn: information blocks, imagery, supporters, containers and their edges, designed treatments, rendering treatments, surface detail, and color intensity, counted together by category against the space budget and the stage's parsing time. Judged for the whole, never element by element. A composition fits only with headroom (see Governing axis): every item at its minimum and every gap at its tightest is not a fit |
| Fit ladder | The fixed order in which things give way when content or load exceeds the plan (full text under "Fit ladder (full)" below). Applied from Phase 3 on |
| Governing axis / axis ledger / headroom | **Governing axis:** the dimension along which a deliverable's groups stack or run (the vertical for most portrait pieces, the horizontal for a wide banner). **Axis ledger:** the space plan's check along it: each group's extent (its space need divided by the width its region gives it, wraps recorded) in reading order, plus separations and margins, totaled against the axis. Computed once, in the space plan after the strategy is chosen (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md` B2); later tests read it. **Headroom:** after the total, at least one separation step of the axis is still unassigned |
| Reading sequence | The order in which the viewer takes content in, following the audience's reading path and the content's own structure. Distinct from the **attention hierarchy** (what is noticed first, by rank) and from **placement** (the region each item occupies). The three are recorded together and agree; the sequence is stated as a route over placed regions |
| Treatment vocabulary | Phase 3: the few rendering treatments (outline, shadow, gradient, texture, highlight, dimensional effect) that the one primary visual language admits, each with its job and the roles it applies to. A treatment outside it is not used |

## Fit ladder (full)

The fixed order in which things give way when content or load exceeds the plan. Applied from Phase 3 on (Phase 3 load budget test, Phase 4 element allowance, Phase 5 load and capacity checks). Phase 5 applies it in `13_load_balance_and_capacity_checks.md`, B5.

- **Fit ladder** (when content or load exceeds the plan). Give way in this order, each rung exhausted before the next, spending the least meaning first and the quiet last:
  1. what has the least purpose or takes ground that information needs: rendering effects, surface detail, ornament, and supporters that sit on or crowd information, and supporters beyond essential deficits (atmosphere in free ground that information does not need is not spent here; it yields only after the quiet the composition needs is secured);
  2. weight of enclosure: containers to a lighter rung, merged, or dropped (spacing before rules before boxes);
  3. arrangement: grouping, columns, grids, wrapping, a more compact form (`10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`, A2), and where none fits the content, the next strategy (`11_space_plan_and_spatial_strategy.md`, C);
  4. treatment: designed text to set text, lowest rank first;
  5. stage: demote content to the later stage where it is needed (`10_stage_assignment_and_relationship_map.md`, B1);
  6. optional content, lowest value first;
  7. rescaling within rank: the hero group and identity unit down toward what their role at their stage needs (never below what the message needs), with no other tier inflated into the freed area;
  8. only then the quiet share, narrowed to the least that still isolates glance content, with a volume risk recorded for the notes.
- **Applying the ladder.** Measure the shortfall once on the axis ledger (or the area total), walk the rungs in order adding what each recovers until the shortfall closes, apply those rungs together, and re-check once. Trial layouts are not an application of the ladder.
- **Never, at any rung:** shrink a tier below its minimum; compress, stretch, or slant letterforms; delete mandatory content; resize one member of an equivalent group; take separation from a neighbor. If mandatory content cannot fit at its minimums after rung 8, `global_standards.md` section 7 decides and the volume risk is reported.

## Quality checks

- One meaning per term in every file.
- The fit ladder is applied in its order, each rung exhausted before the next; the quiet share narrows only at the last rung, and the "Never, at any rung" limits hold.
