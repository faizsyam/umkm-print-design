# Phase 1: Brief

A complete, classified, verifiable record of what is known, required, and uncertain, so no later phase guesses or invents.

**Inputs needed:** User message, attachments (logos, photos, references), prior conversation; for a revision, the log.

## 1. Process

1. Read everything given, attachments included. Capture the deliverable set, medium, and context (A); infer what is missing and tag it.
2. Capture subject and identity (B), content (C), preferences, fixed assets, exclusions, and sensitivities (D). Treat references as evidence of taste, never as templates.
3. Record every stated quality and priority in the user's words and translate it (D).
4. Sort every gap (E). Infer the inferable (tagged), pass the researchable to Phase 2, and ask about blocking and valuable gaps through the guided intake.
5. Note tensions; read the openness of each Direction decision (Open, Steered, or Fixed) into S.
6. Run the gate checks below and the hand-off check (`SKILL.md`, Checks), show the compact summary, ask for confirmation and mode, and wait. Corrections update the ledger and show only changed rows. Phase 1 closes on the user's confirmation; then create the Design Log with S and the Phase 1 Forward block.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Deliverables, medium, and context

**Deliverable set.** Every deliverable the request needs, derived from what the user must accomplish and not only from what they named. For each, record:
- its purpose in the whole request;
- its medium and use;
- its format (dimensions or ratio, orientation, sides, panels, or screens, physical area or display size);
- its relationship to the others (series, variant, adaptation, parts of one piece).

One deliverable per image that must exist; what is one image stays one; a different size at the same ratio and encounter is the same deliverable (`scope_roles_and_depth.md` section 6). When the set or a format is unspecified, derive it from medium and purpose and tag it inferred. If it would change the composition and cannot be derived, it is a blocking gap (E).

**Ratio.** Per deliverable, the user's intended ratio; if none is given, one derived from stated dimensions or from how this kind of deliverable is made and shown, tagged inferred. Each deliverable is designed and generated at its own ratio, never substituted.

**Context.**
- Record purpose and objectives.
- Record where and how each deliverable is encountered: physical or on-screen setting, orientation, who meets it and in what state, distance or display scale, dwell time, visibility, light or display conditions, neighbors.
- Record reproduction limits, meaning what making or displaying it allows: material, finish, color limits, display constraints, budget tier, quantity.
- If the setting is not stated, infer the most probable one from how the deliverable is made, shown, and reached. That inference fixes who encounters it, where, at what distance or scale, with what attention and time, under what conditions, and against what neighbors, and those fix the encounter stages (Phase 2). Tag it provisional and pass it on; ask only if plausible settings would need opposing designs.
- Record the format's area or size beside content volume as a **load read**: how heavy the content is for the format and use. Note the number of text items and groups, the longest strings and their lengths, strings that render at risk (digits, codes, non-Latin script; `terms_brief_and_context.md`, Renderability), and any required non-text content (supplied images, codes, logos). Class the load light, moderate, or heavy at the legibility reach this use probably needs (the distance or display scale it is read from), never from the content alone. A heavy read sets Full depth and is said once in the summary with the way out (a larger size, less text, or a second piece); work continues at the stated format unless the user changes it. The read drives density, space, and scale later and is the earliest capacity signal: a read that no triage could make readable in the stated format and use is a blocking gap (E), never absorbed silently.
- Record any image generator or output language the user names (Phase 6 uses it).

### B. Subject and identity

Record what is being presented and by whom (name, kind, offerings or content, operating context), the audience, the differentiators, and the positioning and existing visual identity. Record what the user said, not what the category implies.

### C. Content

Capture all content that must or may appear: names and identifiers, text of every kind, data and figures, offers or instructions, contacts and handles, required or legal statements, trust indicators. For each item record the exact string, necessity, provenance, referent, deliverable, and share of content volume.

- **Verbatim.** Keep spelling, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, price and contact formats, and non-standard brand spellings. A suspected typo is preserved and flagged for the notes. Record each contact, handle, or similar item as its data string plus any label word the user wrote beside it. The label is the user's text and stays; where a recognizable symbol would say the same thing, carrying the label by the symbol alone is offered as a flagged assumption in the summary (data stays verbatim), and applies only on the user's confirmation.
- **Never fabricate** figures, claims, testimonials, certifications, regulated or trust marks, contacts, credentials, dates, specifications, or statistics. Regulated or trust marks appear only when supplied.
- **Supplied codes** (QR, QRIS, barcode) are fixed assets: record as attached, with the size needed to scan. Never imitate one.
- **Supplied visuals and structure.**
  - Record images, depicted people, mascots, characters, and handles as fixed assets: what is fixed, and whether permission to show a depicted person is known.
  - Record whether the user wants a figure created and what it should do. An unmentioned figure is not assumed; it may be offered once as a suggestion.
  - Record structure the content already has (sequence, levels, item-value rows). Later phases may express structure visually only from what is given.
- **Generated copy.** Functional labels derived from supplied content are acceptable only where they say something the content's own form or a recognizable symbol does not; a label that a symbol already says is not added, and the data itself (a handle, number, address, time) always stays. Write a headline, tagline, or descriptor only when the piece cannot function without one or the user requested copy. It must be factual, modest, tagged creative choice, and flagged in the notes.
- **Necessity.**
  - Mandatory: the user said it must appear, or the piece cannot work without it.
  - Optional: offered or helpful.
  - Excluded: the user said to leave it out.
  - When unsure, treat as mandatory. Ask once only if the answer would change content volume; otherwise Phase 5 fits it by demotion or grouping, never omission.

### D. Preferences, constraints, quality requirements

Record style, color, mood, theme, references, assets, exclusions, and cultural or religious sensitivities.

- **Sensitivities** arise wherever a visual choice asserts a claim, invokes a belief, authority, or group, or depicts a person. For each depicted element, symbol, script, color, and mark, ask what it asserts or invokes for this audience, whether the subject may make that claim, and whether its meaning is verified. Omit what stays uncertain.
- **References.** Extract what the user responds to (structure, tone, density, material); never reproduce the reference.
- **Fixed assets.** List each with exactly what is fixed. For a supplied logo image, record form, construction, and colors precisely, so Phase 6 can describe it if the model cannot take an image.

**Quality requirements (binding).**
- Record every experiential, tonal, affective, or priority statement, and every named style, color, or technique, however vague, in the user's words. A minimal brief makes these the primary requirements.
- For each, write the **quality translation**:
  1. Visual targets in specialist terms (value key, chroma, temperature, density, formality, warmth, hierarchy priority, sensory cues).
  2. Two to four quality carriers from color and value key, shape language, surface, imagery, type treatment, spacing.
  3. Contradicting signals: the visible properties that would deliver the quality's opposite, found by reversing each target.
- A quality word has no fixed visual meaning. Its translation answers what this audience, in this setting and against these neighbors, would see that makes them say it, and what would make them say the opposite.
- Targets are observable at the stated encounter stage and, where the quality is comparative, stated relative to the neighbors.
- A translation that could apply unchanged to any subject has not been made.
- A whole-piece quality constrains the dominant field and largest masses, not only an accent.
- Qualities that pull apart are recorded as a pair, with a carrier for each side.
- Ambiguous words are interpreted by the rest of the brief and tagged.

### E. Gaps

- **Explicit:** record.
- **Inferable:** infer and tag.
- **Researchable:** pass to Phase 2 naming the decision it could change; drop it if it changes none.
- **Materially uncertain:** a **blocking gap** when it passes the clarification test (`00_conversation_voice_modes_and_intake.md` section 3) and its answer decides one of four items: which deliverable carries the message; the number or format of deliverables (when medium and purpose do not imply them); whether mandatory content exists; whether the format can contain the content (the load read, A). Ask blocking gaps, and valuable gaps (answers that change story, setting, content, or look), through the intake. Never ask what can be derived or investigated.
- Facts about the subject are never invented.
- A missing aesthetic preference is never blocking (Phase 3 decides it). A vague quality statement is not a missing preference but the requirement.

## Quality checks

**Gate checks for this step (fails if any is false):**
- Every S field listed under State (`workflow_and_state.md` section 4) is filled, tagged (given, researched, inferred), or listed as an open gap with its gap class; every mandatory string is restated, so no later phase needs the original message.
- Every mandatory string is exact; every item has necessity, provenance, and referent; fixed assets and exclusions are listed; no unsourced fact; no suggestion recorded as given.
- Every stated quality and priority is kept in the user's words with a full translation (each translation names at least one visible, checkable feature; a quality with none fails).
- The deliverable set is recorded with each deliverable's purpose, format, ratio, content volume and load read, and setting (inferred and tagged if unstated); references are recorded as taste, never layout requirements; supplied visual material is listed as fixed assets.
- Intake stayed within limits; every question passed the clarification test and, for a new user, had options or a default; none was answerable from the brief; the summary was shown with the mode question (Phase 2 starts only after the user confirms).
- Production check: does S record, for every deliverable, its medium, format, reproduction limits, and encounter conditions, and does the content volume fit the format at or above each tier's legibility minimum, or is the over-capacity raised as a blocking gap?
- The openness map is recorded (`exploration_protocol.md` section 1).
- Even a simple brief has a written ledger; simplicity shortens it, never removes it.
