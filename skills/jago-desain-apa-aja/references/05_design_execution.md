# Phase 5: Design Execution

## 1. Process

Run these steps for each deliverable, using the shared decisions as inputs.

Order logic: steps 2 to 5 run in a fixed order (stages, space plan, strategy, placement), because the strategy arranges content inside space already reserved. Balance is measured before the capacity check, because balance remedies can change sizes and positions; the capacity check then freezes the sizes.

1. Confirm the message from Phase 2. Reword it only if triage or content volume changes what the glance viewer can take away, and record the change.
2. Read the format (B9), assign every content item to an encounter stage (B1), and map referents and coupling modes (A2).
3. Decide the space plan before placing anything, reserving the hero group's field as one region (B2).
4. Decide the spatial strategy by the protocol (C).
5. Rank and place content (A, D), settling each group's arrangement form (A2) against the region the space plan gives it, with provisional sizes (each tier at its legibility minimum plus hierarchy needs). Triage if content exceeds the space plan (B4, B5). An item promoted to first or second rank with no treatment decision takes the Phase 4 treatment check (B2 there).
6. Check balance (A3) on the placed composition and remedy it.
7. Run the capacity check (B7) and freeze sizes. A remedy that moves or resizes an element re-estimates that element's balance only.
8. Decide backgrounds, reproduction conditions, and the set (E).
9. Review the composition (F). On failure, return to the earliest responsible decision and re-run forward. Gate.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Visual hierarchy and planes

Decide the order in which the audience should notice, interpret, and use the information, with text and imagery ranked together. The number of levels follows the piece, not a standard.

- **Explicit rank.**
  - Record the reading order per stage as numbered ranks (first, second, third …), each with the levers it holds.
  - Rank follows function: what the piece's job needs the viewer to get first ranks first, as the purpose lines state. Each element's levers are then set to deliver its stated effect at its stage.
  - Exactly one element is first at glance. Every element holds a rank or is removed.
  - Elements sharing a rank share their weight levers (scale, contrast, isolation, position) so they read as a set, not as rivals. Form follows content role (Phase 4), so same-rank items of different roles differ in form, not in weight.
- **Importance versus prominence.** Informational importance (what the viewer must have, mandatory status, volume) is not visual prominence (what is noticed first).
  - Prominence is the combined effect of allocated area, scale, value contrast, weight, isolation, and position on the reading path, and it follows rank. A zone's area share, a text mass's weight, and a utility item's emphasis never exceed what its rank earns.
  - Secondary and utility information stays findable by position, grouping, and a legible tier, not by size or weight.
  - A large volume of secondary text is contained by compact arrangement and by weight and contrast kept to what legibility needs, so its area mass does not outrank the hero group or the first-rank message.
- **Rank separation.**
  - Each rank leads the next on at least two levers, one of them primary (size, value contrast, isolation, position), so the step is decisive. A step that is only slight is a hedge: merge the ranks or separate them.
  - Levers are scarce: the strongest value contrast, heaviest weight, largest scale, and accent color each belong to one rank (the first, unless deliberately paired), because a lever spent on many elements ranks none.
  - Spacing groups: distance within a group is clearly less than between groups.
- **Verification.** Predict the attention sequence from the placed composition itself, not from recorded intent.
  1. Order the elements at each stage by combined salience (allocated area, size, value contrast, weight, isolation, and position on the reading path, using the A3 weight estimates).
  2. Blur or squint the same composition.
  3. The predicted order must equal the recorded order. An inversion is remedied on the lightest lever; an element pulling more than its rank is lowered on its strongest lever; one whose effect does not register is handled by Fulfillment.
- **Fulfillment.** For each element, name what limits its stated effect at its stage (a neighbor taking its levers or pull, placement off the viewer's path, too little contrast, isolation, or scale for its stage's reach, a relationship that hides it) and remove that limit by the lightest means: reposition, isolate, separate levers, link, resize within rank. An element that cannot reach its effect within its rank and weight ceiling is re-ranked, re-treated (back to Phase 4 for that element), or removed. One carrying more weight than its function earns is lowered.
- **Who leads.** The hero and identity unit (per mark role) lead; evidence imagery and designed hooks follow; guides, structure, and ground stay below the required information they serve, each within its Phase 4 weight ceiling.
  - The unit's distinction comes from Phase 4 mechanisms, not size alone.
  - Its scale follows its mark role relative to the hero and the first-rank message: it stays recognizable at the stage where recognition is needed, neither dominating a hero it should anchor beneath nor shrinking below recognition.
- **Connection.** Every major element is linked to at least one other by a stated link (shared axis, overlap, shared color or shape, echoed motif, connector, proximity group), so the piece reads as one composition. Links never outrank what they connect.
- **Planes** (skip the first two rules for a single-plane stance):
  - Plane order agrees with rank.
  - The hero and lead content take the nearest plane that serves them; supporters sit on or behind what they serve.
  - An element forward for another reason is held to its weight ceiling through the other means.
  - Each plane differs from its neighbor by more than one layer cue, and one element's cues agree.
  - Only cues that survive the glance stage's widest reach and the reproduction method carry the glance stage.
- **Overlap principle.** Overlap is a depth device with four purposes: depth and rank (the front reads as nearer), integration (text and hero read as one object), connection (linking major elements), and richness.
  - **Use** it when it serves one of them and the covered element keeps its **essential part**: for text every letterform whole, for the mark the whole mark, for the hero or product the identifying silhouette and the features that prove the offer, for information whatever the viewer needs at its stage. Cover only inessential parts (redundant body of an image, ground, decoration, bleed).
  - **Do not overlap** when it hides an essential part, when the covering item lacks contrast against what it covers, when the render cannot hold it reliably, when the amount is a graze or near-touch that reads as an accident, or when looks are the only reason.
  - *Function test:* after the overlap, can each element still do its job at its stage?

### A2. Relationship map and grouping

Group content by what it refers to, not only by when it is read; grouping by attention order alone yields a picture with captions.

**Apply the stance.** The Phase 3 integration stance stands unless a named failure appears:
- the hero cannot host an item the stance couples to it;
- more items would be integrated than stay readable (below);
- coupling would hide or crowd the hero's identifying form.

Where the hero carries the message alone, or coupling would dilute it, its text stays separate by stage and the hero keeps its isolation (the modes below then apply only to what is still coupled). On a named failure, return to Phase 3 and amend the stated stance.

**Map every text item** from its referent and content role: what it refers to, whether the hero's form can host it, and its mode.

| Mode | Use when | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated | The item names, describes, quantifies, substantiates, or dramatizes the hero or a visible part, is short, and the hero can host it | Callout with a pointer to the named part; label on or against the object; text following the hero's contour or an arc over it; a price, offer, or evidence tag overlapping or abutting the object; a headline emerging from the depicted thing |
| Attached | The item concerns the hero as a whole, or the hero cannot host it | Same group: shares the hero's field, axis, or rail; nearer the hero than any other group |
| Separate | Self-standing: identity unit, action and contact, anything about the subject as a whole that no depicted thing grounds | Its own zone, by stage |

**Rules**
- **Most specific referent.** An item refers to the most specific thing it is about or evidence of: a claim that substantiates a depicted thing refers to that thing, not to the subject. Parallel items (several items doing the same job) are separate units, each placed by its own referent in its own container. They merge into one strip or group only when the viewer should read them as a single claim. Compactness is not a reason to merge.
- **Hero stays dominant.** Integrated and attached text is subordinate in scale, weight, and contrast; the hero group reads as one object at glance, its details resolving at approach. The resting field around the hero can host the group's text, provided the isolation the hero needs and the field's open character survive. Text squeezed into a separate band beside open ground that could host it without losing either is a misallocation.
- **Balance and clarity.** Integrated items counterbalance the hero's composition (one-sided only when the asymmetry is the plan). Integrate only as many items as stay readable at approach (typically five labels at most): one rail or radial rhythm, one short uncrossed pointer style ending on the part named, text on quiet ground, never across detailed parts of the hero.
- **Verbatim.** Integration changes position and form, never wording or order. Split a string across labels only if each fragment is verbatim, in order, and reads alone; otherwise attach it whole. A group title stays with its items.
- **Group count.** Count perceptual groups at glance. Merging items that share a referent lowers the count, and units arranged around a shared referent read as one group; many equal separate bands make a list.
- **Reliability and isolation.** Use only forms the model renders reliably (one short line on one smooth arc, straight pointers, few labels). An integrated form that overlaps or wraps the hero needs a clean isolated hero edge. If doubtful, attach.
- **Row guidance.** Where a label and value sit far apart, a fine leader or shared rail ties the pair; where proximity already does, add nothing.
- **Arrangement form per group.**
  - **Choose** each group's form from its function, its relations (sequence, comparison, parallel items, label and value), content length, neighbors, and the region it occupies: stacked, inline or run-in, columns or grid, table, or clustered around its referent, with separators, markers, or icons only where they do a job (`04_design_elements.md` D).
  - **Fit to region and quantity:** a long list, or several comparable items, is tested against columns, groups, or a grid before a single stacked column, and a short run in a wide band against inline form, so neither vertical length nor line length stretches without need. A single stacked column is chosen like any other form, never as the unexamined default.
  - **Across columns or sections,** preserve logical grouping, reading order (stated as column-first or row-first to suit the reading path), alignment on shared rails, and one item form.
  - **Compactness:** compact forms are a first means of making room, ahead of shrinking type or space, but only where they keep clarity and the separation of the space plan.
  - **Consistency:** one arrangement repeated across groups of different function is a default, not a decision. Consistency comes from the shared system (alignment logic, spacing rhythm, shape language, type roles). Each zone and sub-composition is decided from its own function, then reconciled with the whole.

**Tests**
- *Referent:* each item sits nearest what it refers to. If two coupled items could trade places without changing meaning, they are only allocated, not integrated.
- *Caption:* where the stance couples, covering the text must not leave a picture with captions beneath it.

### A3. Balance

Balance is judged on the composed whole after placement. It is measured, not assumed, deliverable by deliverable (and across a spread).

- **Weight.** Estimate each element's weight as area share of the format × intensity (1 pale, low-contrast, sparse; 2 medium; 3 dark or saturated against its ground, dense, detailed), raised for isolation, a figure or face, warm saturated color, or an irregular silhouette. Rough values suffice. Pull grows with distance from center, so a small heavy element far out can offset a large one near the center. Empty area weighs nothing: a resting field balances only by letting heavy mass sit nearer the center.
- **Check.** Give each element coordinates x, y in [-1, 1] from the center (y positive downward); centroid X = Σ(w·x)/Σw, Y = Σ(w·y)/Σw.
  - Balanced: |X| ≤ 0.1 and −0.1 ≤ Y ≤ 0.2 (a slightly low centroid is stable; a high one is top-heavy).
  - Leaning: 0.1 < |X| ≤ 0.25 is acceptable only as the chosen asymmetric equilibrium or deliberate tension, with its counterweight named.
  - Beyond that, or outside the Y range: unbalanced.
  - A radial stance is read about its focal center.
  - The tolerances are a working heuristic on rough estimates, not a measurement; a result near a limit is settled by the cross-check: fold along each axis and squint, and the eye must not settle on one side or find one half emptier than planned.
- **Remedy, least new weight first.**
  1. Reposition or reorder existing elements: heavy mass toward the center, swap sides, shift a group axis, resize the hero within its rank.
  2. Rebalance within ranks: lighten the heavy side or strengthen the light side by intensity or area, without breaking the rank order or any weight ceiling.
  3. Deploy the Phase 4 counterweight on the light side, gaining weight from area and distance rather than intensity, linked to the composition, below every rank, never carrying required information.
  4. If the element system has none, return to Phase 4.

  Counterweights count against the element allowance and space intent.

### B. Scale, stages, space, density

Visibility requirements guide prioritization; they do not entitle every element to be large.

1. **Stage assignment.** Assign by when the viewer needs each item, not by how important the requester feels it is. Stages come from the Environment Profile; only glance-stage content is sized for the widest reach. Record each item's stage and tier.
2. **Space plan** before placing anything, as proportions of the full format at this deliverable's ratio. It honors the Phase 3 space intent.
   - **Area shares:** each zone's portion of the format follows its communication role and rank (hero group and first-rank message largest, the identity unit per its mark role, supporting then utility information progressively smaller), so area agrees with rank before any content is placed.
   - **Order of reservation:** the quiet reserve, outer margin, and separations are set first and the zones are laid out in the remainder, so the whole format is accounted for and a zone's region never includes its own separation. Fitting inside its region is never the test of adequacy; the room left around its items is.
   - **Spacing as a graded system,** each level distinct from the next:
     - an outer margin scaling with the format;
     - separations between zones and groups, clearly larger than the gaps inside a zone (space is the first separator, ahead of rules and boxes);
     - isolation zones around glance content, hero, and identity unit;
     - internal spacing within each zone (container padding, gaps between items, line spacing) scaled to the zone's text size and reading distance;
     - resting fields placed to support the eye's path.
   - **Scaling:** every level scales with the format's size and ratio, the viewing distance, and content density (denser content needs clearer separation between groups, not less).
   - **Residual area:** area that rank, legibility, and grouping do not call for stays quiet; it is never spent on enlarging elements or stretching content across it.
   - **If content does not fit,** change the content (triage), grouping, or sizes; the space plan shrinks only as the last resort in B5.
3. **Size follows stage, not format.**
   - A larger format changes how much absolute area exists and, for a physical piece, how far the glance viewer stands; it does not change the reach at which approach and close content is read.
   - Each tier's legibility minimum comes from its own stage's reach, material, light, and contrast.
   - Only glance content must be understood at the widest reach, and only it is sized for it. Approach and close tiers are sized for their nearer reach and take a smaller share of the format as the format grows, because the audience that wants them comes closer.
   - Confirm each stage is reachable (Phase 2). Content whose stage is unreachable moves to glance, is demoted out, or is recorded as a volume risk.
   - Area this frees goes to the space plan (a stronger hero, isolation, resting fields, more generous separation at every level), never to extra items or enlarged lower tiers.
4. **Legibility is a minimum** per tier, from its own stage. Size beyond it buys emphasis, which works only when most elements lack it. Derive it as `terms.md` (Legibility minimum) describes.
5. **Budget the content.**
   - **Per tier:** count groups and items against what the viewer can parse in that stage's time.
   - **Whole composition:** text groups, containers, supporters, illustrations, designed treatments, and surface detail together are the visual load. Each may be justified alone yet together exceed what the space plan and the stage's parsing time carry. When they do, remove or merge the lowest-ranked contributors (supporters, containers, treatments, surface detail) before any quiet is given up, and subdivide content into no more groups or containers than its own structure requires.
   - **Triage** when content exceeds the space plan: demote to a later stage, group, rearrange into a more compact form (A2), simplify, or omit optional content. Never delete mandatory content, shrink below the minimum, or inflate every tier, and do not shrink the space plan until triage is exhausted.
   - **If mandatory content still cannot fit at its minimums,** `SKILL.md` section 7 decides: content and legibility outrank space intent, so the quiet share narrows to the least that still isolates glance content, and a volume risk is recorded for the notes.
   - **Renderability:** mandatory text in the at-risk band (`terms.md`) is first made safer by design (shorter lines, larger size, simpler grouping); what remains is recorded as a rendering risk for the notes.
6. **Space as visibility and scale as relationships.** Isolation raises glance visibility more reliably than size. Express scale as proportions and steps between tiers.
7. **Capacity check (before any size is stated).**
   - **Estimate:** for each text string, width = characters × cap height × a width factor for the role's decided width class (about 0.75 to 0.9 for text faces, up to about 1.0 for heavy or wide display faces), against its inner container area after padding at the planned size, with headroom (a string that only just fits counts as not fitting, since a render short of room compresses or skews letters). Count lines against container height.
   - **On failure change, in order:** line breaks and grouping, container size or shape, tier size down to (never below) its minimum, triage.
   - **Remedies act on the group or role,** never on one member: the longest member sets the group's fit, and no single member is resized, reweighted, or compressed to pass. Letterform proportions and baseline are never a remedy.
   - **Then** record final sizes; the sizes recorded at this gate are the sizes the prompt uses. The check runs after balance (A3).
8. **Proportion, fill, and zone checks.**
   - **Proportion:** reduce any role or group whose size sits far above its minimum without being the hero or the glance message (as a whole, never member by member).
   - **Fill test:** every quiet area has a job (rest, isolation, separation, framing the hero, or hosting the hero group's text within the A2 limit) or is reassigned in the plan, never filled with an item. The Phase 4 ground's surface character may run through it.
   - **Zone test:** each zone's internal spacing is set at its own scale and reading distance, in proportion to the text it holds. A zone whose spacing is only what remained after sizing is re-planned, by arrangement and grouping first. Content sits inside its allocated region with resting room left, never at its edges. A zone that needs more room finds it within its own content (arrangement, grouping, triage), never by taking separation from its neighbors.
9. **Format.** The deliverable's format (proportion, orientation, display scale) is a design input, not a container. It fixes which regions exist for the hero group, text, and quiet space, how far apart glance and approach content sit, the reading path, and where weight can balance.
   - Compose for this format; never derive it by scaling or cropping another deliverable's composition. Across the set what transfers is decisions (identity, style, color, type, element system, shared hero treatment), not layout.
   - A size change at the same ratio and encounter changes nothing; a changed ratio, orientation, or encounter changes reach, regions, and path and is recomposed.
   - Extreme proportions change the strategy path (C): decide it explicitly.

### C. Spatial strategy

Compare at least three structurally different strategies (different organizing logic, not a different position of the same arrangement). **Card fields:**
1. Where the eye enters and how it travels, including behavior at the aspect ratio (extreme ratios change the path: decide it explicitly).
2. How groups relate: zones, alignments, grid logic, and the arrangement form of each group.
3. How hero, identity unit, and information divide the format; figure-ground.
4. Balance type and where its counterweight comes from, tension, density, rhythm.
5. How the space plan is realized.
6. Edge behavior and plane order.
7. Behavior at each stage, fit with and departure from neighbors.
8. Where supporters and designed copy sit and how they relieve, group, and balance text areas.
9. Coupling: what content and supporters are integrated, attached, separate, and the resulting group count.

Judge with the protocol lenses. Density, scale, and finish are judged relative to the surroundings. No universal grid, fixed zone count, or stock layout without compelling reason. Unless the stance is a hero carrying the message alone, at least one card couples the hero's related content to it; message coherence outweighs tidy zoning.

### D. Implementing elements

For each element specify role, scale, weight, color, treatment, plane, position, relationship, and the decision it traces to.

- **Equivalent items** (entries of a list, parallel claims, repeated labels, prices) share one typographic treatment (size, weight, width class, posture, case, color role, line spacing) in whatever zone they sit, adjacent or apart.
  - Text length never sets prominence: a shorter entry is not enlarged to fill its width, and a longer one is not reduced or compressed to fit.
  - Length is absorbed by layout (available width, column distribution, line wrapping, alignment, spacing, arrangement form, A2). The longest entry sets the group's fit, and the group's width, columns, or container change before any entry's type.
  - Variation is deliberate only where entries differ in importance, semantic role, emphasis, or purpose, recorded with its reason. Items that sit together but differ in role keep their differences.
- Type sizes follow encounter stages. Prices, calls to action, and contact details are functional text, clear at their own stage, not enlarged by default.
- The identity unit claims its own region with protected clear space measured from the unit.
- Specify where every mandatory text item sits and in what typographic role. Integrated items name the part they point to or follow, their side or path, and the pointer style; attached items name the group they join.
- Designed copy keeps its Phase 4 treatment (breaks, emphasis, container, color role) and never reverts to plain text in layout.
- Place supporters where they answer their deficit:
  - markers and glyphs align with the items they label, at one size;
  - pointers end on what they name and never cross;
  - a path carries only a given sequence;
  - borders and frames stay inside the safe margin unless they bleed deliberately;
  - a block or band holds one group;
  - a tag attaches to the one fact it flags without competing with the hero;
  - a scrim sits behind only the text zone it serves.
- A supplied asset the generator cannot reproduce faithfully (QR or QRIS, barcode, supplied photograph, unattachable logo file) gets a **reserved asset panel**: its own region at the size it needs (a code sized to scan at its use distance, with quiet zone), inside the space plan, with isolation and a quiet frame in the system's colors. Never an invented stand-in.
- Figures are placed by Phase 4 role: lead = the hero; demonstrate = within the hero group, subordinate to the offer; guide = gesture or gaze ending on the target, covering no information; mood = supporter scale; in the mark = inside the unit's region. One design everywhere; a supplied figure is never redrawn.

### E. Backgrounds, reproduction conditions, the set

- **Backgrounds and transitions:** continuity and contrast across zones; transitions between major areas; quiet where information lives. Realize the Phase 4 ground, scrim, edge behavior, zone transitions, and surface only where decided, subordinate to the hierarchy.
- **Reproduction conditions:** apply what the medium reading imposes (what making, reproducing, or displaying does to the artwork between the generated image and the audience), and carry forward only what changes the look or the generation.
  - Wherever the medium can lose or cover the edge (trimming, cropping, rounding, overlays), keep mandatory content, the identity unit, and fine borders inside the safe margin; an element that deliberately bleeds meets none of them.
  - Bleed is visual direction only, never guides or marks in the prompt.
  - For a physically produced piece, add: viewing distance per stage, ambient light, material and finish, handling and exposure, format behavior (folds, panels, curvature), and trim tolerance.
- **Across the set:** shared identity established once, with the identity form each deliverable uses and one figure design; a distinct purpose per deliverable; consistent treatment; per deliverable its own stage assignment, space plan, and strategy; one standalone prompt per deliverable.

### F. Composition review

Review the composed whole once against the checks below, each read through the role that owns it (`SKILL.md` section 2); a borderline check draws that role's objection. Check:

- **Message and hierarchy.**
  - The message is understood at first glance.
  - The predicted attention sequence (A, Verification) equals the recorded ranks, and no element, secondary or utility information included, competes with its rank.
  - The piece carries character at its density, not blank or category-generic.
  - The unit is integrated, distinctive, and passes the text-neighborhood test.
- **Quality and traceability.**
  - Every stated quality requirement reads off the composed whole at the glance stage, value key included (recompute the key only if this phase changed the area of ground, bands, or hero).
  - Every element has a distinct purpose, and no decoration outranks the required information it serves.
  - Every placement traces to a plan or element decision, and every plan commitment is visibly realized.
- **Legibility, balance, space.** Legibility; balance as measured in A3; completeness; proportion and space (realized space matches the plan).
- **Context fit.** Fit with actual neighbors and light while departing on the intended dimension; would it fail unchanged for another subject?
- **Relationships.**
  - Coupled items sit nearest their referents; no picture-with-captions bands; the hero is dominant (or isolated and dominant where it carries the message alone).
  - Arrangement form is chosen per group from its function, not repeated by default.
  - No zone is crowded at its own scale or takes separation from its neighbors; area shares agree with rank.
  - Cumulative visual load stays within what the space plan carries, and the open regions are contiguous and calm.
- **Plan features.** The point of tension, if the plan set one, reads at glance. Layering: plane order reads at glance, agrees with hierarchy, leaves required information unoccluded, and a single plane is the style's choice with surface richness carried.
- **Decided, not defaulted.** Every look-shaping attribute (`terms.md`) traces to a decision; nothing is generic, templated, or overdecorated.
- **Typography and containers.**
  - Equivalent items share one treatment whatever their length.
  - Text keeps each role's decided proportions and level baseline, with no string compressed, stretched, or slanted to fit.
  - Containers differ by information role and rank; parallel claims are separate units unless read as one.
- **Effect.** Each element's recorded effect registers at its stage, and none carries more weight than its function earns.
- **Across the set.** Shared decisions appear identically in every deliverable, and composition differs only as format or encounter requires.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- A designer could lay out the piece from the specification. The strategy was chosen from three structurally different cards. Ranks are recorded per stage with one first at glance, separated on two or more levers, with no lever spent on many elements; the predicted attention sequence equals the recorded ranks.
- Stage assignment is recorded. Only glance content is sized for the widest reach, and lower tiers are sized for their own stage's reach. Legibility minimums are respected and triage is used only as defined; no mandatory content is deleted.
- The capacity check passed before sizes were stated; no size changed after, and no letterform was altered to fit; remedies acted on groups, not on individual members.
- The space plan is stated with proportions, area shares by role and graded spacing levels included, and realized. Every quiet area has a job and reads as contiguous, calm relief. The unit has its own region. The space plan shrank only as the B5 last resort.
- Designed copy and supporters are placed as specified. No deliverable's composition was derived by resizing or cropping another's. Bleed never meets mandatory content, the unit, or fine borders. Every pointer ends on its referent; no figure covers information.
- Balance was measured in A3 and remedied in order, with any counterweight named. Every major element is linked; every overlap passes the function test; the composition review passed; failures were routed to their earliest cause.
- Objection raised and resolved (owner of the riskiest decision: visual communication specialist, production specialist, audience researcher, or senior art director; two objections at Full depth).

**Entry fields** (the source for the user's table; `00_conversation_ux.md` section 5 decides what is shown): the reading order in brief (what is taken in at each encounter stage); what is attached to the picture and what stands apart; the arrangement chosen with at most two alternatives and their weakness; and the share of quiet space (rows for a deliverable only where it differs). The capacity check passes silently.
