## 1. Process

1. Set the plan criteria, decide brand design and theme (A), and survey territories (B).
2. Decide the core idea (C), then style (D), then hero (E), each by the full protocol.
3. Decide the governing principles and confirm how each Phase 2 tension is carried (F).
4. Evaluate the assembled plan as one system and run the stress tests (G); gate.

## 2. Guidance

### Plan criteria (set before any card)

Fix what every card is judged against, so no choice is made blind to a constraint it must meet.
1. **Space envelope** (provisional space intent): a range for the quiet share of the format and the job that space does. Derive it from format size and proportion, encounter stages, content volume at legibility minimums, and the positioning the brief implies. It must pass the rough capacity test (F). F finalizes it once the hero's space cost is known.
2. **Tension leads:** for each Phase 2 tension still open, the side that leads (by `SKILL.md` section 7, the user's priority, and the Communication Problem) and the minimum the other side keeps.
3. **Carry targets:** the Phase 2 message and the quality requirements the core idea must be able to carry.

Criteria are constraints on candidates, not decisions with cards. Revise one only when a card shows it was infeasible, and record the change.

### A. Brand design and theme

Compare two ways to express the brand's impression in this environment. Then state **brand design** and **theme** separately, so the theme can vary without damaging the brand:
- **brand design:** the enduring identity. Fixed assets bind it; for a new brand, propose it with restraint proportional to evidence.
- **theme:** the idea for this piece, built on the brand design.

Check that the theme does not contradict the brand design (otherwise the theme is wrong, or the brand needs a deliberate, approved evolution) and that it leaves room for a specific core idea.

### B. Territory survey

Shallow options come from the model's category associations; widen the field before writing any card. Survey territories across source families (Full: five territories from three families; Focused: three from two):
- historical and movement lineages in design, illustration, lettering, and print;
- regional and vernacular traditions of the subject's place or community;
- craft, material, and production traditions;
- contemporary practice in other categories and regions;
- the subject's own world (inventory and profile).

Record each territory in one table row:
- defining traits (depiction mode, technique, line and form behavior, material and surface, light, chromatic logic, typographic stance, compositional behavior);
- the lineage and conditions it arose from, so it is used with understanding, not as surface;
- where it is true to this subject and where it would be imported whole;
- behavior at glance and approach in the real setting;
- reproduction and renderability demands.

With search tools, look at real precedents first; without them, reason from knowledge and mark the survey provisional. Study precedents for principles, never copy a competitor or an existing design. Verify any culturally loaded territory or omit it.

### C. Core idea

A core idea relates something true about the subject to something the audience recognizes, in a way that suggests a visual structure. A mood word or product category is not one. Cards differ on a structural axis (what carries the identity, what the viewer does first, how information is structured, register, image-making method).

**Card fields:**
1. the idea in one sentence;
2. source (territory, inventory item, departure dimension, weakest link, differentiator, name or origin);
3. the true thing and the audience recognition it relates;
4. the visual structure it suggests;
5. behavior in the setting at each encounter stage;
6. risks (renderability, capacity);
7. compatibility with each quality requirement.

**Five properties** every leader must show:
- **specific:** this subject's;
- **visual;**
- **generative:** theme, hero, style, and layout can follow;
- **truthful;**
- **functional:** helps the piece work in its setting and reads at the glance stage without being decoded.

Also check its interaction with brand design and theme.

### D. Style

One synthesis resolved across seven dimensions, each in specialist terms:
1. **Visual language:** form and line behavior, corner and edge geometry (the root of the shape language), abstraction, geometric or organic.
2. **Influences,** named by lineage and the traits borrowed.
3. **Compositional behavior:** axis, alignment, grid logic, figure-ground, plane behavior and layer cues, balance type (symmetric, asymmetric equilibrium, radial, deliberate tension), tension, rhythm.
4. **Material character:** surface, texture, the physical process evoked.
5. **Image-making technique and rendering logic,** stated as a specific language for each image type the piece uses, derived from this brand and its territories and never a medium or category name:
   - illustration: lineage, proportions, linework, rendering, texture, detail level, color treatment, degree of stylization;
   - photography: framing, light, grade, finish, retouch level;
   - mixed media: how the media combine.
6. **Typographic expression:** classification, stroke contrast, proportion, spacing character.
7. **Light, atmosphere, chromatic stance:** light direction and quality, tonal key, value structure, temperature and harmony logic (Phase 4's palette must realize it).

**Card:** one entry per dimension, plus territories drawn on, the core idea realized, and quality compatibility. Mood adjectives are not a card. For each dimension of the leading cards, compare at least two options and repair any conflicting dimension before choosing. Style comes from synthesis of this brief's conditions, not arbitrary additions; a familiar combination needs an evaluated justification.

### E. Hero

- **Options:** symbolic marks, objects, products, in-use scenes, places, figures (supplied or wanted), illustrations, photography, abstract forms, typography-led concepts, an equal set of images, or no hero.
- **When to choose a hero:** only when one subject deserves primary attention and a strong, renderable depiction exists. When several messages weigh equally or no suitable depiction exists, choose no hero or a small equal set, never a forced focal image.
- **Figures:** a figure leads only when supplied or wanted, as a designed character (Phase 4), never a stock figure or a real person's likeness.
- **Depiction mode** follows what must be true: where the viewer must see exactly what is sold, a mode that stylizes it away is ineligible.
- **Standalone logo request:** the mark is the hero by definition (the protocol still runs for idea and style).

**Card:**
1. What it is, down to the true specifics (form, material, construction, arrangement), with depiction mode and technique.
2. What it truthfully represents.
3. The stage at which it registers.
4. Which mark roles it permits (leads, anchors, signs off) and which would crowd it.
5. Renderability (`terms.md`), avoiding real identifiable people and dependence on complex hands, crowds, or fine faces.
6. Space cost against space intent.
7. Quality compatibility.
8. Purpose line (`terms.md`): the function it serves toward the message, what is lost without it, and the effect it must have at its stage.
9. Hosting capacity:
   - what its form and visible parts can carry (text along or over its contour, callouts to its named parts, an overlapping tag);
   - which related content or supporters, from S and the inventory, would reinforce its message, clarify its value, or give context, at what scale and edge behavior (isolated, or running to the trim);
   - which parts may sit in front of or behind text.

The hero's scale, crop, and edge behavior must express the message, not merely fit: a hero too small or contained for its message fails even if attractive.

### F. Governing principles (the stance sheet)

Each is a decision, not a default.
- **Compared** principles: two or more options, one line of strength and weakness each, judged against the core idea, style, hero, and quality requirements (compact comparison).
- **Decided directly:** a one-line basis.
- The depth table in `SKILL.md` section 6 says which are compared.

**Compared at every depth**
- **Space intent.**
  - **Definition:** the share of the format that stays quiet and its job (separate glance content, rest the eye, signal positioning), and the breathing room each zone keeps. What counts as quiet is defined in `terms.md` (Space intent).
  - **Derived from** format size and proportion, encounter stages, content volume, and positioning: the plan-criteria envelope, confirmed or narrowed once the hero's space cost is known.
  - **Scale:** larger media raise space intent or the strength of the main idea, not element count. Space intent is a floor: area the content does not need stays quiet and is never spent on enlargement.
  - **Rough capacity test:** mandatory content at legibility minimums must fit the non-quiet share. If not, plan triage and compact grouping now, and lower the space intent only when triage is exhausted.
- **Imagery stance and supporter budget.** Which supporter families are open, the share of the format they may take, and their shared form language.
  - An open family is a permission, never an obligation.
  - Supporters rank below the hero, identity unit, and required information they serve.
  - Heavy text raises the need for relief, never licenses clutter.
  - A whole family is excluded only for a stated reason (brief, style, production, culture).
- **Integration stance.** Whether the hero is composed with related content and supporters (integrated or attached) or stands alone in separate zones.
  - Presumption to test, not assume: content that labels, explains, prices, or substantiates the hero, and supporters that clarify its value or context, belong with it, drawn only from supplied or true material.
  - Exception: a hero that carries the message alone, or one that coupling would dilute (competing for its edge, hiding its form, shrinking it). There, separate zones with isolation are stronger.
  - A standalone hero is a stated decision with its reason, never the unconsidered default.
  - Decide from the core idea, hosting capacity, and content inventory.
- **Mark role.** Whether the identity unit leads, anchors, or signs off, against the Communication Problem, chosen from the roles the hero permits, with its purpose line. It fixes the weight Phases 4 and 5 give it.

**Compared at Full depth (direct at Focused)**
- **Layering stance.** One to three planes, what each carries (hero, information, supporters, base field), and how strongly the order is expressed. Plane order ranks content, integrates it with the hero, and adds richness.
  - Compare options with a single plane among them; more planes only for a named gain.
  - A flat style keeps one picture plane and gains richness from tonal steps, quiet surface detail, and overlap of flat shapes.
  - Overlap is allowed wherever it passes the function test (`05_design_execution.md` A). Layering adds no element beyond space intent, and a plane's added prominence counts against the weight ceiling.
- **Tone and positioning.**
  - Tone: personality and register as checkable qualities (formality, warmth, assertiveness), governing typographic stance and the hero's manner. A tone signaling the opposite of a stated quality is eliminated.
  - Positioning: where the offer sits from economical to premium, and the cues that signal it together (restraint or abundance, finish, scale, space, price visibility). It is honest to the offer; one contradicting cue undoes the rest.
- **Density and rhythm.**
  - Density: a stated level (element count, information grain), bounded by space intent and the hero's need to dominate. Content beyond it is triaged now (cut, merge, demote), not shrunk. Higher density demands stricter grouping, never more decoration.
  - Rhythm: the glance stage needs a clear first beat; uniform loudness or quiet is no rhythm.

**Decided directly**
- **Character and connection.** Name the ownable character carriers the plan relies on, each drawn from the core idea, inventory, or survey, and how major elements will be linked. Required at every density: the sparser or calmer the plan, the more each element must carry. Quality compatibility is not enough; the plan must also pass "with the name covered, could this belong to any other subject of its kind at this density?"
- **Boundaries.** What the design must never become, as qualities and treatments, drawn from the apparent options and neighbors' look (the model's included), then from brief, production, and culture. Each is realized later as a decided opposite trait, not a bare exclusion.
- **Point of tension.** At most one deliberate break from the plan's own coherent system (scale, contrast, placement, or concept). It is carried only by an element allowed to lead, is compatible with every quality requirement, and lowers reading effort or raises delight at the glance stage. None is the answer when the core idea, hero, or departure already supplies the unexpected, or when style and qualities call for calm.
- **Figure stance** (only when a figure is supplied or wanted). Roles: identify as part of the mark, lead as hero, demonstrate, guide, carry mood. Each role answers a need no other element answers; one figure serves all roles; it is subordinate to required information except as hero or mark. An unmentioned figure is offered once as a suggestion where a real deficit calls for it, and never enters unconfirmed.
- **Quality carriers.** Assign each quality requirement's carriers to plan decisions (color and value key, style, hero, density, tone). A requirement with none is a gap.

**Carry the Phase 2 tensions** one by one: confirm the lead side set in the plan criteria against the assembled plan and record what the other side keeps. A lead the plan cannot honor returns to the decision that broke it.

### G. Assembled-plan evaluation and stress tests

Check the plan as one system:
- Do brand design, theme, idea, style, hero, and principles reinforce one another?
- Does the plan answer the weakest link in the real setting?
- Do content volume, space intent, medium, budget, and renderability fit together?
- Do accepted costs compound?

Return to the responsible decision on a conflict (two cycles per issue). Then run and record:
1. **Swap / category:** with a competitor's name, or the product removed, does it still work unchanged? If so it is generic or stereotyped.
2. **Context-swap:** is it equally right in another setting? Then it ignores the environment.
3. **Distance and attention:** does the first impression hold at the glance stage in the real setting?
4. **Contradiction scan** (`quality_control.md` section 1): do all plan decisions agree?
5. **Ambition:** does it fit content, budget, and format?
6. **Brief-quality** (`quality_control.md` section 1), against the carrier map: does each stated quality have a carrier at the glance stage, and does any decision signal its opposite? This run is also the gate's quality check.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- The survey meets the depth minimum; no direction derives from a competitor or existing design; no territory is used as surface without its lineage understood.
- Every card is filled in specialist terms; no apparent option won without a decisive criterion; the leaders passed the protocol's three tests; every material weakness has a move or an accepted cost.
- The core idea fits one sentence and shows the five properties. Style is one synthesis an illustrator or printer could act on, its image-making language specific to this brand. The hero registers at its stage, truthfully represents what is sold, is not forced, and has its reinforcing content and supporters decided or a stated reason to stand alone. A figure exists only if supplied or wanted, and has a role.
- Compared principles show their options. Space intent states share and job and passed the capacity estimate. Imagery stance leaves open the families Phase 4 may need and excludes none by blanket default.
- Character carriers and linking logic are named; six stress tests are recorded; every quality requirement has carriers in the plan; every Phase 2 tension is carried.
- Plan criteria were set before the first card, and any change to them is recorded.
- Objection raised and resolved (owner of the riskiest decision: senior art director or brand strategist; two objections at Full depth).

**Entry fields** (the source for the user's table; `00_conversation_ux.md` section 5 decides what is shown): for core idea, style, hero, and the principles that had real alternatives (space intent and mark role always): the choice, a one-sentence reason in the subject's terms (the decisive criterion in plain words), and at most two alternatives with their weakness, the obvious option included when weighed. Add one line on how each stated quality is carried. Mark decisions fixed by the user or evidence as such.
