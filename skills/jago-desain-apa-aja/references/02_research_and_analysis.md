# Phase 2: Analysis

## Phase card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | Establish the piece's real problem and setting well enough that every later decision rests on a finding, reduced to design implications plus two creative inputs: the Environment Profile and the Visual source inventory |
| Receives | S and the Phase 1 Forward block (research questions, tensions, open flags). Outside: research, only where it can change a decision |
| Decisions | Which research questions to pursue; differentiation strategy (or execution-led); weakest link; the message; medium reading and encounter stages; departure and fit dimensions; Visual source inventory; design implications |

S is not restated |

## 1. Process

1. Triage research questions: keep only those naming a decision they inform.
2. Read the brand (A), find differentiation (B), build the Environment Profile (C), the audience encounter (D), and the Communication Problem (E).
3. Research (F) early enough that C and G rest on it, then ground culturally and build the Visual source inventory (G).
4. Convert findings into design implications ("because [finding], the design must / should / must avoid …", tagged); discard any finding that yields none. Carry the Phase 1 quality translations forward as given implications.
5. Gate.

## 2. Guidance

### A. The subject as it is
Values, personality, and positioning as shown by what the subject does and says; the credibility it actually holds (origin, skill, history, proof supplied); the commissioner's aspirations; the relationship with the audience. Separate established fact from plausible interpretation and aspiration from reality. A reading richer than its evidence is downgraded. Record the traits to express and the claims not to make.

### B. Differentiation
List candidate differentiators from the subject's specifics, process, materials, origin, scale and intimacy, behavior, offer logic, location, people, name, signature item, ritual, and what each peer already claims. Test each: **true** (the subject can honestly claim it; failure discards it), **relevant** (the audience cares at the moment of encounter), **distinctive** (not shared by dozens of peers), **visualizable** (can become something seen; a failure may still shape tone or hierarchy). Choose a strategy; if none is strong, choose execution-led differentiation (the piece differs through how it is composed and made) and say so. Record options with test results.

### C. Environment Profile (governing parameter)
One profile per distinct context; deliverables that share a context share it. Start from the Phase 1 inference and test it against the user's account and research. Derive each part by reasoning from how the deliverable is made, shown, and reached, never from its medium's name.
- **Encounter:** where and how the deliverable reaches its audience; what surrounds it (neighbors' dominant colors, density, scale, order or clutter, finish; competing stimuli; the physical or on-screen setting); the audience's state there (goal, passive or active, moving or still, attention and time, distance or display scale, light or display conditions, cultural context); and the reading path (the audience's script direction and habits).
- **Encounter stages** (`terms.md`): the ordered moments of engagement that follow from the encounter, each with what the audience can perceive, the attention and time it has, what it is trying to decide or do, and whether it can reach the stage (a stage the audience cannot reach cannot carry content). Derive their number and nature; do not assume the usual three.
- **Medium reading:** what the medium **imposes** (what making, reproducing, or displaying does to an image between the generated file and the audience, and the limits it sets), what the audience **relies on** (conventions learned for this kind of piece: structure, reading path, where information is expected), and what it **affords** (what this medium can do that others cannot). Record only what changes a decision. No medium convention is applied by default (`SKILL.md` principle 10): keep a convention where departing costs the audience effort or credibility; depart where it is the apparent option and the purpose gains.
- Hold the **impression** constant; let the setting decide the **expression**. The impression is what the viewer should feel and conclude about the subject (from the desired perception and the quality requirements); the expression is the visual means that deliver it (palette, density, scale, form), chosen to suit this context.
- Find where neighbors are uniform on a dimension (color, density, scale, order, finish, form). Distinctness is relative to neighbors on a dimension and can lie in either direction; more intensity is not by itself departure. Then record the **fit dimensions**: what must still match the context (conventions the audience relies on, credibility cues) so the piece is not rejected or misread.
- Choose the **departure** dimensions where the piece differs and the **fit** dimensions where it conforms. The departure is a decision with a reason, never a default.
- If the context cannot be established, state the most probable one and mark it provisional.

### D. Audience encounter
Cultural references, behavior, motivations, decision considerations, the language register the audience trusts, and the trust cues the context provides or lacks. Record what the audience does and must conclude at each encounter stage. Drop demographic stereotypes and unsupported psychology.

### E. Communication Problem
Objectives (a user-stated priority fixes the primary objective; the weakest link is chosen within it and never replaces it); desired response; necessary information; desired perception of the subject (quality requirements included). List tensions: pairs of needs that compete for the same limited resource (attention at the glance stage, space, legibility distance, budget, register), each stated as the two needs and the resource they share. Choose the **weakest link** in the chain from encounter to the intended outcome (attention → comprehension → credibility → action, keeping the links the purpose needs): the link most likely to fail in this context, weighing the cost of each failure. Test the brief's own assumptions (the deliverables asked for, the audience assumed, the success measure): where one would undermine the objective, record it as a tension and handle it per `SKILL.md` section 7. Where `SKILL.md` section 7 or the user's stated priority decides a tension, record its lead side; Phase 3 decides the rest. **Message:** one sentence naming what the glance-stage viewer takes away and does next, compressed from the desired response and the necessary information; it serves the primary objective and never contradicts a quality requirement. Phase 3 builds the core idea to carry it; Phase 5 confirms it after triage.

### F. Research
Only what can change a decision; stop at diminishing returns; run independent searches together. Priority: the subject and its public materials; visual conventions of the category and medium to differ from or conform to; peers and alternatives; the environment; cultural references; the offering's reality. Prefer authoritative and first-hand sources; reviews show perception, not fact; confirm a found subject is the right one (same name is not same subject); competitor visuals are a baseline to differ from, never a model. Without research tools, reason from the brief, mark conclusions provisional, and avoid choices that depend on unverified specifics. Record findings with provenance and the decision each informs.

### G. Cultural grounding and the Visual source inventory
Identify the cultures, languages, religions, and commercial conventions actually relevant (a national or ethnic label names many aesthetics and audiences); preserve the user's register; use regional or traditional references only where the subject genuinely connects to them. Mine deeper specifics across seven dimensions: practices and rituals; materials and tools; how things are made, handled, and shared; spatial and sensory details of daily life; language and its play; geography and seasons; the history of this subject. Screen each: **true** (the subject can claim it), **respectful** (no tokenism), **meaning verified** (omit a culturally loaded symbol if uncertain), **workable** (can be seen, drawn, and rendered at distance), and how it can be depicted truthfully (the offer, in use, a place, or from supplied imagery; a depiction the generator cannot render accurately is not workable). A figure the user wants is sourced from the same true specifics, never the category's stock mascot.

Record the inventory in three parts: (1) apparent associations as baseline; (2) deeper true specifics that could become visual ideas (at least one beyond the baseline); (3) which are visually workable.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- Every finding changes or confirms a decision; no narrative richer than its evidence; every implication names its evidence.
- Differentiation fits this subject, not dozens of competitors, and shows its option set (or "fixed by evidence"); no manufactured alternatives.
- Someone who never visited could picture the context and name where the piece departs and fits; the profile is concrete, not applicable to any context; departure is more than "bigger or brighter" and compatible with every quality requirement.
- Primary objective follows the user's stated priority; the weakest link is a secondary lens, not a replacement.
- Inventory goes beyond emblems, stays true and respectful; no unverified symbol; no competitor treated as a model; audience claims rest on evidence.
- The message is one takeaway, serves the primary objective, and contradicts no quality requirement.
- Objection raised and resolved (owner of the riskiest decision: brand strategist or audience researcher; two objections at Full depth).

**Entry fields:** in the phase card; mark decisions fixed by evidence as such.
