# Phase 1: Brief

## Phase card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | A complete, classified, verifiable record of what is known, required, and uncertain, so no later phase guesses or invents |
| Receives | User message, attachments (logos, photos, references), prior conversation; for a revision, the log |
| Decisions | The deliverable set (each deliverable's purpose, format, ratio); necessity and provenance of every item; verbatim form of every mandatory string; gap class of every gap and which are blocking; quality translation of every stated quality and priority; intake questions; depth |

| Output | Brief Ledger (with the deliverable set and quality translations), research questions (each naming the decision it could change), tensions; then the guided intake and the confirmed summary and mode (`00_conversation_ux.md` section 3). | Forward | S (ledger fields, deliverable set, quality translations, depth, mode, user language and literacy); research questions; tensions; open flags (inferred setting and other tagged assumptions) |

## 1. Process

1. Read everything given, attachments included. Capture the deliverable set, medium, and context (A); infer what is missing and tag it.
2. Capture subject and identity (B), content (C), preferences, fixed assets, exclusions, sensitivities (D). Treat references as evidence of taste, never templates.
3. Record every stated quality and priority in the user's words and translate it (D).
4. Sort every gap (E). Infer the inferable (tagged), pass the researchable to Phase 2, ask about blocking and valuable gaps through the guided intake.
5. Note tensions; set the depth.
6. Run the gate checks (`quality_control.md` section 1), show the compact summary, ask for confirmation and mode, and wait. Corrections update the ledger and show only changed rows. Phase 1 closes on the user's confirmation; then create the Design Log with S and the Phase 1 Forward block.

## 2. Guidance

### A. Deliverables, medium, and context
**Deliverable set.** Every deliverable the request needs, derived from what the user must accomplish and not only from what they named. For each: its purpose in the whole request; its medium and use; its format (dimensions or ratio, orientation, sides, panels, or screens, physical area or display size); and its relationship to the others (series, variant, adaptation, parts of one piece). One deliverable per image that must exist; what is one image stays one; a different size at the same ratio and encounter is the same deliverable (`SKILL.md` section 6). When the set or a format is unspecified, derive it from medium and purpose and tag it inferred; if it would change the composition and cannot be derived, it is a blocking gap (E).

**Ratio.** Per deliverable, the user's intended ratio or, if not given, one derived from stated dimensions or from how this kind of deliverable is made and shown, tagged inferred. Each is designed and generated at its own ratio, never substituted.

**Context.** Purpose and objectives; where and how each deliverable is encountered (physical or on-screen setting, orientation, who meets it and in what state, distance or display scale, dwell time, visibility, light or display conditions, neighbors); reproduction limits (what making or displaying it allows: material, finish, color limits, display constraints, budget tier, quantity). If the setting is not stated, infer the most probable one by reasoning from how the deliverable is made, shown, and reached: that fixes who encounters it, where, at what distance or scale, with what attention and time, under what conditions, and against what neighbors, and those fix the encounter stages (Phase 2). Tag it provisional and pass it on; ask only if plausible settings would need opposing designs. Record the format's area or size beside content volume: their relation drives density, space, and scale later. Record any image generator or output language the user names (Phase 6 uses it).

### B. Subject and identity
What is being presented and by whom (name, kind, offerings or content, operating context); audience; differentiators; positioning and existing visual identity. Record what the user said, not what the category implies.

### C. Content
Capture all content that must or may appear (names and identifiers, text of every kind, data and figures, offers or instructions, contacts and handles, required or legal statements, trust indicators). For each item record: exact string, necessity, provenance, referent, deliverable, share of content volume.

- **Verbatim:** spelling, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, price and contact formats, non-standard brand spellings. A suspected typo is preserved and flagged for the notes.
- **Never fabricate** figures, claims, testimonials, certifications, regulated or trust marks, contacts, credentials, dates, specifications, or statistics. Regulated or trust marks appear only when supplied.
- **Supplied codes** (QR, QRIS, barcode) are fixed assets: record as attached, with the size needed to scan. Never imitate one.
- **Supplied visuals and structure.** Record images, depicted people, mascots, characters, and handles as fixed assets: what is fixed, and whether permission to show a depicted person is known. Record whether the user wants a figure created and what it should do; an unmentioned figure is not assumed and may be offered once as a suggestion. Record structure the content already has (sequence, levels, item-value rows); later phases may express structure visually only from what is given.
- **Generated copy.** Functional labels derived from supplied content are acceptable. A headline, tagline, or descriptor is written only when the piece cannot function without one or the user requested copy: factual, modest, tagged creative choice, flagged in the notes.
- **Necessity.** Mandatory if the user said it must appear or the piece cannot work without it; optional if offered or helpful; excluded if the user said to leave it out. When unsure, treat as mandatory: ask once only if the answer would change content volume; otherwise Phase 5 fits it by demotion or grouping, never omission.

### D. Preferences, constraints, quality requirements
Style, color, mood, theme, references, assets, exclusions, cultural or religious sensitivities. Sensitivities arise wherever a visual choice asserts a claim, invokes a belief, authority, or group, or depicts a person. For each depicted element, symbol, script, color, and mark, ask what it asserts or invokes for this audience, whether the subject may make that claim, and whether its meaning is verified; omit what stays uncertain. From a reference, extract what the user responds to (structure, tone, density, material), never reproduce it. List each fixed asset with exactly what is fixed; for a supplied logo image record form, construction, and colors precisely so Phase 6 can describe it if the model cannot take an image.

**Quality requirements (binding).** Every experiential, tonal, affective, or priority statement, and every named style, color, or technique, however vague, is recorded in the user's words. A minimal brief makes these the primary requirements. For each write the **quality translation**: (1) visual targets in specialist terms (value key, chroma, temperature, density, formality, warmth, hierarchy priority, sensory cues); (2) two to four quality carriers from color and value key, shape language, surface, imagery, type treatment, spacing; (3) contradicting signals: the visible properties that would deliver the quality's opposite, found by reversing each target. A quality word has no fixed visual meaning: its translation answers what this audience, in this setting and against these neighbors, would see that makes them say it, and what would make them say the opposite. Targets are observable at the stated encounter stage and, where the quality is comparative, stated relative to the neighbors. A translation that could apply unchanged to any subject has not been made. A whole-piece quality constrains the dominant field and largest masses, not only an accent. Qualities that pull apart are recorded as a pair with a carrier for each side. Ambiguous words are interpreted by the rest of the brief and tagged.

### E. Gaps
- **Explicit:** record. **Inferable:** infer and tag. **Researchable:** pass to Phase 2 naming the decision it could change; drop it if it changes none.
- **Materially uncertain:** a **blocking gap** when its answer would change which deliverable carries the message, the number or format of deliverables (when medium and purpose do not imply them), whether mandatory content exists, or whether the format can contain the content. Weigh the cost of a wrong guess against the cost of asking (`00_conversation_ux.md` section 3). Ask blocking gaps and valuable gaps (answers that change story, setting, content, or look) through the intake; never ask what can be derived or investigated.
- Facts about the subject are never invented. A missing aesthetic preference is never blocking (Phase 3 decides it); a vague quality statement is not a missing preference but the requirement.

## 3. Gate checks (fails if any is false)

- A colleague could design from the ledger without the original message.
- Every mandatory string is exact; every item has necessity, provenance, and referent; fixed assets and exclusions are listed; no unsourced fact; no suggestion recorded as given.
- Every stated quality and priority is kept in the user's words with a full translation (a mood-word brief that yields no qualities fails).
- The deliverable set is recorded with each deliverable's purpose, format, ratio, content volume, and setting (inferred and tagged if unstated); references recorded as taste, never layout requirements; supplied visual material listed as fixed assets.
- Intake stayed within limits, every question passed the clarification test and, for a new user, had options or a default, none answerable from the brief; summary shown with the mode question (Phase 2 starts only after the user confirms).
- Objection (production specialist): are the deliverables, medium, format, reproduction limits, and encounter conditions captured well enough to design for?
- Depth is recorded with the Full condition that set it, or none.
- Even a simple brief has a written ledger; simplicity shortens it, never removes it.
