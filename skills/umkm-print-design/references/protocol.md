# Exploration protocol

Read once on entering Phase 2. Phase files point here. Depth (`SKILL.md` section 6) scales card detail, never the steps.

## 1. Three tiers

| Tier | Decisions | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Full protocol | Core idea, style synthesis, hero (Phase 3); logo mark and lockup (Phase 4, plus its extended development); spatial strategy (Phase 5) | Section 2 |
| Compact comparison | Phase 2: differentiation, weakest link, departure and fit dimensions. Phase 3: governing principles marked "compared". Phase 4: color strategy, typographic voice, copy treatments, supporters, containers and shape language, any open hero treatment | Section 3 |
| Direct | Everything else | Decide with a one-line basis |

Phases 1 and 6 hold no exploration. A Full decision resolved in one pass, with no recorded challenge, has not been decided. A decision fixed by the user, a supplied asset, or evidence is recorded as fixed (`SKILL.md` section 11) and gets no cards; only what stays open inside it is explored (a user-named style fixes the lineage, not the seven-dimension synthesis).

## 2. Full protocol

1. **Name the apparent option** as the benchmark. It may stay as one candidate but must earn its place.
2. **Write at least three cards,** at most one apparent, at least one from beyond it, differing on a structural axis and sourced differently (product truth, customer moment, environment, differentiator, category tension, name or origin, material, Visual source inventory, territory survey). Fill every field the phase sets, in specialist terms (section 4). Finish all cards before judging any. Where the core idea already fixes the hero's subject, hero cards differ by depiction mode, crop and scale, or how the hero hosts text instead, and need not be sourced differently; no hero stays a candidate where it is eligible.
3. **Judge, last-written card first.**
   - Eliminate any card that contradicts a quality requirement or mandatory content (unless `SKILL.md` section 7 forces the trade).
   - Rate survivors, one line each and only where cards differ, on the lenses that discriminate: **quality requirements** (supports, strains, neutral, each one); **environment** (neighbors, light, distance, departure and fit); **capacity and production** (content volume, space intent, material, budget, renderability by an image model, `terms.md`); **coherence** with decisions already made (amplifies, conflicts, neutral; flag weaknesses that would compound); brand, audience, and cultural fit where they differ. An argument from a single lens ("it looks refined") is not an evaluation.
4. **Challenge before choosing.** Name the runner-up; state the strongest case against the leader; remove the leader and see who wins; state the leader's margin as one decisive criterion. If none can be stated, keep working. An apparent option wins only by naming the criterion on which the less obvious candidates fell short; "it is fitting" or "it is common for the category" is not one.
5. **Test the leader:** *predictable?* (a stranger could guess it from category and place: rebuild); *specific?* (nameable by lineage and defining traits, derived from this brief, would fail unchanged for a competitor); *ambitious enough?* (a merely competent candidate beats an ambitious one only on a stated decisive criterion). A failing leader is rebuilt, not defended.
6. **Engineer.** Every material weakness gets a named design move or a recorded accepted cost; every key strength gets a named place where it is amplified. Select outright, transplant a strength, repair a weakness, or combine under one logic (hybrid, never compromise).
7. **Record** in the log, in this order: apparent option; cards (axis and source each); judgment; runner-up and case against the leader; decisive criterion; engineered result with mitigations; what was kept from the others. A missing field means the decision is not made. These fields go to the Record. The chosen option, as a specification, and each mitigation, accepted cost, and amplify-here move go to the Forward block, the moves as carry-forward obligations.

## 3. Compact comparison

Name the apparent option. Compare two or more alternatives (three where real options exist) that differ in logic, not detail, one line each against quality compatibility, environment, and coherence. Choose; state the decisive criterion and the weakness handled in a line. Where evidence leaves one defensible option, say so and manufacture no alternatives.

## 4. Specialist terminology

Name things at a specialist's precision and use a term only if it can be defined; an undefinable prestigious word is vagueness and is cut.
- Style: lineage and defining traits. Imagery: depiction mode, technique, light, finish.
- Color: hue family, value structure, chroma, temperature, harmony structure, area proportion.
- Type: classification, stroke contrast, proportion, x-height, widths, spacing.
- Composition: axis, alignment, grid logic, figure-ground, focal hierarchy, grouping, visual weight, tension.
- Print: ink, substrate, finish.
